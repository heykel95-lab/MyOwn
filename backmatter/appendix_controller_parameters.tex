\subsection{Calibration Relations}

For the seated surface pose, the nominal end-effector axis and the tool-face
centre offset supplied the stored plane quantities,

\begin{equation}
n_s=R_{\mathrm{EE}}[0,0,1]^\top,
\qquad
p_s=p_{\mathrm{EE}}+R_{\mathrm{EE}}r_{\mathrm{face,EE}}.
\label{eq:appendix_surface_calibration}
\end{equation}

The first tangent was the normalised projection of the base-frame
\mbox{\(x\)} direction onto the plane, and
\mbox{\(t_2=n_s\times t_1\)} completed the surface frame. For the tool-normal
calibration, the fitted direction was

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}
=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\lVert n\rVert=1}
\sum_{i<j}
\left\lVert(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}-R_{\mathrm{cal},j})n\right\rVert^2.
\label{eq:appendix_tool_normal_fit}
\end{equation}

Three seated yaw captures entered the fit. The sign of the fitted eigenvector
was selected towards nominal \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}.

\section{Inertia-Scaled Damping and Centre of Compliance}

Inertia-scaled damping follows \Cref{eq:implementation_inertia_scaled_damping},
whose directional inertia \(\lambda_i(q)\) is the corresponding diagonal entry
of the operational-space inertia resolved along the axes of the active gain
block. In the base frame that matrix is

\begin{equation}
\Lambda_0(q)
=
\left(
J(q)M(q)^{-1}J^\top(q)+\varepsilon I_6
\right)^{-1},
\qquad
\varepsilon=10^{-9}.
\label{eq:appendix_cartesian_inertia}
\end{equation}

The implementation never forms \(M(q)^{-1}\). An \abbr{LDLT} factorisation of
\(M(q)\) solves \(M(q)Y(q)=J^\top(q)\), and \(\Lambda_0(q)\) is then evaluated
as \((J(q)Y(q)+\varepsilon I_6)^{-1}\); the regularisation term
\(\varepsilon\) keeps that inversion conditioned.

Inertia-scaled damping is enabled for approach, set-up, the configured grinding
phase, and the pause gate. The approach factor is \(\zeta=1.9\), while set-up and grinding use
\(\zeta=1.0\). The pause gate fits separate translational and rotational
factors to its two configured damping targets. The configured maximum
coefficient \texttt{auto\_damping\_max} is \(400\). The configured values in
\Cref{tab:active_phase_gains} provide fallback damping for the phases using
inertia-scaled damping. With
\texttt{auto\_damping\_min\_from\_manual} left at zero, those manual values act
only as that fallback and impose no lower bound on a calculated coefficient.

Standard Cartesian pose hold instead used its configured matrices directly.
All twelve reported null-space runs recorded
\texttt{hold\_auto\_damping=0}, with
\(D_p=50I_3\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}\) and
\(D_R=8I_3\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\). The hold-damping values in
\Cref{tab:active_phase_gains} are therefore active values rather than
fallbacks.

For set-up, the source damping blocks are calculated from TCP task inertia
\(\Lambda\) and then shifted to the selected \abbr{CoC} \(p_c\). This
preserves symmetry and positive
semidefiniteness. The factor \(\zeta=1\) is the directional coefficient used by
the online damping calculation.

The coupled \(6\times6\) law is enabled only during set-up and only for
conditions with a displaced \abbr{CoC}. The surface-fixed conditions of
the definition-frame check in \mbox{\Cref{sec:supporting_definition_frame}}
used a displacement defined in the calibrated surface frame.
The other shifted conditions used tool-fixed displacements configured in
end-effector coordinates. For the surface-fixed definition,
\[
r_{c,0}=R_{\mathrm{surface}}\,r_{c,S},
\qquad
r_{c,S}=[\,r_{c,t_1},\ r_{c,t_2},\ r_{c,n}\,]^\top .
\]
The tested definitions and components are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:experimental_test_cases} and
\Cref{app:supporting_contact_checks}. A
tool-fixed lever remains constant in end-effector coordinates and rotates in
the base frame with the end effector; a surface-fixed lever remains constant
in surface coordinates. The controller transforms the selected definition to
base coordinates during each set-up cycle.
