\chapter{Recorded Experimental Data}
\label{app:recorded_data}
\ReviewMark{green}{The appendix records the signals actually used in the
evaluation and identifies the common configured surface reference used by the controller
and analysis.}

The controller stores experimental signals in a preallocated, fixed-capacity
ring buffer inside the \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\) control callback. After the
controller stops, the current rows are written in chronological order to a CSV
file.

\section{Control States}
\ReviewMark{green}{The numeric phase values are mapped directly to their
recorded state names.}

The source field \texttt{state} identifies the active controller state:
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\quad}l@{\qquad}c@{\quad}l}
0 & \texttt{tool\_orientation}       & 7 & \texttt{pre\_grinding\_hold} \\
1 & \texttt{surface\_approach}       & 3 & \texttt{grinding} \\
6 & \texttt{pre\_contact\_hold}     & 4 & \texttt{cartesian\_pose\_hold} \\
2 & \texttt{contact\_establishment} & 5 & \texttt{manual\_guidance}
\end{array}
\]

\section{Recorded Signals}
\ReviewMark{green}{The table is a useful reproducibility record and keeps units,
frames, and model-estimated quantities explicit.}

\Added{\mbox{Table~\ref{tab:csv_column_groups}} lists the union of the two CSV schemas
used for the experiments. The surface-contact schema contains the
contact-geometry fields, whereas the Cartesian pose-hold schema contains the
singular-value probe and projected-motion fields. Command, state, wrench, and
disturbance groups are common to both schemas. A suffix
\texttt{\_x,\_y,\_z} denotes base-frame components, and \texttt{\_1..\_7}
denotes joint components.}
