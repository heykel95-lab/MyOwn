The alignment angle can carry a common offset from the calibrated tool normal.
Because the same calibration is used for all runs, this offset is common to the
evaluation chain, but it does not necessarily appear as an identical additive
angular offset in every orientation. The primary signed set-up rotation does
not use the calibrated tool normal; it is obtained from the measured
end-effector motion and then resolved in the surface frame, which makes it the
more direct quantity for comparing the controller-induced rotation between
settings.

\section{Consistency of the Two Angular Quantities}
\label{app:metric_agreement}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_DQ_metric_comparison.pdf}
\caption{Alignment angle against end-effector rotation.}
\label{fig:results_metric_agreement}
\end{figure}

\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_metric_agreement}} is a check on the choice of response
metric. The signed set-up rotation
\mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}\)} is the quantity reported throughout
\mbox{\Cref{ch:results_discussion}}, and the change in the pose-based alignment
angle \mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\)} supports it. Defined differently,
the two are not interchangeable.

Panel~(a) plots both quantities for one representative trial. One is the
alignment angle calculated from the end-effector pose; the other reconstructs
that angle from the end-effector rotation since the start of set-up.
Panel~(b) reduces the comparison to one point per evaluated trial. Each point
carries the magnitude of the change, and the marker distinguishes whether the
calculated alignment angle decreased or increased during set-up. Across those
trials the two magnitudes are broadly consistent, and the larger disagreements
are confined to a small number of conditions.

Both quantities rest on the same assumed tool-to-gripper relation, so their
agreement is internal to that assumption rather than an independent measurement
of physical tool orientation. The approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of
mounting play was measured unloaded and was not characterised as an uncertainty
bound under contact load.
