\chapter{Supporting Contact Checks and Results}
\label{app:additional_plots}
\label{app:supporting_contact_checks}

This appendix reports three supporting checks of the compliance-centre
geometry. Together they comprise \mbox{\(42\)} runs across \mbox{\(14\)}
independently counted settings, with three repetitions per setting. The
calibrated geometry, phase sequence, Cartesian gains, null-space settings, and
evaluation quantities were those of
\mbox{\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration,sec:experimental_controller_parameters}}
unless a changed condition is stated below.

\section{Check 1: Compliance-Centre Definition Frame}
\label{sec:supporting_definition_frame}

This check compared tool-fixed and surface-fixed definitions of the same
nominal \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} tangential displacement. The tool-fixed
vector rotated with the end effector. The surface-fixed vector remained
constant in the calibrated frame \mbox{\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\)}. Both vectors were
transformed to the base frame during each control cycle before the stiffness
and damping matrices were shifted to the TCP.

The supporting-check settings were the tool-fixed displacement without an
orientation offset and the surface-fixed displacement with no offset or with a
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} offset about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. These three settings gave
\mbox{\(9\)} runs. The shared tool-fixed \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} condition is
counted with the \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} reference of Case~D.

The two definitions produced markedly different responses.
\mbox{\Cref{tab:supporting_definition_frame}} pairs them at each command, and
\mbox{\Cref{fig:supporting_definition_frame}} shows the separation. For the
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} command about \mbox{\(t_1\)}, the tool-fixed definition
gave \mbox{\(+7.87\pm0.01^\circ\)} of set-up rotation. The surface-fixed
definition gave \mbox{\(+0.13\pm0.01^\circ\)} and was classified as tilted by
TCP height. At zero commanded offset, the two definitions produced
\mbox{\(-0.06\pm0.01^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-1.05\pm0.02^\circ\)}.

A non-zero \abbr{CoC} displacement is therefore not fully characterised by its
magnitude alone. The displacement direction and the frame holding it determine
how the vector moves relative to the contact geometry. Returning the centre to
the TCP removes that direction and definition-frame dependence together with
the point-shift coupling.

\section{Check 2: Tool-Axis Displacement}
\label{sec:supporting_tool_axis}

This check separated the distance between the \abbr{CoC} and TCP from the
direction of the displacement. The centre was moved by \mbox{\(-40\)},
\mbox{\(-20\)}, \mbox{\(-10\)}, \mbox{\(+10\)}, \mbox{\(+20\)}, and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} along the tool axis at a fixed
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} command about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. These six settings gave
\mbox{\(18\)} runs. The shared zero-displacement condition is counted with
the Case-A reference.

\begin{figure}[H]

The tool-axis response varied weakly across the tested range.
\mbox{\Cref{tab:supporting_tool_axis}} lists the seven positions, including the
shared zero-displacement reference. The signed set-up rotation changed from
\mbox{\(+7.26\)} to \mbox{\(+7.60^\circ\)} between \mbox{\(-40\)} and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, a span of \mbox{\(0.34^\circ\)}. The purely
tangential displacement spanned \mbox{\(7.73^\circ\)} over the same interval
in Case~D, as shown in \mbox{\Cref{fig:supporting_tool_axis}}. Every tool-axis
position was classified as flat, and the response was not strictly monotonic.

As \mbox{\Cref{eq:coc_normal_press_tangential_only}} shows, only the tangential
component of the \abbr{CoC} displacement contributes to the moment generated
by the commanded normal force. Here the displacement was defined along the
tool axis rather than along a surface tangent. Because the tool was inclined
relative to the surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)}, that displacement contained a
normal component and a smaller component in the surface plane.

Let \mbox{\(\alpha_{\mathrm{axis}}\)} denote the angle between the tool axis
and \mbox{\(n_s\)}. The tangential magnitude was

\begin{equation}
\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert
=
\lVert r_c\rVert\sin\alpha_{\mathrm{axis}}.
\label{eq:supporting_tool_axis_projection}
\end{equation}

At the maximum tested displacement,
\mbox{\(\lVert r_c\rVert=40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The tool axis was inclined by
approximately \mbox{\(10^\circ\)}. The corresponding projection was

\[
\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert
=
(40\,\mathrm{mm})\sin(10^\circ)
=
6.95\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]

The \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} value is the total configured \abbr{CoC}
displacement along the tool axis. The \mbox{\(6.95\,\mathrm{mm}\)} value is
only its projection onto the surface tangent plane. This effective tangential
lever is considerably smaller than the \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} purely
tangential displacement of Case~D. The difference is consistent with the weak
variation in \mbox{\Cref{fig:supporting_tool_axis}}. Tangential force
components, finite rotation, changing contact geometry, and tool-mount
compliance were not isolated by this check.

\section{Check 3: Intermediate Tangent Directions}
\label{sec:supporting_intermediate_directions}

This check examined whether the direction rule of Case~D also held between the
principal surface tangents. For a commanded rotation with surface components
\mbox{\((\theta_{t_1},\theta_{t_2})\)}, the selected tangential displacement
was

\begin{equation}
r_{c,t}
=
\frac{\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert}{\sqrt{\theta_{t_1}^{2}+\theta_{t_2}^{2}}}
\left(\theta_{t_1}t_2-\theta_{t_2}t_1\right),
\label{eq:supporting_general_lever_rule}
\end{equation}

where \mbox{\(\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert=40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The selected vector was
perpendicular to the commanded rotation in the tangent plane. Its sign made
the press-induced moment act in the required alignment direction. The fixed
comparison retained the vector selected for the \mbox{\(t_1\)} command.

\section{Conditions Classified as Tilted by TCP Height}
\label{app:not_flat}

The TCP-height criterion is retained here as a supporting geometric check and
does not enter the primary response tables of Chapter~5. All five Case-A
settings, including the zero-orientation-offset condition, satisfied this
criterion. This classification is not a direct measurement of the physical
tool-face orientation. The mounting play was characterised in the unloaded
condition, and the instantaneous relative tool--gripper rotation was not
tracked separately during the contact runs.

Of the \mbox{\(55\)} tested parameter settings, \mbox{\(49\)} satisfied the
TCP-height flatness criterion. \mbox{\Cref{tab:results_not_flat}} lists the

\section{Comparison with the Pose-Based Alignment Estimate}
\label{app:metric_agreement}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_DQ_metric_comparison.pdf}
\caption{Pose-based alignment estimate against measured set-up rotation.}
\label{fig:results_metric_agreement}
\end{figure}

The main results use the signed end-effector set-up rotation
\mbox{\(\Delta\theta_i\)}. This appendix compares that direct motion
measurement with a secondary alignment estimate reconstructed from the
calibrated tool normal. The pose-based alignment error is

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align}}(t)
=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(-n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}(t)^\top n_s\right),
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}.
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

It is unsigned and depends on the assumed fixed relation between the tool and
end effector. Its reduction over set-up is the value before set-up minus the
value afterwards. This change and \(\Delta\theta_i\) are defined differently
and are not interchangeable.

Panel~(a) plots the pose-based error for one representative trial and the value
reconstructed from the measured rotation since the start of set-up. Panel~(b)
reduces the comparison to one point per evaluated trial. Each point carries the
magnitude of the measured set-up rotation and the signed reduction of the
pose-based error. Where the pose-based error decreased, its reduction followed
the same scale as the measured rotation for most trials. Conditions in which
the pose-based error increased appear below zero and show why the two quantities
are not interchangeable.

The pose-based alignment quantity depends on the assumed tool-to-gripper
relation, whereas the set-up rotation is obtained directly from the measured
end-effector motion. Their agreement therefore provides an internal consistency
check but does not constitute an independent measurement of the physical tool
orientation. The approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of
mounting play was measured unloaded and was not characterised as an uncertainty
bound under contact load.
