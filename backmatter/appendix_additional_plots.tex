The signed contact-establishment rotation changed from \mbox{\(+7.26\)} to
\mbox{\(+7.60^\circ\)} between \mbox{\(-40\)} and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, a span of \mbox{\(0.34^\circ\)}, against
\mbox{\(7.73^\circ\)} for the purely tangential displacement of Case~D over
the same interval, as \mbox{\Cref{fig:supporting_tool_axis}} shows. Every
tool-axis position was classified as flat.

Only the tangential component of the \abbr{CoC} displacement contributes to
the moment generated by the commanded normal force, by
\mbox{\Cref{eq:coc_normal_press_tangential_only}}. Let
\mbox{\(\alpha_{\mathrm{axis}}\)} denote the acute inclination of the tool
axis relative to the inward surface-normal direction \mbox{\(-n_s\)}, which is
the direction the tool axis takes when the face is flat on the surface. The
check used a \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} controller input about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}. The pose-based initial inclination from \mbox{\(-n_s\)}
was approximately \mbox{\(9.30^\circ\)}, so a displacement along the tool axis
carried a tangential magnitude

\begin{equation}
\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert
=
\lVert r_c\rVert\sin\alpha_{\mathrm{axis}}.
\label{eq:supporting_tool_axis_projection}
\end{equation}

At the largest tested tool-axis displacement,
\mbox{\(\lVert r_c\rVert=40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Evaluating the projection at the
pose-based inclination, to two decimal places, gives

\[
\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert
=
(40\,\mathrm{mm})\sin(9.30^\circ)
=
6.47\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]

The \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} value is the total configured \abbr{CoC}
displacement along the tool axis, and the \mbox{\(6.47\,\mathrm{mm}\)}
value only its projection onto the surface tangent plane. That effective
tangential component is considerably smaller than the tangential displacement of
Case~D, which is consistent with the weak variation measured here. Tangential
force components, finite rotation, changing contact geometry and tool-mount
compliance were not isolated by this check.

\section{Pose-Based Alignment Consistency Check}
\label{app:metric_agreement}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_DQ_metric_summary.pdf}
\caption{Pose-based alignment estimate against measured contact-establishment rotation.}
\label{fig:results_metric_agreement}
\end{figure}

The main results use the signed contact-establishment response \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_i}\)}.
This appendix compares it with a secondary alignment estimate reconstructed
from the calibrated tool normal,

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align}}(t)
=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(-n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}(t)^\top n_s\right),
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}.
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

which is unsigned and depends on the assumed fixed relation between the tool
and the end effector. Its reduction over contact establishment is

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\theta_{\mathrm{align,before}}-\theta_{\mathrm{align,after}},
\label{eq:appendix_alignment_reduction}
\end{equation}

taking \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{align,before}}\)} at the start of contact establishment and
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{align,after}}\)} from the settled contact-establishment interval.
It is used in this appendix alone.

\Cref{fig:results_metric_agreement} gives one point per evaluated trial. Each
point carries the magnitude of the measured contact-establishment rotation and
the signed reduction of the pose-based error. Where the pose-based error
decreased, its reduction followed the same scale as
the measured rotation for most trials; conditions in which it increased appear
below zero.

Their agreement is an internal consistency check rather than an independent
measurement of the physical tool orientation, because the pose-based quantity
depends on the assumed tool-to-gripper relation while
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_i}\)} is obtained directly from the measured end-effector
orientations. The approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of mounting play was
measured unloaded and was not characterised as an uncertainty bound under
contact load.
