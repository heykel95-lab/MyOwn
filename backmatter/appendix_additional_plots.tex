The tool-axis response varied weakly across the tested range.
\mbox{\Cref{tab:supporting_tool_axis}} lists the seven positions, including the
shared zero-displacement reference. The signed set-up rotation changed from
\mbox{\(+7.26\)} to \mbox{\(+7.60^\circ\)} between \mbox{\(-40\)} and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, a span of \mbox{\(0.34^\circ\)}. The purely
tangential displacement spanned \mbox{\(7.73^\circ\)} over the same interval
in Case~D, as shown in \mbox{\Cref{fig:supporting_tool_axis}}. Every tool-axis
position was classified as flat, and the response was not strictly monotonic.

As \mbox{\Cref{eq:coc_normal_press_tangential_only}} shows, only the tangential
component of the \abbr{CoC} displacement contributes to the moment generated
by the commanded normal force. Here the displacement was defined along the
tool axis rather than along a surface tangent.

Let \mbox{\(\alpha_{\mathrm{axis}}\)} denote the acute inclination of the tool
axis relative to the inward surface-normal direction \mbox{\(-n_s\)}, which is
the direction the tool axis takes when the face is flat on the surface. The
check used a \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} commanded orientation offset about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}, so the tool axis was nominally inclined by
\mbox{\(10^\circ\)} from \mbox{\(-n_s\)}. A displacement along that inclined
axis therefore contained both a normal and a tangential component. Its
tangential magnitude was

\begin{equation}
\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert
=
\lVert r_c\rVert\sin\alpha_{\mathrm{axis}}.
\label{eq:supporting_tool_axis_projection}
\end{equation}

At the largest tested tool-axis displacement,
\mbox{\(\lVert r_c\rVert=40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Evaluating the projection at the
nominal inclination, to two decimal places, gives

\[
\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert
=
(40\,\mathrm{mm})\sin(10^\circ)
=
6.95\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]

The \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} value is the total configured \abbr{CoC}
displacement along the tool axis. The \mbox{\(6.95\,\mathrm{mm}\)} value is
only its projection onto the surface tangent plane. This effective tangential
lever is considerably smaller than the \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} purely
tangential displacement of Case~D. The difference is consistent with the weak
variation in \mbox{\Cref{fig:supporting_tool_axis}}. Tangential force
components, finite rotation, changing contact geometry, and tool-mount
compliance were not isolated by this check.

\section{Intermediate Tangent-Plane Directions}

principal surface tangents. For a commanded rotation with surface components
\mbox{\((\theta_{t_1},\theta_{t_2})\)}, the selected tangential displacement

\begin{equation}
r_{c,t}
=
\frac{\lVert r_{c,t}\rVert}{\sqrt{\theta_{t_1}^{2}+\theta_{t_2}^{2}}}

\section{Pose-Based Alignment Consistency Check}
\label{app:metric_agreement}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_DQ_metric_comparison.pdf}
\caption{Pose-based alignment estimate against measured set-up rotation.}
\label{fig:results_metric_agreement}
\end{figure}

The main results use the signed set-up response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_i}\)}. This appendix compares that direct orientation
response with a secondary alignment estimate reconstructed from the
calibrated tool normal. The pose-based alignment error is

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align}}(t)
=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(-n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}(t)^\top n_s\right),
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}.
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

It is unsigned and depends on the assumed fixed relation between the tool and
end effector. Its reduction over set-up is written

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\theta_{\mathrm{align,before}}-\theta_{\mathrm{align,after}},
\label{eq:appendix_alignment_reduction}
\end{equation}

which is used in this appendix only and does not enter the notation of the
main chapters. It and \(\gamma_{t_i}\) are defined differently and are not
interchangeable.

Panel~(a) plots the pose-based error for one representative trial and the value
reconstructed from the measured rotation since the start of set-up. Panel~(b)
reduces the comparison to one point per evaluated trial. Each point carries the
magnitude of the measured set-up rotation and the signed reduction of the
pose-based error. Where the pose-based error decreased, its reduction followed
the same scale as the measured rotation for most trials. Conditions in which
the pose-based error increased appear below zero and show why the two quantities
are not interchangeable.

The pose-based alignment quantity depends on the assumed tool-to-gripper
relation, whereas the set-up rotation is obtained directly from the measured
end-effector orientations. Their agreement therefore provides an internal consistency
check but does not constitute an independent measurement of the physical tool
orientation. The approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of
mounting play was measured unloaded and was not characterised as an uncertainty
bound under contact load.
