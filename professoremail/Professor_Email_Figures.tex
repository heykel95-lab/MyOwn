\section*{Contact-Establishment Wrench Trace, \(r_c=0\)}

\textbf{Used impedance.}
The following gains were applied in the surface basis \([t_1,t_2,n_s]\):
\begin{align*}
 K_p &= [2000,\ 2000,\ 350]\,\mathrm{N/m}, &
 D_p &= [231.2,\ 177.9,\ 112.1]\,\mathrm{N\,s/m},\\
 K_R &= [5,\ 5,\ 50]\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}, &
 D_R &= [1.5,\ 1.9,\ 1.2]\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}.
\end{align*}
The damping values were calculated automatically at Contact Establishment
entry with damping factor \(1.9\); the manual damping values were not used as
lower bounds.  This contact sequence used null-space mode 3.
The centre of compliance was placed at the TCP, with
\(r_c=p_c-p_{\mathrm{TCP}}=0\).
The first tangent \(t_1\) is the normalised projection of the base-frame
\(x_0\)-direction onto the configured surface plane:
\[
 t_1=\frac{e_{x_0}-(n_s\mathbin{\cdot}e_{x_0})\mathbin{\cdot}n_s}
 {\lVert e_{x_0}-(n_s\mathbin{\cdot}e_{x_0})\mathbin{\cdot}n_s\rVert}
 =(0.999851,-0.000477,-0.017238)^\top.
\]

The curves show one five-second Contact Establishment trace.  The grey area
from \(4\) to \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) is the stationary interval used for the
measured means.  Both plotted signals are absolute quantities resolved on the
same surface axes.  The physical contact force is denoted throughout by
\({}^{O}\!f_{\mathrm{contact}}\).

\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Normal-force comparison, \(F_n\)}\par
The mean desired and measured TCP positions in the grey interval were
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_d&=(0.511749,-0.005127,-0.210071)^\top\,\mathrm{m},\\
 p_{\mathrm{TCP}}&=(0.513909,0.005024,0.019199)^\top\,\mathrm{m}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Their normal error gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e_n&=n_s^\top\mathbin{\cdot}(p_d-p_{\mathrm{TCP}})
 =-0.229466\,\mathrm{m},\\
 F_{n,\mathrm{qs}}&=K_{p,n}\mathbin{\cdot}e_n
 =350\mathbin{\cdot}(-0.229466)=-80.313\,\mathrm{N},\\
 F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}&=-80.312\,\mathrm{N},\qquad
 F_{n,\mathrm{est}}=-78.451\,\mathrm{N}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The estimate differs from the command by \(1.861\,\mathrm{N}\), or
\(2.32\%\).  The quasi-static value and the full command differ by
\(0.001\,\mathrm{N}\), since the mean normal speed was nearly zero.
The corresponding physical reaction on the robot is
\[
 F_{n,\mathrm{contact}}
 =n_s^\top\mathbin{\cdot}f_{\mathrm{contact}}
 =-F_{n,\mathrm{est}}=+78.451\,\mathrm{N}.
\]

\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Moment comparison about \(t_1\)}\par
The centre of compliance is at the TCP.  It therefore introduces no virtual
force-to-moment coupling:
\[
 m_{\mathrm{CoC}}=r_c\times f=0.
\]
The physical contact quantities shown in Figure~1 are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_{\mathrm{contact/TCP}}
 &=p_{\mathrm{contact}}-p_{\mathrm{TCP}},\\
 {}^{O}\!m_{\mathrm{contact},\mathrm{TCP}}
 &=r_{\mathrm{contact/TCP}}\times{}^{O}\!f_{\mathrm{contact}},\\
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{contact}}
 &=t_1^\top\mathbin{\cdot}{}^{O}\!m_{\mathrm{contact},\mathrm{TCP}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The pressure centre was not measured, so no contact lever is assigned a
measured value.

In the grey interval, the measured orientation error and angular speed give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e_{R,t_1}&=0.126923\,\mathrm{rad}=7.272^\circ,\\
 \omega_{t_1}&=-0.0000185\,\mathrm{rad/s},\\
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{cmd}}
 &=K_{R,t_1}\mathbin{\cdot}e_{R,t_1}
   -D_{R,t_1}\mathbin{\cdot}\omega_{t_1}\\
 &=5\mathbin{\cdot}0.126923
   -1.5\mathbin{\cdot}(-0.0000185)\\
 &=+0.635\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The quasi-static spring value is also \(+0.635\,\mathrm{N\,m}\).  Force and
moment equilibrium then require the opposing surface-on-robot moment
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{contact}}+M_{t_1,\mathrm{cmd}}&=0,\\
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{contact}}&=-0.635\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
With the model-estimated force magnitude, this corresponds to the
quasi-static equivalent lever
\[
 r_{\perp,\mathrm{qs}}
 =\frac{\lvert M_{t_1,\mathrm{contact}}\rvert}
 {\lvert F_{n,\mathrm{est}}\rvert}
 =\frac{0.635}{78.451}=0.00809\,\mathrm{m}=8.09\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]
This is an equilibrium equivalent, not a measured contact location.

The measured robot state confirmed \({}^{EE}T_K=I\), so stiffness frame \(K\)
coincides with the controller TCP.  The base-expressed spatial wrench is
therefore shifted to the TCP by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 {}^{O}\!m_{\mathrm{TCP},\mathrm{est}}
 &= {}^{O}\!m_{O,\mathrm{est}}
 -p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\times{}^{O}\!f_{\mathrm{est}},\\
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{est}}
 &=t_1^\top\mathbin{\cdot}{}^{O}\!m_{\mathrm{TCP},\mathrm{est}}
 =+0.604\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The estimate uses the opposite action side to
\(M_{t_1,\mathrm{contact}}\).  It is therefore compared with
\(-M_{t_1,\mathrm{contact}}=+0.635\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), as shown in Figure~1.
Its difference from the command is \(0.031\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), or \(4.87\%\).
Subtracting the non-zero clearance estimate would instead give
\(-0.601\,\mathrm{N\,m}\); that change signal is not compared with the
absolute command and is not plotted here.

\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Orientation change}\par
The measured component about \(t_1\) fell from \(9.26^\circ\) at contact entry
to \(1.99^\circ\) at the endpoint.  Thus,
\[
 \Delta\theta_{t_1}=1.99^\circ-9.26^\circ=-7.27^\circ.
\]
The off-centre contact rotates the tool towards the plane.  The positive
rotational command develops in the opposing direction and retains the entry
orientation.

\noindent\textbf{Normal-force summary.}
The quasi-static, commanded and model-estimated endpoint values are
\(-80.313\), \(-80.312\) and \(-78.451\,\mathrm{N}\).  The estimate differs
from the command by \(2.32\%\).

\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Moment summary.}
The quasi-static, commanded and model-estimated endpoint values are
\(+0.635\), \(+0.635\) and \(+0.604\,\mathrm{N\,m}\).  The estimate differs
from the command by \(4.87\%\).  This single Contact Establishment trace is
consistent in sign and magnitude for both components.

\section*{T-Mode Quasi-Static Consistency Test}

The test uses the decoupled setup impedance in surface coordinates, with
\(r_c=0\).  The commanded components are
\begin{align*}
 F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}} &= K_{p,n}\mathbin{\cdot}e_n
                       +D_{p,n}\mathbin{\cdot}\dot e_n,\\
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{cmd}} &= K_{R,t_1}\mathbin{\cdot}e_{R,t_1}
                         +D_{R,t_1}\mathbin{\cdot}\dot e_{R,t_1}.
\end{align*}
During each stationary hold, \(\dot e_n\simeq0\) and
\(\dot e_{R,t_1}\simeq0\).  The quasi-static predictions therefore reduce to
\[
 F_{n,\mathrm{qs}}=K_{p,n}\mathbin{\cdot}e_n,
 \qquad
 M_{t_1,\mathrm{qs}}=K_{R,t_1}\mathbin{\cdot}e_{R,t_1}.
\]

The trial uses manual damping.  The gains in the surface basis
\([t_1,t_2,n]\) are
\begin{align*}
 K_p&=[2000,2000,1000]~\mathrm{N/m}, &
 D_p&=[10,10,175]~\mathrm{N\,s/m},\\
 K_R&=[15,5,50]~\mathrm{N\,m/rad}, &
 D_R&=[10.01,10.01,10]~\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}.
\end{align*}
The centre of compliance is at the TCP, \(r_c=0\).
The accepted nominal checks were approximately \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) and
\(6^\circ\).  At exactly \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\), the force prediction is
\[
 |F_{n,\mathrm{qs}}|=1000\mathbin{\cdot}0.020=20.0\,\mathrm{N},
\]
and at exactly \(6^\circ=0.104720\,\mathrm{rad}\), the moment prediction is
\[
 |M_{t_1,\mathrm{qs}}|=15\mathbin{\cdot}0.104720=1.571\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\]
