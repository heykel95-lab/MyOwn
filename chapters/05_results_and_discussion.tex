\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results_discussion}

The surface-contact experiments measured how the controller parameters affected
the end-effector rotation during Contact Establishment. The primary response
quantity is the contact-establishment response \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} about
surface tangent \mbox{\(t_1\)}, defined in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}}.

The achieved pose-based initial angular offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}
describes the tool orientation at contact entry relative to the configured
surface reference. The response \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} is calculated from
the measured end orientation back to the orientation held at contact entry. Its
direction is therefore opposite to the start-to-end rotation of the end
effector. When contact rotates the end effector towards parallel alignment with
the configured surface, \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} points in the same
surface-tangent direction as \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. The magnitude
\mbox{\(\lvert\gamma_{t_1}\rvert\)} gives the size of that response.

The orientation reached at contact entry differed slightly from the
configured offset \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}\)}. The response
figures therefore identify the directional test conditions by the achieved
offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. The nominal zero-offset condition is
described by the initial angular-offset magnitude \mbox{\(\phi_0\)}.
\Cref{app:additional_plots} tabulates the corresponding numerical values.

\Added{Each contact parameter setting was repeated three times. The comparative
response values in Cases~A--D are the arithmetic means over those repetitions,
and \mbox{\Cref{app:additional_plots}} tabulates their sample standard
deviations.} At the TCP-centred condition, the configured offsets of
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} produced mean achieved initial
angular offsets of \mbox{\(+9.31^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-9.41^\circ\)} about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}. These two are named the positive-offset and negative-offset
conditions throughout this chapter.

\section{Main Surface-Contact Results}
\label{sec:results_main_contact_cases}

\subsection{Baseline Contact Response with the CoC at the TCP: Case~A}
\label{sec:results_case_a}

In Case~A the \abbr{CoC} was placed at the TCP, so \mbox{\(r_c=0\)} and the
CoC-induced moment \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)} was zero. The rotational impedance
and the physical contact interaction remained active. This condition provides
the baseline for the stiffness and CoC-position comparisons.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_a_bars.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response with the CoC at the TCP.}
\label{fig:results_case_a}
\end{figure}

For the positive-offset condition, with
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=+9.31^\circ\)}, the measured response was
\mbox{\(+7.57^\circ\)} in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_a}}. For the
negative-offset condition, with \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=-9.41^\circ\)}, it was
\mbox{\(-10.83^\circ\)}. Both responses corresponded to end-effector rotation
towards parallel alignment with the configured surface.

With zero configured orientation offset, the initial angular-offset magnitude
was \mbox{\(\phi_0=0.74^\circ\)} and the measured response about
\mbox{\(t_1\)} was \mbox{\(-0.97^\circ\)}. This value forms the measured
zero-offset reference. For the positive- and negative-offset conditions, the
response magnitude was \mbox{\(81.3\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(115.1\,\%\)} of the
respective achieved initial angular-offset magnitude.

\subsection{Effect of Cartesian Stiffness: Cases~B and~C}

\subsubsection{Rotational Stiffness: Case~B}
\label{sec:results_case_b}

In Case~B the rotational stiffness about the investigated tangent was varied
while the other controller settings were held at their baseline values. The
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} condition is the corresponding TCP-centred
reference from Case~A.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_b_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response by rotational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_b}
\end{figure}

Increasing \mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} from \mbox{\(5\)} to
\mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} reduced the measured response from
\mbox{\(+7.57^\circ\)} to \mbox{\(+2.73^\circ\)} in
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_b}}. This is a reduction of
\mbox{\(4.84^\circ\)}, or \mbox{\(63.9\,\%\)} of the response measured at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. All measured responses remained directed
towards parallel alignment with the configured surface.

\subsubsection{Cross-Axis Translational Stiffness: Case~C}
\label{sec:results_case_c}

In Case~C the translational stiffness perpendicular to the investigated
rotation axis was varied. The rotational stiffness about that axis was held at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}, and the \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}
condition is the corresponding TCP-centred reference from Case~A.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_c_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response by cross-axis translational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_c}
\end{figure}

Changing the cross-axis translational stiffness \mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}\)} produced
only a small change in the measured response. Across the tested values,
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} ranged from \mbox{\(+7.56^\circ\)} to
\mbox{\(+7.59^\circ\)} in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_c}}, giving a span of
\mbox{\(0.03^\circ\)}. This is \mbox{\(0.4\,\%\)} of the
\mbox{\(7.57^\circ\)} TCP-centred reference and considerably smaller than the
rotational-stiffness effect.

\subsection{Effect of Tangential CoC Position: Case~D}
\label{sec:results_case_d}

In Case~D the tangential position of the \abbr{CoC} was varied along
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, perpendicular to the measured response about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
Cartesian stiffness and damping were held at their baseline values, and the
TCP-centred condition \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=0\)} provides the reference.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_panels.tex}
\caption[Contact response by tangential CoC position.]{\Added{Contact response by tangential CoC position.}}
\label{fig:results_case_d}
\end{figure}

The effect of the CoC displacement reversed with the direction of the achieved
initial angular offset. For the positive-offset condition,
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=+9.32^\circ\)},
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d}} shows the response increasing from
\mbox{\(+0.18^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} to
\mbox{\(+7.87^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. For the
negative-offset condition, \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=-9.36^\circ\)}, the opposite
trend was measured: the response changed from \mbox{\(-11.30^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} to \mbox{\(-0.09^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.

Relative to the TCP-centred condition, each tested non-zero CoC displacement
increased the response towards parallel alignment for one direction of the
initial angular offset and reduced it for the other. The displacement direction
that increased the response depended on the direction of
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. \Added{The largest Case-D sample
standard deviation was \mbox{\(0.44^\circ\)}, for the positive-offset condition
at \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-20\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.}

To relate this direction dependence to the commanded wrench, the
\mbox{\(-40\)}, \mbox{\(0\)}, and \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} positions of the
positive-offset condition were examined in the time domain. Each trace shows
one measured repetition for the corresponding centre position.

The commanded Cartesian wrench \mbox{\(F=[f^\top,m^\top]^\top\)} was resolved
for the time histories as

\[
F_n=n_s^\top f,
\qquad
M_{t_1}=t_1^\top m.
\]

The normal-force component is negative while the tool presses into the
surface. Both quantities are controller commands.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_wrench_relabel.tex}
\caption[Contact response and commanded wrench.]{\Added{Contact response and commanded wrench.}}
\label{fig:results_case_d_wrench}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench} places the contact-establishment response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} in panel~(a), the commanded normal force
\mbox{\(F_n\)} in panel~(b), and the commanded TCP moment
\mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} in panel~(c), on a common time axis for the three centre
positions. Every condition shown uses
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}=+10^\circ\)} and a mean achieved initial
angular offset of \mbox{\(+9.32^\circ\)}. The commanded normal force settled
between approximately \mbox{\(-77\)} and \mbox{\(-82\,\mathrm{N}\)} for all
three positions. The commanded TCP moment about \mbox{\(t_1\)} settled at
approximately \mbox{\(+3.28\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at
\mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, \mbox{\(+0.66\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at the TCP, and
\mbox{\(-2.44\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.

The normal-force variation was therefore small against the change in commanded
moment, which also reversed direction between the two outer positions. The
corresponding contact-establishment responses differed by
\mbox{\(7.73^\circ\)}. These measurements associate the response difference
primarily with the additional moment introduced by the displaced impedance
reference.

\mbox{\Cref{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}} defines
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} from the measured end orientation back to the held
entry reference. Its direction is therefore opposite to the start-to-end
rotation of the end effector. At \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} the
commanded moment \mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} points along \mbox{\(-t_1\)}, and so
does the measured start-to-end end-effector rotation. The reported response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} points along \mbox{\(+t_1\)}.

A tangential CoC displacement couples the translational press to the rotational
impedance through the contribution \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)} of
\mbox{\Cref{eq:commanded_moment_decomposition}}. For the normal-force
component, reversing the tangential displacement reverses
\mbox{\(r_c\times f_n\)}. The two outer positions of
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench}} therefore generated moments in opposite
directions.

At the TCP, \mbox{\(r_c=0\)} and the CoC-induced moment is zero, so the
TCP-centred condition favours neither direction of rotation. It is the neutral
fixed centre for the reported \mbox{\(t_1\)} study. Its measured end-effector
rotation was towards parallel alignment with the configured surface in both
tested rotational directions. A fixed displaced CoC instead
favours one direction and opposes the other, following
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:coc_tangential_displacement}}, and it retains that
preference while the press continues. Increasing the alignment response with a
displaced CoC therefore requires selecting the displacement direction for the
expected direction of the initial angular offset.

Across Cases~A--D, the tangential CoC position produced the largest measured
response variation. Its outer positions produced response spans of
\mbox{\(7.69^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(11.21^\circ\)} for the positive- and
negative-offset conditions. Rotational stiffness produced a
\mbox{\(4.84^\circ\)} span, and cross-axis translational stiffness
\mbox{\(0.03^\circ\)}.

The effect was strongly direction dependent. Relative to the TCP-centred
response magnitudes, the CoC position selected for each offset direction
increased the response by \mbox{\(4.0\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(4.3\,\%\)}. The
position in the other displacement direction reduced it by
\mbox{\(97.6\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(99.2\,\%\)}. Within the tested range, the
main effect of a fixed displaced CoC was therefore not a large increase above
the TCP-centred response. It was the strong selection between the two
rotational directions.

\section{Null-Space Pose-Hold Results}
\label{sec:results_nullspace}

The redundant joint-space response was measured in the null-space pose-hold
experiment while the Cartesian pose was held. The selectable null-space terms
were compared in matched trials under the same virtual disturbance. The commanded disturbance
reached \mbox{\(20.00\,\mathrm{N}\)} in all twelve trials, with peak equivalent
joint-torque norms between \mbox{\(2.17\)} and
\mbox{\(2.45\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. A torque-limit scale of one preserved the
complete waveform.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_NS_nullspace_automatic.pdf}
\caption{Null-space pose-hold response.}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic} shows the response during the
Cartesian pose-hold disturbance test in three panels. Panel (a) gives the mean
cumulative projected null-space motion \mbox{\(E_N\)} for the inactive and
projected-damping modes, the shaded bands showing \mbox{\(\pm\)} one
standard deviation across the three repetitions. Panel (b) gives
\mbox{\(\Delta\sigma_{\min,\mathrm{dist}}\)} and the peak Cartesian position
error for both singular-value conditioning magnitudes. Its error bars show
\mbox{\(\pm\)} one standard deviation, and the horizontal line marks the
\mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} position-retention limit. Panel (c) gives the net
redundant displacement \mbox{\(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\)} of all four
settings, with the mean printed above each bar and error bars showing
\mbox{\(\pm\)} one standard deviation.

\subsection{Projected Null-Space Damping}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_damping}

Projected null-space damping reduced the cumulative projected null-space motion
\mbox{\(E_N\)} from \mbox{\(7.596^\circ\)} in the inactive mode to
\mbox{\(5.687^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)}; panel~(a) of
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}} shows both traces. The net
redundant displacement decreased in the same proportion, from
\mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} to \mbox{\(0.098\,\mathrm{rad}\)}. Over the
same interval, \mbox{\(\Delta\sigma_{\min,\mathrm{dist}}\)} changed from
\mbox{\(-2.13\times10^{-3}\)} to \mbox{\(-1.26\times10^{-3}\)}. The damping
torque of \mbox{\Cref{eq:nullspace_damping_torque}} opposes the projected joint
velocity and therefore reduces existing redundant motion; at zero projected
velocity it is zero.

\subsection{Singular-Value Conditioning}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_sigma}

Singular-value conditioning was active during the \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)}
preceding the disturbance and remained active throughout it. As a result, it
shaped the redundant entry configuration and continued to drive the
configuration during the disturbance. Both
singular-value-conditioning settings ended the
disturbance interval with a net redundant displacement close to zero:
\mbox{\(0.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} at
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and
\mbox{\(-0.1\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} at \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}.
Both values are more than two orders of magnitude smaller than the
\mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} measured in the inactive mode in panel~(c)
of \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}}. Over the same interval
\mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} increased by approximately
\mbox{\(1.95\times10^{-5}\)} and \mbox{\(1.93\times10^{-5}\)}. A change of
\mbox{\(-2.13\times10^{-3}\)} was measured in the inactive mode. With the tested
disturbance direction, the conditioning term strongly reduced the resulting
redundant displacement.

Despite the similar suppression of net displacement, the two conditioning
magnitudes produced substantially different cumulative motion. At
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, \mbox{\(E_N\)} was
\mbox{\(1.619^\circ\)}, compared with \mbox{\(0.283^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, a factor of about six. The conditioning term
switches a fixed torque magnitude between the selected directions and zero,
according to the sampled singular-value difference and its deadband. The larger
cumulative motion at \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} is therefore consistent with
greater back-and-forth redundant motion around a similar final region.

Cartesian position retention also remained within the acceptance limit. The
mean peak Cartesian position error was \mbox{\(0.889\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and \mbox{\(0.983\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at
\mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The largest position error measured in the
complete pose-hold study was \mbox{\(1.304\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, below the
predefined \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} acceptance limit. Of the two tested
magnitudes, \mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} achieved comparable suppression of
net disturbance-induced displacement with substantially less redundant motion.

\Added{Projected damping dissipates existing redundant joint motion, whereas
the singular-value term drives the configuration towards the sampled neighbour
with the larger \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)}. Both terms act through the
null-space projector and preserve the first-order Cartesian objective.}

At \mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, conditioning reduced the net
redundant-displacement magnitude by approximately \mbox{\(99.8\,\%\)} relative
to the \mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} measured in the inactive mode.
