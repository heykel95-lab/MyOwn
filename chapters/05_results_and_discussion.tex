\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results_discussion}

The surface-contact experiments measured how the controller parameters affected
the angular error during Contact Establishment. The primary quantity is the
angular-error component \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{err},t_1}(t_{\mathrm{end}})\)}
relative to the calibrated surface reference, defined in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}}.
Its magnitude gives the remaining error about the first surface tangent.
Positive and negative values identify the rotational direction relative to that
reference. A change through zero reverses the first-tangent error direction.

The measured pose-based angular offset \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}\)}
is the value at contact entry and identifies every angular condition.
At the TCP-centred condition, the nominal-zero command gave
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}=0.69^\circ\)}.
The \mbox{\(10^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} commands produced mean measured
offsets of \mbox{\(9.31^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-9.41^\circ\)}, respectively.
These are named the positive-offset and negative-offset conditions throughout
this chapter.

Each contact parameter setting was repeated three times. The comparison values
are arithmetic means of the angular errors from those repetitions.
\Cref{app:additional_plots} tabulates the corresponding sample standard
deviations. The endpoint components were stored to \mbox{\(0.01^\circ\)}, as
described in \mbox{\Cref{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}}.

\subsection{Quasi-Static Plausibility Assessment}
\label{subsec:results_plausibility}

The translational perturbation reached \mbox{\(-19.65\,\mathrm{mm}\)} relative
to its reference state, while the rotational perturbation reached
\mbox{\(6.565^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_1\)} relative to its reference state.
The quasi-static predictions are evaluated at these measured displacements
using \mbox{\(K_{p,n}=1000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} and
\mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}=15\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}.

The quasi-static prediction of the normal force is
\mbox{\(-19.650\,\mathrm{N}\)}, against a commanded mean of
\mbox{\(-19.640\,\mathrm{N}\)} and a model-estimated mean of
\mbox{\(-22.292\,\mathrm{N}\)}. In the stationary interval, the quasi-static
spring prediction differed from the commanded force by \mbox{\(0.05\,\%\)},
while the model-estimated force differed from the command by
\mbox{\(13.50\,\%\)}. The close agreement between the spring prediction and the
commanded value supports the plausibility of the implemented translational
impedance relation.

\section{Main Surface-Contact Results}
\label{sec:results_main_contact_cases}

\subsection{Baseline Angular Error with the CoC at the TCP: Case~A}
\label{sec:results_case_a}

In Case~A, the \abbr{CoC} was placed at the TCP, so \mbox{\(r_c=0\)} and the
CoC-induced moment \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)} was zero. Contact Establishment was
tested with three configured pre-contact orientation offsets about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}: \mbox{\(0^\circ\)}, \mbox{\(10^\circ\)}, and
\mbox{\(-10^\circ\)}. These conditions establish the TCP-centred baseline for
the following stiffness and CoC-position comparisons.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/ch05/results_case_a_bars.tex}
\caption{Angular error with the CoC at the TCP.}
\label{fig:results_case_a}
\end{figure}

For the positive-offset condition, the angular error decreased from the entry
value of \mbox{\(9.31^\circ\)} to \mbox{\(1.75^\circ\)} at contact end in
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_a}}.
For the negative-offset condition, it changed from \mbox{\(-9.41^\circ\)} to
\mbox{\(1.41^\circ\)}. The negative-offset component crossed zero and ended
with a positive value.
The angular error magnitudes were smaller than their respective entry magnitudes
by \mbox{\(81.2\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(85.0\,\%\)}.

For the nominal-zero condition, the angular error increased from
\mbox{\(0.69^\circ\)} to \mbox{\(1.65^\circ\)}.
This condition therefore showed an increase in the first-tangent error.
The TCP-centred condition reduced that error for both larger entry offsets,
with different final values and a zero crossing in the negative-offset
condition.

\subsection{Rotational Stiffness: Case~B}
\label{sec:results_case_b}

Case~B investigated how the rotational stiffness about \mbox{\(t_1\)}
affects the angular error. Contact Establishment was performed
with the positive \mbox{\(10^\circ\)} configured orientation offset while
\mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} was compared at \mbox{\(5\)}, \mbox{\(15\)}, and
\mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. The \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}
condition is the TCP-centred baseline from Case~A, while the remaining
controller settings were kept unchanged.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/ch05/results_case_b_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Angular error by rotational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_b}
\end{figure}

Increasing \mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} from \mbox{\(5\)} to
\mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} increased the angular error from
\mbox{\(1.75^\circ\)} to \mbox{\(6.58^\circ\)} in
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_b}}.
The intermediate stiffness of \mbox{\(15\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} gave
\mbox{\(2.04^\circ\)}.
The endpoint increase across the tested range was \mbox{\(4.83^\circ\)}, or
\mbox{\(276.2\,\%\)} of the error at \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}.
Higher rotational stiffness resisted the contact-induced end-effector rotation
and left a larger angular error relative to the calibrated surface.

\subsection{Translational Stiffness Along \texorpdfstring{\(t_2\)}{t2}: Case~C}
\label{sec:results_case_c}

Case~C investigated the influence of the translational stiffness along
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, perpendicular to the measured rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
Contact Establishment was performed with the positive \mbox{\(10^\circ\)}
configured orientation offset while \mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}\)} was compared at
\mbox{\(300\)}, \mbox{\(800\)}, and \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}. The
rotational stiffness was kept at
\mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}=5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}, while the translational stiffness
along \mbox{\(t_1\)} remained \mbox{\(K_{p,t_1}=2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}. The
\mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}=2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} condition is the corresponding
TCP-centred reference from Case~A.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/ch05/results_case_c_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Angular error by translational stiffness along \(t_2\).}
\label{fig:results_case_c}
\end{figure}

The angular errors were \mbox{\(1.68^\circ\)}, \mbox{\(1.69^\circ\)}
and \mbox{\(1.75^\circ\)} at translational stiffnesses of
\mbox{\(300\)}, \mbox{\(800\)} and \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}, respectively,
in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_c}}.
Their span was \mbox{\(0.07^\circ\)}, or \mbox{\(3.8\,\%\)} of the
TCP-centred reference error at \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}.
This variation was considerably smaller than the rotational-stiffness effect.
The result concerns translation along \mbox{\(t_2\)}, with the
\mbox{\(t_1\)} translational stiffness held constant.

\subsection{Effect of Tangential CoC Position: Case~D}
\label{sec:results_case_d}

Case~D investigated how the tangential \abbr{CoC} position affects the
angular error. The \abbr{CoC} was displaced along
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, perpendicular to the measured rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)},
and Contact Establishment was performed for both the positive
\mbox{\(10^\circ\)} and negative \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} configured orientation
offsets. The compared \abbr{CoC} positions were
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-100\)}, \mbox{\(-90\)}, \mbox{\(-80\)},
\mbox{\(-40\)}, \mbox{\(-20\)}, \mbox{\(-10\)}, \mbox{\(0\)},
\mbox{\(10\)}, \mbox{\(20\)}, \mbox{\(40\)}, \mbox{\(80\)},
\mbox{\(90\)}, and \mbox{\(100\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, where
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=0\)} is the TCP-centred reference from Case~A. Cartesian
stiffness, damping, and the remaining controller settings were kept
unchanged.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/ch05/results_case_d_panels.tex}
\caption{Angular error by tangential CoC position.}
\label{fig:results_case_d}
\end{figure}

The angular error depended on the \abbr{CoC} position and entry
direction. For the positive-offset condition,
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}=9.33^\circ\)},
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d}} shows the angular error decreasing from
\mbox{\(31.77^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-100\,\mathrm{mm}\)} to
\mbox{\(0.83^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(100\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.
The latter was the smallest mean error magnitude among the tested positions
for this entry direction. It was \mbox{\(52.6\,\%\)} below the
\mbox{\(1.75^\circ\)} TCP-centred reference.

For the negative-offset condition,
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}=-9.38^\circ\)}, the angular error changed
from \mbox{\(2.76^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(-100\,\mathrm{mm}\)} to
\mbox{\(-33.98^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(100\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.
The smallest measured mean magnitude was \mbox{\(0.94^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(10\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, which was \mbox{\(33.7\,\%\)} below the
\mbox{\(1.41^\circ\)} TCP-centred reference.
At \mbox{\(-100\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, the first-tangent error component crossed
zero and ended with a larger magnitude than at the TCP.
An increased contact-induced rotation therefore did not necessarily reduce
the angular error.

The two entry directions gave their smallest measured mean error magnitudes
at different tested positions. A \abbr{CoC} setting that reduces the error
for one entry direction can leave a larger error for the other.
The largest sample standard deviation was \mbox{\(1.33^\circ\)}, for the
positive-offset condition at \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-100\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.

To relate the angular error to the interaction wrench, the model-estimated
external wrench was evaluated for the positive-offset condition.
The three \abbr{CoC} positions were tested with a configured pre-contact offset
of \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}=10^\circ\)}.
The compared positions were \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-40\)}, \mbox{\(0\)} and
\mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Each trace shows one measured repetition for
the corresponding centre position.

The model-estimated external force and moment supplied by libfranka are
evaluated as the measured interaction wrench available from the robot. The
\mbox{\(\mathrm{est}\)} index is retained because they are obtained from the
robot's model-based external-wrench estimator rather than from a separate
force/torque sensor. The estimated moment is expressed about the TCP before
being resolved about the surface tangent, so the three \abbr{CoC} positions are
compared at one reference point. The estimated normal-force component and the
estimated TCP moment about the first surface tangent are

\[
F_{n,\mathrm{est}}=n_s^\top f_{\mathrm{est}},
\qquad
M_{t_1,\mathrm{est}}=t_1^\top m_{\mathrm{est,TCP}}.
\]

The normal-force component is negative while the tool presses into the
surface.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/ch05/MAIN_D_wrench.pdf}
\caption{Angular error and model-estimated interaction wrench.}
\label{fig:results_case_d_wrench}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench} compares the angular error
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{err},t_1}(t)\)} in panel~(a), the model-estimated normal force
\mbox{\(F_{n,\mathrm{est}}\)} in panel~(b), and the model-estimated TCP moment
about the first surface tangent \mbox{\(M_{t_1,\mathrm{est}}\)} in panel~(c),
on a common time axis for the three centre positions. Over the final second of
contact establishment, the estimated normal force settled at approximately
\mbox{\(-82.9\,\mathrm{N}\)} at \mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)},
\mbox{\(-79.0\,\mathrm{N}\)} at the TCP, and \mbox{\(-78.0\,\mathrm{N}\)} at
\mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The estimated TCP moment about \mbox{\(t_1\)}
settled at approximately \mbox{\(4.07\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)},
\mbox{\(0.73\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and \mbox{\(-2.54\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at the same
three positions.

The estimated normal force remained similar for the three \abbr{CoC}
positions. The estimated TCP moment changed substantially and reversed
direction between the two displaced positions.
The angular errors in the illustrated trials were approximately
\mbox{\(9.12^\circ\)}, \mbox{\(1.75^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(1.49^\circ\)},
at \mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, the TCP and \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, respectively.
The comparison links the changed angular error mainly to the changed contact
moment, while the normal force remained similar.

For the two traces in panel~(a) of \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench}},
the approach to the angular error was compared using a common band.
The comparison time is the first recorded instant after which the component
stays within \mbox{\(0.1^\circ\)} of its own final value through contact end.
This condition was reached at about \mbox{\(2.5\,\mathrm{s}\)} for
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} and \mbox{\(3.6\,\mathrm{s}\)} at the TCP.
The displaced-centre trace reached the band approximately
\mbox{\(1.1\,\mathrm{s}\)} earlier.

At the TCP, \mbox{\(r_c=0\)}, so the additional CoC-induced moment vanishes.
The rotational spring and damper remain active.
A displaced \abbr{CoC} adds a preferred moment direction and changes the
angular evolution during contact. Its angular error depends on both the entry
direction and how far the measured tool-normal component moves relative to
the calibrated reference.

\section{Null-Space Pose-Hold Results}
\label{sec:results_nullspace}

The redundant joint-space response was measured with a fixed Cartesian pose
reference under the same virtual disturbance. Four settings were compared:
no null-space torque, projected damping, singular-value conditioning, and
combined damping and conditioning. The enabled values were
\mbox{\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)} and
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}.
The commanded disturbance reached \mbox{\(20.00\,\mathrm{N}\)} in all twelve
trials. Peak equivalent joint-torque norms ranged from
\mbox{\(2.16\)} to \mbox{\(2.45\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, with a torque-limit
scale of one throughout the disturbance.

The figures show three-trial means and one sample standard deviation.
The measured interval from \mbox{\(5\)} to \mbox{\(9\,\mathrm{s}\)} is
displayed from \mbox{\(0\)} to \mbox{\(4\,\mathrm{s}\)}, relative to
disturbance onset. In all twelve trials, the maximum measured TCP position
error remained below \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. This criterion assesses
position only.

\subsection{Cumulative Joint Motion}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_damping}

\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_cumulative} shows the cumulative joint motion
\mbox{\(E_N(t)\)} during the disturbance. Its mean endpoint decreased from
\mbox{\(7.60^\circ\)} without null-space torque to
\mbox{\(5.69^\circ\)} with projected damping, a reduction of
\mbox{\(25.1\,\%\)} relative to the inactive setting.
At \mbox{\(k_\sigma=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, conditioning alone produced
\mbox{\(1.69^\circ\)} of cumulative joint motion. Adding projected damping
reduced this value to \mbox{\(0.83^\circ\)}, a reduction of
\mbox{\(50.7\,\%\)} relative to conditioning alone.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/ch05/nullspace_cumulative_main.pdf}
\caption{Cumulative joint motion during the virtual disturbance.}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_cumulative}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}
\end{figure}

Projected damping opposes the projected joint velocity. The conditioning term
selects a torque direction from the sampled singular-value difference.
Both terms act through the null-space torque projector.
Their combination reduced the accumulated redundant motion in the tested
pose-hold configuration.

\subsection{Jacobian Conditioning}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_sigma}

\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_conditioning} shows the minimum singular value
\mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}(t)\)} for the same four settings.
It decreased during the disturbance without null-space torque and with
projected damping alone. With conditioning alone and with combined control,
the curves remained close to their respective disturbance-entry values.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/ch05/nullspace_conditioning_main.pdf}
\caption{Jacobian conditioning during the virtual disturbance.}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_conditioning}
\end{figure}

Conditioning was active during the \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} preceding the
disturbance and remained active throughout it. The comparison therefore
includes the redundant configurations reached during this settling interval.
The conditioning objective drives the configuration towards a local region
with a larger minimum singular value. Adding damping reduced cumulative joint
motion while retaining this behaviour in the measured trajectories.

\subsection{Joint Motion Over Time}
\label{subsec:results_joint_motion}

\Cref{fig:results_joint_motion} shows the mean measured joint-1 angle change
\mbox{\(\Delta q_1(t)\)} relative to disturbance onset.
Panel~(a) compares all four settings over the complete disturbance interval.
Panel~(b) enlarges the conditioning and combined-control traces over the same
\mbox{\(0\)--\(4\,\mathrm{s}\)} interval.
The mean joint-angle change was substantially smaller with either
conditioning setting than without conditioning.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/ch05/joint_motion_mean_main.pdf}
\caption{Joint motion during the virtual disturbance.}
\label{fig:results_joint_motion}
\end{figure}

The combined setting showed a smaller mean joint-angle change during the
constant-force part of the disturbance than conditioning alone.
Both means decreased towards their onset values as the disturbance was
released. The shaded bands describe the spread between trials at each instant.
The joint-1 trace illustrates the temporal response, while the cumulative
projected joint motion in \Cref{fig:results_nullspace_cumulative} evaluates
motion across all seven joints.
