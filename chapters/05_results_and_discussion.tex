\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results_discussion}

The influence of the controller parameters on the end-effector rotation
generated during Contact Establishment was measured in the surface-contact
experiments. The
primary response quantity is the contact-establishment response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} about surface tangent
\mbox{\(t_1\)}, defined in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}}. It represents the
end-of-contact-establishment orientation change relative to the orientation reference held
through contact establishment. When the end effector rotates towards the
configured flat direction, \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} points in the same
surface-tangent direction as the achieved initial angular offset
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. Its size is
\mbox{\(\lvert\gamma_{t_1}\rvert\)}.

The configured orientation-offset component
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}\)} established the pre-contact reference
condition. The achieved pose-based initial angular offset
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)} is the angle reached at contact entry, and
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} is the measured response. Accordingly, the main response
figures identify each condition by its achieved offset. Directional conditions use
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}; the nominal zero-offset condition uses the full
initial angular-offset magnitude \mbox{\(\phi_0\)}.
\Cref{app:additional_plots} tabulates the corresponding numerical values.

\Added{Each contact parameter setting was repeated three times. The comparative
response values in Cases~A--D are the arithmetic means over those repetitions,
and \mbox{\Cref{app:additional_plots}} tabulates their sample standard
deviations.} At the TCP, a configured orientation offset of
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} defined the desired pre-contact direction and produced a
mean achieved initial angular offset of \mbox{\(+9.31^\circ\)} about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}. The configured \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} offset produced an
achieved offset of \mbox{\(-9.41^\circ\)}.

\section{Main Surface-Contact Results}
\label{sec:results_main_contact_cases}

\subsection{Baseline Contact Response with the CoC at the TCP: Case~A}
\label{sec:results_case_a}

In Case~A the \abbr{CoC} was placed at the TCP, so
\mbox{\(r_c=0\)} and \mbox{\(r_c\times f=0\)}. The ordinary rotational
impedance and physical contact geometry remained active. The measured response
formed the baseline for the stiffness and compliance-centre-position
comparisons.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_a_bars.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response with the CoC at the TCP.}
\label{fig:results_case_a}
\end{figure}

For the direction of rotation generated by the configured
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} offset about \mbox{\(t_1\)}, the measured response was
\mbox{\(+7.57^\circ\)} in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_a}}. The direction
generated by the configured \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} offset produced
\mbox{\(-10.83^\circ\)}. Both responses represented end-effector rotation
towards the configured flat direction.

For comparison, the nominal zero orientation offset produced
\mbox{\(\phi_0=0.74^\circ\)} and gave
\mbox{\(-0.97^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. This value forms the measured
zero-offset reference. The measured response-to-entry-magnitude ratios were
\mbox{\(81.3\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(115.1\,\%\)} for the directions generated by
the configured \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} offsets,
respectively.

\subsection{Effect of Cartesian Stiffness: Cases~B and~C}

\subsubsection{Rotational Stiffness: Case~B}
\label{sec:results_case_b}

In Case~B the rotational stiffness about the investigated tangent was varied
while the other controller settings were held at their baseline values. The
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} condition is the corresponding TCP-centred
reference from Case~A.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_b_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response by rotational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_b}
\end{figure}

Increasing \mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} reduced the measured response over the tested
range, from
\mbox{\(+7.57^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} to
\mbox{\(+2.73^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} in
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_b}}. Every measured response remained directed
towards the configured flat direction. The
\mbox{\(4.84^\circ\)} reduction equalled
\mbox{\(63.9\,\%\)} of the \mbox{\(7.57^\circ\)} response measured at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}.

\subsubsection{Cross-Axis Translational Stiffness: Case~C}
\label{sec:results_case_c}

In Case~C the translational stiffness perpendicular to the investigated
rotation axis was varied. The rotational stiffness about that axis was held at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. The \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}
condition is the corresponding TCP-centred reference from Case~A.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_c_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response by cross-axis translational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_c}
\end{figure}

The measured response spanned \mbox{\(+7.56^\circ\)} to
\mbox{\(+7.59^\circ\)} across the tested range in
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_c}}. This \mbox{\(0.03^\circ\)} span equalled
\mbox{\(0.4\,\%\)} of the \mbox{\(7.57^\circ\)} TCP-centred reference and
was considerably smaller than the rotational-stiffness effect.

\subsection{Effect of Tangential CoC Position: Case~D}
\label{sec:results_case_d}

In Case~D the tangential position of the \abbr{CoC} was varied along
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, perpendicular to the measured response about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}. Cartesian stiffness and damping were held at their baseline
values. The
TCP-centred condition is included as the zero-position reference.
\Added{The largest Case-D sample standard deviation was
\mbox{\(0.44^\circ\)} for the \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} configured direction at
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-20\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_panels.tex}
\caption[Contact response by tangential CoC position.]{\Added{Contact response by tangential CoC position.}}
\label{fig:results_case_d}
\end{figure}

Changing the tested direction of rotation changed which displacement direction
from the TCP produced the larger response. For the direction generated by the
configured \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} offset,
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d}} shows \mbox{\(+7.87^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} and \mbox{\(+0.18^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. For the direction generated by the configured
\mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} offset, the corresponding responses were
\mbox{\(-11.30^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-0.09^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} and \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, respectively.

Every tested non-zero tangential \abbr{CoC} increased the magnitude of the
end-effector rotation towards the configured flat direction for one tested
rotational direction and reduced it for the other relative to the TCP-centred
condition. The displacement direction that increased the response depended on
the achieved initial angular offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}.

To relate this direction dependence to the commanded wrench, the direction
generated by the configured \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} offset about \mbox{\(t_1\)}
was examined in the time domain. Its two outer positions produced
a large difference in \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)}, and the TCP-centred condition
formed the zero-position reference. Each trace shows one measured repetition
for the corresponding centre position.

The commanded Cartesian wrench \mbox{\(F=[f^\top,m^\top]^\top\)} was resolved
for the time histories as

\[
F_n=n_s^\top f,
\qquad
M_{t_1}=t_1^\top m.
\]

The normal-force component is negative while the tool presses into the
surface. Both quantities are controller commands.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_wrench_relabel.tex}
\caption[Contact response and commanded wrench.]{\Added{Contact response and commanded wrench.}}
\label{fig:results_case_d_wrench}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench} places the contact-establishment response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} in panel~(a), the commanded normal force
\mbox{\(F_n\)} in panel~(b), and the commanded TCP moment
\mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} in panel~(c), on a common time axis for three centre
positions. Every condition shown uses
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}=+10^\circ\)}, corresponding to a mean
achieved pose-based initial angular offset of approximately
\mbox{\(+9.32^\circ\)}. At
\mbox{\(-40\)}, \mbox{\(0\)}, and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, the commanded normal force settles in a similar
range of approximately \mbox{\(-77\)} to \mbox{\(-82\,\mathrm{N}\)}, while the
commanded TCP moment about \mbox{\(t_1\)} settles at approximately
\mbox{\(+3.28\)}, \mbox{\(+0.66\)}, and \mbox{\(-2.44\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The
outer positions produced moments in opposite directions and a
\mbox{\(7.73^\circ\)} difference in contact-establishment response. The
commanded normal force, by contrast, occupied a \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N}\)} range. These
measurements associate the response difference primarily with the moment
generated by the displaced impedance reference.

At the selected \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} condition, the commanded moment
\mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} points along \mbox{\(-t_1\)}, while
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} points along \mbox{\(+t_1\)}. This response is formed from the measured end-effector
orientation back to the reference held through contact establishment, as
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}} defines it. The measured
start-to-end end-effector rotation pointed along \mbox{\(-t_1\)}.

A tangential displacement of the \abbr{CoC} moves the impedance reference
point away from the TCP, so the translational press is coupled to the rotational
part of the impedance. According to
\mbox{\Cref{eq:commanded_moment_decomposition}}, a non-zero tangential
displacement adds the contribution \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)} from the commanded
press. This coupling produced the strong change in commanded moment across
centre positions. Reversing the tangential displacement reverses the
contribution \mbox{\(r_c\times f_n\)} generated by the normal press.

This direction dependence follows
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:coc_tangential_displacement}}. A fixed tangential
\mbox{\(r_c\neq0\)} retains one preferred moment direction while the press
continues. At \mbox{\(r_c=0\)}, the point-shift contribution satisfies
\mbox{\(r_c\times f=0\)}. The TCP-centred condition provided a neutral fixed
reference with no direction-selected lever. The measured end-effector rotation
was towards the configured flat direction in both tested directions of
rotation. Every displaced position
increased the response in one direction and reduced it in the other relative to
the TCP-centred response. Additional
rotational authority required selecting the centre position for the
known direction of rotation.

Across Cases~A--D, tangential CoC position produced the largest measured
response variation. Its outer-position spans were \mbox{\(7.69^\circ\)} and
\mbox{\(11.21^\circ\)} for the directions generated by the configured
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} offsets. These spans were
compared with \mbox{\(4.84^\circ\)} for rotational stiffness and
\mbox{\(0.03^\circ\)} for cross-axis translational stiffness. Relative to the
TCP-centred response magnitudes, the outer position selected for each direction
increased the response by \mbox{\(4.0\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(4.3\,\%\)}. The outer
position in the other displacement direction reduced it by
\mbox{\(97.6\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(99.2\,\%\)}, respectively.

\section{Null-Space Pose-Hold Results}
\label{sec:results_nullspace}

The redundant joint-space response was measured in the null-space pose-hold
experiment while the Cartesian pose was held. The selectable null-space terms
were compared in matched trials under the same internally commanded
point-force-equivalent disturbance. The commanded disturbance
reached \mbox{\(20.00\,\mathrm{N}\)} in all twelve trials, with peak equivalent
joint-torque norms between \mbox{\(2.17\)} and
\mbox{\(2.45\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. A torque-limit scale of one preserved the
complete waveform.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_NS_nullspace_automatic.pdf}
\caption{Null-space pose-hold response.}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic} shows the response during the
Cartesian pose-hold disturbance test in three panels. Panel (a) gives the mean
cumulative projected null-space motion \mbox{\(E_N\)} for the inactive and
projected-damping modes, the shaded bands showing \mbox{\(\pm\)} one
standard deviation across the three repetitions. Panel (b) gives
\mbox{\(\Delta\sigma_{\min,\mathrm{dist}}\)} and the peak Cartesian position
error for both singular-value conditioning magnitudes. Its error bars show
\mbox{\(\pm\)} one standard deviation, and the horizontal line marks the
\mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} position-retention limit. Panel (c) gives the net
redundant displacement \mbox{\(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\)} of all four
settings, with the mean printed above each bar and error bars showing
\mbox{\(\pm\)} one standard deviation.

\subsection{Projected Null-Space Damping}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_damping}

Projected null-space damping reduced \mbox{\(E_N\)} from
\mbox{\(7.596^\circ\)} in the inactive mode to \mbox{\(5.687^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)}; panel~(a) of
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}} shows both traces. The net
redundant displacement decreased in
the same proportion, from \mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} to
\mbox{\(0.098\,\mathrm{rad}\)}, while the change in \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)}
across the disturbance interval became less negative, from
\mbox{\(-2.13\times10^{-3}\)} to \mbox{\(-1.26\times10^{-3}\)}. This
behaviour follows from the projected damping law of
\mbox{\Cref{eq:nullspace_damping_torque}}. At zero projected joint velocity,
the damping torque equals zero.

\subsection{Singular-Value Conditioning}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_sigma}

Singular-value conditioning was active during the \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)}
preceding the disturbance and remained active throughout it. As a result, it
shaped the redundant entry configuration and continued to drive the
configuration during the disturbance. Both
singular-value-conditioning settings ended the
disturbance interval with a net redundant displacement close to zero:
\mbox{\(0.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} at
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and
\mbox{\(-0.1\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} at \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}.
Both values are more than two orders of magnitude smaller than the
\mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} measured in the inactive mode in panel~(c)
of \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}}. Over the same interval
\mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} increased by approximately
\mbox{\(1.95\times10^{-5}\)} and \mbox{\(1.93\times10^{-5}\)}. A change of
\mbox{\(-2.13\times10^{-3}\)} was measured in the inactive mode. With the tested
disturbance direction, the conditioning term strongly reduced the resulting
redundant displacement.

Despite their similar suppression of net displacement, the two conditioning
magnitudes differed in cumulative motion. At
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, the cumulative projected null-space
motion was \mbox{\(1.619^\circ\)}, compared with \mbox{\(0.283^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, a factor of about six. Net displacement
magnitudes at or below \mbox{\(0.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} were
measured for both settings. The conditioning term commands a
fixed torque magnitude along the selected direction, while the comparison
deadband sets \mbox{\(s_\sigma=0\)}. The factor-of-six increase in cumulative
motion quantified the greater switching activity around the same region.

Cartesian position retention also remained within the acceptance limit. The
mean peak Cartesian position error was \mbox{\(0.889\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and \mbox{\(0.983\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at
\mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The largest position error measured in the
complete pose-hold study was \mbox{\(1.304\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, below the
predefined \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} acceptance limit. Of the two tested
magnitudes, \mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} achieved comparable suppression of
net disturbance-induced displacement with substantially less redundant motion.

\Added{Projected damping dissipates existing redundant joint motion, whereas
the singular-value term drives the configuration towards the sampled neighbour
with the larger \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)}. Both terms act through the
null-space projector and preserve the first-order Cartesian objective.}

At \mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, conditioning reduced the net
redundant-displacement magnitude by approximately \mbox{\(99.8\,\%\)} relative
to the \mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} measured in the inactive mode.
