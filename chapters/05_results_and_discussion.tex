\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results_discussion}

The surface-contact experiments measured how the controller parameters affected
the end-effector rotation during Contact Establishment. The primary response
quantity is the contact-establishment response \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} about
surface tangent \mbox{\(t_1\)}, defined in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}}.

The achieved pose-based initial angular offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}
describes the tool orientation at contact entry relative to the configured
surface reference. The response \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} is calculated from
the measured end orientation back to the orientation held at contact entry. Its
direction is therefore opposite to the start-to-end rotation of the end
effector. When contact rotates the end effector towards parallel alignment with
the configured surface, \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} points in the same
surface-tangent direction as \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. The magnitude
\mbox{\(\lvert\gamma_{t_1}\rvert\)} gives the size of that response.

The orientation reached at contact entry differed slightly from the
configured offset \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}\)}. The response
figures therefore identify the directional test conditions by the achieved
offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. For the nominal zero-offset condition, no
angular rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)} was commanded. Small residual angular
components about the surface tangents can nevertheless remain in the achieved
tool orientation, including a component about \mbox{\(t_2\)}. The initial
condition is therefore reported by the total angular-mismatch magnitude
\mbox{\(\phi_0\)}, rather than only by the \mbox{\(t_1\)} component
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. \Cref{app:additional_plots} tabulates the
corresponding numerical values.

\Added{Each contact parameter setting was repeated three times. The comparative
response values in Cases~A--D are the arithmetic means over those repetitions,
and \mbox{\Cref{app:additional_plots}} tabulates their sample standard
deviations.} At the TCP-centred condition, the zero-offset case had
\mbox{\(\phi_0=0.74^\circ\)}, while the \mbox{\(10^\circ\)} and
\mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} commands produced mean achieved offsets of
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=9.31^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-9.41^\circ\)},
respectively. These two are named the positive-offset and negative-offset
conditions throughout this chapter.

\section{Main Surface-Contact Results}
\label{sec:results_main_contact_cases}

\subsection{Baseline Contact Response with the CoC at the TCP: Case~A}
\label{sec:results_case_a}

In Case~A, the \abbr{CoC} was placed at the TCP, so \mbox{\(r_c=0\)} and the
CoC-induced moment \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)} was zero. Contact Establishment was
tested with three configured pre-contact orientation offsets about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}: \mbox{\(0^\circ\)}, \mbox{\(10^\circ\)}, and
\mbox{\(-10^\circ\)}. These conditions establish the TCP-centred baseline for
the following stiffness and CoC-position comparisons.

For the nominal zero-offset condition, the initial condition is reported by the
total angular-mismatch magnitude \mbox{\(\phi_0\)}. For the
\mbox{\(10^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} conditions, the achieved initial
angular offset is reported by \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_a_bars.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response with the CoC at the TCP.}
\label{fig:results_case_a}
\end{figure}

For the positive-offset condition, with
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=9.31^\circ\)}, the measured response was
\mbox{\(7.57^\circ\)} in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_a}}. For the
negative-offset condition, with \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=-9.41^\circ\)}, it was
\mbox{\(-10.83^\circ\)}. Both responses corresponded to end-effector rotation
towards parallel alignment with the configured surface.

With zero configured orientation offset, the initial angular-offset magnitude
was \mbox{\(\phi_0=0.74^\circ\)} and the measured response about
\mbox{\(t_1\)} was \mbox{\(-0.97^\circ\)}. This value forms the measured
zero-offset reference. For the positive- and negative-offset conditions, the
response magnitude was \mbox{\(81.3\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(115.1\,\%\)} of the
respective achieved initial angular-offset magnitude.

Case~A therefore shows that, with the \abbr{CoC} at the TCP, the
contact-induced rotation towards the configured surface-parallel orientation
was almost symmetrical with respect to the two initial rotational directions,
although the measured response magnitudes were different.

\subsection{Effect of Cartesian Stiffness: Cases~B and~C}

\subsubsection{Rotational Stiffness: Case~B}
\label{sec:results_case_b}

Case~B investigated how the rotational stiffness about \mbox{\(t_1\)}
affects the contact-establishment rotation. Contact Establishment was performed
with the positive \mbox{\(10^\circ\)} configured orientation offset while
\mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} was compared at \mbox{\(5\)}, \mbox{\(15\)}, and
\mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. The \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}
condition is the TCP-centred baseline from Case~A, while the remaining
controller settings were kept unchanged.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_b_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response by rotational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_b}
\end{figure}

Increasing \mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} from \mbox{\(5\)} to
\mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} reduced the measured response from
\mbox{\(7.57^\circ\)} to \mbox{\(2.73^\circ\)} in
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_b}}. This is a reduction of
\mbox{\(4.84^\circ\)}, or \mbox{\(63.9\,\%\)} of the response measured at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}.

Increasing the rotational stiffness about \mbox{\(t_1\)} therefore reduced the
tool's ability to rotate towards the configured surface-parallel orientation
during contact.

\subsubsection{Translational Stiffness Along \texorpdfstring{\(t_2\)}{t2}: Case~C}
\label{sec:results_case_c}

Case~C investigated the influence of the translational stiffness along
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, perpendicular to the measured rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
Contact Establishment was performed with the positive \mbox{\(10^\circ\)}
configured orientation offset while \mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}\)} was compared at
\mbox{\(300\)}, \mbox{\(800\)}, and \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}. The
rotational stiffness was kept at
\mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}=5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}, while the translational stiffness
along \mbox{\(t_1\)} remained \mbox{\(K_{p,t_1}=2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}. The
\mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}=2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} condition is the corresponding
TCP-centred reference from Case~A.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_c_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response by translational stiffness along \(t_2\).}
\label{fig:results_case_c}
\end{figure}

Changing the translational stiffness \mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}\)} produced only a
small change in the measured response. Across the tested values,
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} ranged from \mbox{\(7.56^\circ\)} to
\mbox{\(7.59^\circ\)} in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_c}}, giving a span of
\mbox{\(0.03^\circ\)}. This is \mbox{\(0.4\,\%\)} of the
\mbox{\(7.57^\circ\)} TCP-centred reference and considerably smaller than the
rotational-stiffness effect.

The measured alignment response therefore remained nearly unchanged across the
tested translational stiffness values along \mbox{\(t_2\)}.

\subsection{Effect of Tangential CoC Position: Case~D}
\label{sec:results_case_d}

Case~D investigated how the tangential \abbr{CoC} position affects the
contact-establishment rotation. The \abbr{CoC} was displaced along
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, perpendicular to the measured rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)},
and Contact Establishment was performed for both the positive
\mbox{\(10^\circ\)} and negative \mbox{\(-10^\circ\)} configured orientation
offsets. The compared \abbr{CoC} positions were
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-40\)}, \mbox{\(-20\)}, \mbox{\(-10\)}, \mbox{\(0\)},
\mbox{\(10\)}, \mbox{\(20\)}, and \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, where
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=0\)} is the TCP-centred reference from Case~A. Cartesian
stiffness, damping, and the remaining controller settings were kept
unchanged.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_panels.tex}
\caption[Contact response by tangential CoC position.]{\Added{Contact response by tangential CoC position.}}
\label{fig:results_case_d}
\end{figure}

The effect of the \abbr{CoC} displacement depended on the direction of the
achieved initial angular offset. For the positive-offset condition,
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=9.32^\circ\)}, \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d}} shows
the response increasing from \mbox{\(0.18^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} to \mbox{\(7.87^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. For the negative-offset condition,
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=-9.36^\circ\)}, the response also increased
numerically, from \mbox{\(-11.30^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} to
\mbox{\(-0.09^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Because the response is
negative in this condition, its magnitude decreased as \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}\)}
increased.

Increasing \mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}\)} therefore increased the alignment response for
the positive initial angular offset and reduced it for the negative initial
angular offset. \Added{The largest Case-D sample standard deviation was
\mbox{\(0.44^\circ\)}, for the positive-offset condition at
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-20\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.}

To relate this direction dependence to the commanded wrench, the
positive-offset condition was examined in the time domain. All three
\abbr{CoC} positions were tested with a configured pre-contact orientation
offset of \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}=10^\circ\)}, corresponding to a
mean achieved initial angular offset of
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}=9.32^\circ\)}. The compared \abbr{CoC} positions were
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=-40\)}, \mbox{\(0\)}, and \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Each
trace shows one measured repetition for the corresponding centre position.

The commanded Cartesian wrench \mbox{\(F=[f^\top,m^\top]^\top\)} was resolved
for the time histories as

\[
F_n=n_s^\top f,
\qquad
M_{t_1}=t_1^\top m.
\]

The normal-force component is negative while the tool presses into the
surface. Both quantities are controller commands.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_wrench_relabel.tex}
\caption[Contact response and commanded wrench.]{\Added{Contact response and commanded wrench.}}
\label{fig:results_case_d_wrench}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench} places the contact-establishment response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} in panel~(a), the commanded normal force
\mbox{\(F_n\)} in panel~(b), and the commanded TCP moment
\mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} in panel~(c), on a common time axis for the three centre
positions. The commanded normal force settled
between approximately \mbox{\(-77\)} and \mbox{\(-82\,\mathrm{N}\)} for all
three positions. The commanded TCP moment about \mbox{\(t_1\)} settled at
approximately \mbox{\(3.28\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at
\mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, \mbox{\(0.66\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at the TCP, and
\mbox{\(-2.44\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} at \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}.

The commanded normal force changed only slightly between the three \abbr{CoC}
positions, whereas the commanded moment changed substantially and reversed
direction between the two displaced positions. The measured
contact-establishment response \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} changed accordingly,
indicating that the response variation was mainly associated with the
additional moment generated by the displaced \abbr{CoC}. Panel~(a) also shows
the rotation towards alignment developing more quickly at
\mbox{\(r_{c,t_2}=40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} than at the TCP. That trace reaches its
approximately steady response at about \mbox{\(2.3\,\mathrm{s}\)}, and the
TCP trace at about \mbox{\(3.6\,\mathrm{s}\)}. With the \abbr{CoC}
displaced, the rotation reaches that response roughly
\mbox{\(1.3\,\mathrm{s}\)} earlier.

At the TCP, \mbox{\(r_c=0\)}, so no additional CoC-induced moment is generated
and neither rotational direction is favoured. A displaced \abbr{CoC} instead
favours one rotational direction and opposes the other. Its effect therefore
depends on the direction of the initial angular offset, and the \abbr{CoC}
displacement must be selected accordingly if the alignment response is to be
increased.

\section{Null-Space Pose-Hold Results}
\label{sec:results_nullspace}

The redundant joint-space response was measured in the null-space pose-hold
experiment while the Cartesian pose was held. The selectable null-space terms
were compared in matched trials under the same virtual disturbance. The commanded disturbance
reached \mbox{\(20.00\,\mathrm{N}\)} in all twelve trials, with peak equivalent
joint-torque norms between \mbox{\(2.17\)} and
\mbox{\(2.45\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. A torque-limit scale of one preserved the
complete waveform.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_NS_nullspace_automatic.pdf}
\caption{Null-space pose-hold response.}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic} summarises the Cartesian pose-hold
disturbance test in three panels. Panel~(a) shows the mean cumulative projected
null-space motion \mbox{\(E_N\)} for the inactive and projected-damping modes,
with the shaded bands indicating the spread across the repeated trials.
Panel~(b) shows the singular-value change \mbox{\(\Delta\sigma_{\min}\)}
together with the maximum Cartesian position error
\mbox{\(\lVert e_p\rVert_{\max}\)} for the two singular-value-conditioning
settings; the horizontal line marks the \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)}
position-retention limit. Panel~(c) shows the net displacement
\mbox{\(\Delta\eta\)} for all four tested null-space settings.

\subsection{Projected Null-Space Damping}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_damping}

Projected null-space damping reduced the cumulative projected null-space motion
\mbox{\(E_N\)} from \mbox{\(7.596^\circ\)} in the inactive mode to
\mbox{\(5.687^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)}. The net displacement
decreased from \mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} \mbox{(\(7.51^\circ\))} to
\mbox{\(0.098\,\mathrm{rad}\)} \mbox{(\(5.61^\circ\))}. The cumulative motion
and net displacement were nearly equal, which means that only a small part of
the measured motion was cancelled by motion in the opposite direction.
Projected damping therefore reduced the null-space motion generated by the
disturbance.

\subsection{Singular-Value Conditioning}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_sigma}

In the two settings compared here, the singular-value term acted alone, without
projected damping. Conditioning was active during the
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} preceding the disturbance and remained active
throughout it. As a result, it shaped the redundant entry configuration and
continued to drive the configuration during the disturbance. At
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, the absolute net displacement was
\mbox{\(0.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} \mbox{(\(0.017^\circ\))}. At
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, the measured net displacement was
negative, so its absolute value was
\mbox{\(0.1\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} \mbox{(\(0.006^\circ\))}. Both
settings therefore reduced the remaining net displacement to nearly zero under
the tested disturbance direction.

Both values are more than two orders of magnitude smaller than the
\mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} measured in the inactive mode in panel~(c)
of \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}}. Over the same interval
\mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} increased by approximately
\mbox{\(1.95\times10^{-5}\)} and \mbox{\(1.93\times10^{-5}\)}. A change of
\mbox{\(-2.13\times10^{-3}\)} was measured in the inactive mode.

Despite the similar suppression of net displacement, the two conditioning
magnitudes produced substantially different cumulative motion. At
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, \mbox{\(E_N\)} was
\mbox{\(1.619^\circ\)}, compared with \mbox{\(0.283^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, a factor of about six. The conditioning term
switches a fixed torque magnitude between the selected directions and zero,
according to the sampled singular-value difference and its deadband. The larger
cumulative motion at \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} is therefore consistent with
greater back-and-forth redundant motion around a similar final region.

Both conditioning settings preserved the Cartesian position within the
\mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} acceptance limit. The mean maximum Cartesian
position errors were
\mbox{\(\lVert e_p\rVert_{\max}=0.889\,\mathrm{mm}\)} for
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and
\mbox{\(\lVert e_p\rVert_{\max}=0.983\,\mathrm{mm}\)} for
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. These values verify that the Cartesian
position remained within the specified acceptance limit while the joint
configuration changed. The largest position error measured in the complete
pose-hold study was \mbox{\(1.304\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Of the two tested
magnitudes, \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} left the smaller final displacement,
while producing considerably more null-space motion.

\Added{Projected damping dissipates existing redundant joint motion, whereas
the singular-value term drives the configuration towards the sampled neighbour
with the larger \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)}. Both terms act through the
null-space projector and preserve the first-order Cartesian objective.}

Relative to the \mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} measured in the inactive mode,
conditioning reduced the net-displacement magnitude by approximately
\mbox{\(99.8\,\%\)} at \mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and
\mbox{\(99.9\,\%\)} at \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}.
