\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results_discussion}

The surface-contact experiments evaluate how the controller parameters
influence the end-effector rotation generated during Contact Establishment. The
primary response quantity is the signed contact-establishment response
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_i}\)} about the investigated surface tangent
\mbox{\(t_i\)}, defined in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}}. It represents the
end-of-contact-establishment orientation change relative to the orientation reference held
through contact establishment. Its sign is chosen so that a response reducing the initial
angular deviation carries the same sign as that deviation, and
\mbox{\(\lvert\gamma_{t_i}\rvert\)} is then the size of the correction.

The pose-based initial angular deviation \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_i}\)} defines
the achieved experimental condition. The commanded tool orientation offset
\mbox{\(\theta_{t_i}\)} is the controller input that generates it, whereas
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_i}\)} is the measured response. The main response figures
therefore identify each condition by \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_i}\)} and report
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_i}\)}. The corresponding numerical values are provided in
\mbox{\Cref{app:additional_plots}}.

\Added{Each contact parameter setting was repeated three times. The comparative
response values in Cases~A--D are the arithmetic means over those repetitions,
and the corresponding sample standard deviations are given in
\mbox{\Cref{app:additional_plots}}. Repetition scatter is summarised once for
each main case below. Within these cases, individual values are discussed only
where their spread is relevant to the interpretation. The Case-D comparison
additionally carries error bars.} The preceding Tool Orientation state terminated with a finite tracking
tolerance, so the initial angular magnitude differed slightly from the
commanded value. At the TCP, the \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} input produced mean
initial deviations of \mbox{\(+9.31^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_1\)} and
\mbox{\(+10.05^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_2\)}. The reversed input produced
\mbox{\(-9.41^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-9.55^\circ\)}, respectively.

\section{Main Surface-Contact Results}
\label{sec:results_main_contact_cases}

The cases are reported in the order in which they constrain the choice of
compliance-centre position. The TCP-centred reference is reported in Case~A,
followed by the stiffness sensitivities in Cases~B and~C. In Case~D the
tangential compliance-centre displacement was tested over both surface tangents
and both signs of the pose-based initial angular deviation. The angular-magnitude,
tool-axis, and intermediate-direction comparisons are
reported as supporting checks in
\mbox{\Cref{app:supporting_contact_checks}}.

\subsection{Baseline Contact Response with the CoC at the TCP: Case~A}
\label{sec:results_case_a}

In Case~A the \abbr{CoC} was placed at the TCP, so
\mbox{\(r_c=0\)}. This condition removes the additional
translation--rotation coupling introduced by the virtual point shift. The
ordinary rotational impedance and physical contact geometry remain. The
measured response therefore provides the baseline for the stiffness and
compliance-centre-position comparisons.
\Added{Repetition scatter remained at or below \mbox{\(0.19^\circ\)} across
all Case-A settings in \mbox{\Cref{tab:results_case_a}}.}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_a_bars.tex}
\caption{Measured contact-establishment rotation with the CoC at the TCP.}
\label{fig:results_case_a}
\end{figure}

With the \abbr{CoC} at the TCP, the measured response differs strongly between
the two surface tangents. For the positive initial-deviation conditions,
\Cref{fig:results_case_a} gives \mbox{\(+7.57^\circ\)} about
\mbox{\(t_1\)} and \mbox{\(-1.63^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_2\)}.
Against these positive initial deviations, the \mbox{\(t_1\)} response was
correction-directed, whereas the \mbox{\(t_2\)} response had the opposite sign.
Reversing the initial angular deviation changes the response to
\mbox{\(-10.83^\circ\)} and \mbox{\(-1.25^\circ\)}, respectively.
The figure shows all five baseline conditions.

The nominal zero-offset input produced a pose-based initial deviation of
\mbox{\(0.74^\circ\)} and gave
\mbox{\(-0.97^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. Contact produced end-effector
rotation from the configured zero-offset target orientation as well. The other
conditions are read against that non-zero response rather than against zero.
The TCP-centred condition provides the reference against which the stiffness
and compliance-centre-position variations are compared.

\subsection{Effect of Cartesian Stiffness: Cases~B and~C}

\subsubsection{Rotational Stiffness: Case~B}
\label{sec:results_case_b}

In Case~B the rotational stiffness about the investigated tangent was varied
while the other controller settings remained unchanged. The
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} condition is the corresponding TCP-centred
reference from Case~A.
\Added{Repetition scatter remained at or below \mbox{\(0.31^\circ\)} across
all Case-B settings in \mbox{\Cref{tab:results_case_b}}.}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_b_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Measured contact-establishment rotation by rotational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_b}
\end{figure}

Increasing \mbox{\(K_{R,t_i}\)} reduced the measured rotation over the tested
range. About \mbox{\(t_1\)}, \Cref{fig:results_case_b} shows a decrease from
\mbox{\(+7.57^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} to
\mbox{\(+2.73^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. About
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, the response remained small and changed from
\mbox{\(-1.63^\circ\)} to \mbox{\(-0.48^\circ\)}. Rotational stiffness
therefore changed the response magnitude without removing the directional
difference observed in Case~A.

\subsubsection{Cross-Axis Translational Stiffness: Case~C}
\label{sec:results_case_c}

In Case~C the translational stiffness perpendicular to the investigated
rotation axis was varied. The rotational stiffness about that axis was held at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. The \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)}
condition is the corresponding TCP-centred reference from Case~A.
\Added{Repetition scatter remained at or below \mbox{\(0.17^\circ\)} across
all Case-C settings in \mbox{\Cref{tab:results_case_c}}.}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_c_stiffness.tex}
\caption{Measured contact-establishment rotation by cross-axis translational stiffness.}
\label{fig:results_case_c}
\end{figure}

The influence of perpendicular translational stiffness was considerably
smaller than that of rotational stiffness. About \mbox{\(t_1\)}, the measured
rotation varied by only \mbox{\(0.03^\circ\)} across the tested range. About
\mbox{\(t_2\)}, \Cref{fig:results_case_c} shows a change of
\mbox{\(0.61^\circ\)}, from \mbox{\(-2.24^\circ\)} to
\mbox{\(-1.63^\circ\)}. Varying this stiffness alone therefore did not account
for the directional difference in the baseline response.

\subsection{Effect of Tangential CoC Position: Case~D}
\label{sec:results_case_d}

In Case~D the tangential position of the \abbr{CoC} was varied while the
Cartesian stiffness and damping remained unchanged. For each rotation axis, the centre was
moved along the surface tangent perpendicular to that axis. The TCP-centred
condition is included as the zero-position reference.
\Added{Repetition scatter was small at most Case-D settings, and
\mbox{\Cref{tab:results_case_d}} gives every sample standard deviation. Four
conditions carry a larger spread, and they are named where the error bars
appear below rather than summarised by a single bound.}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_panels.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment rotation by tangential CoC position (mean \(\pm\) SD).}
\label{fig:results_case_d}
\end{figure}

As shown in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d}}, reversing the initial
orientation condition changes which side of the TCP produces the larger
response. About \mbox{\(t_1\)}, the positive condition gives
\mbox{\(+7.87^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} and
\mbox{\(+0.18^\circ\)} at \mbox{\(-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The reversed
condition exchanges the larger response between the two sides.

Changing the rotation axis also changes the required tangential direction.
For a positive condition about \mbox{\(t_2\)}, the response changes from
\mbox{\(-1.63^\circ\)} at the TCP to \mbox{\(+4.43^\circ\)} at the
selected \mbox{\(r_{c,t_1}=-40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} position. The selected
displacement therefore lies along \mbox{\(+t_2\)} for rotation about
\mbox{\(t_1\)} and along \mbox{\(-t_1\)} for rotation about
\mbox{\(t_2\)}. Among the tested positions, no non-zero tangential
\abbr{CoC} was independent of the tangent direction and sign of the
pose-based initial angular deviation.

\Added{The error bars in \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_case_d}} identify the conditions with
the larger repetition spread. The positive \mbox{\(t_1\)} condition at
\mbox{\(-20\,\mathrm{mm}\)} had a standard deviation of
\mbox{\(0.44^\circ\)}. About \mbox{\(t_2\)}, the positive condition had
standard deviations of \mbox{\(0.47^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(+20\,\mathrm{mm}\)} and \mbox{\(2.36^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The reversed condition at
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} had a standard deviation of
\mbox{\(0.71^\circ\)}.}

The positive \mbox{\(t_1\)} condition was examined in the time domain to
relate the rotation response to the commanded wrench. Its two outer positions
produce a large difference in \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)}, while the
TCP-centred condition provides the zero-position reference. Each trace is one
recorded repetition of its centre position.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{figures/results_case_d_wrench_relabel.tex}
\caption{Contact-establishment response and commanded wrench at three CoC positions.}
\label{fig:results_case_d_wrench}
\end{figure}

For this figure, the commanded Cartesian wrench
\mbox{\(F=[f^\top,m^\top]^\top\)} is resolved as

\[
F_n=n_s^\top f,
\qquad
M_{t_1}=t_1^\top m.
\]

The normal-force component is negative while the tool presses into the
surface. Both quantities are controller commands.

\Cref{fig:results_case_d_wrench} places the contact-establishment rotation
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)}, the commanded normal force
\mbox{\(F_n\)}, and the commanded TCP moment
\mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} on a common time axis for three
centre positions. Every condition shown carries a commanded orientation offset
of \mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} about \mbox{\(t_1\)}, corresponding to a mean
pose-based initial deviation of approximately \mbox{\(+9.32^\circ\)}. At
\mbox{\(-40\)}, \mbox{\(0\)}, and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, the commanded normal force settles in a similar
range of approximately \mbox{\(-77\)} to \mbox{\(-82\,\mathrm{N}\)}, while the
commanded TCP moment about \mbox{\(t_1\)} settles at approximately
\mbox{\(+3.28\)}, \mbox{\(+0.66\)}, and \mbox{\(-2.44\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The
outer positions therefore produce moments of similar magnitude and opposite
sign while their contact-establishment rotations differ by \mbox{\(7.73^\circ\)}. The
difference in contact-establishment rotation is therefore associated primarily with the moment
generated by the displaced impedance reference rather than with a substantial
change in the normal pressing force.

At the selected \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} condition, the negative commanded
moment \mbox{\(M_{t_1}\)} accompanies a positive
\mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)}. This response is formed from the measured end-effector
orientation back to the reference held through contact establishment, as
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}} defines it. Its sign is
therefore opposite to the measured end-effector rotation from the start to the
end of Contact Establishment.

A tangential displacement of the \abbr{CoC} moves the impedance reference
point away from the TCP, so the translational press is coupled to the rotational
part of the impedance. According to
\mbox{\Cref{eq:commanded_moment_decomposition}}, a non-zero tangential
displacement adds the contribution \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)} from the commanded
press. This explains
why the commanded moment changes strongly with centre position while
\mbox{\(F_n\)} remains within a similar range. Reversing the tangential
displacement reverses the contribution \mbox{\(r_c\times f_n\)} generated by
the normal press.

The measured sign reversal follows the relation derived in
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:coc_tangential_displacement}}. A fixed tangential
\mbox{\(r_c\neq0\)} retains one preferred moment direction while the press
continues. At \mbox{\(r_c=0\)}, the point-shift contribution vanishes and the
TCP selects no tangential direction. The TCP-centred condition is therefore
the direction-independent fixed reference among the tested positions. A displaced
centre provides greater condition-specific rotational authority where the
required direction is known.

The Grinding state returns to the decoupled impedance branch described in
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:implementation_grinding_phase}}. This state structure is
consistent with removing the direction-selective coupling after contact establishment. It is
an implementation property rather than an experimental result, because the
reported runs ended in the operator-controlled hold before grinding.

Across Cases~A--D, tangential CoC position produced the largest measured
response variation among the tested controller parameters.

\section{Null-Space Pose-Hold Results}
\label{sec:results_nullspace}

The remaining experiment is separate from the contact cases and analyses the
redundant joint-space direction while the Cartesian pose is held. Matched
trials compare the selectable null-space terms under the same internally
commanded point-force-equivalent disturbance. The commanded disturbance
reached \mbox{\(20.00\,\mathrm{N}\)} in all twelve trials, with peak equivalent
joint-torque norms between \mbox{\(2.17\)} and
\mbox{\(2.45\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The torque-limit scale remained equal to one,
so the waveform was applied without clipping.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{figures/MAIN_NS_nullspace_automatic.pdf}
\caption{Null-space pose-hold response.}
\label{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic} reports the response during the
Cartesian pose-hold disturbance test in three panels. Panel (a) gives the mean
cumulative projected null-space motion \mbox{\(E_N\)} without null-space torque
and with projected damping, the shaded bands showing \mbox{\(\pm\)} one
standard deviation across the three repetitions. Panel (b) gives the change in
the minimum Jacobian singular value over the disturbance interval,
\mbox{\(\Delta\sigma_{\min,\mathrm{dist}}\)}, together with the peak Cartesian
position error, for the two singular-value conditioning magnitudes; its error
bars show \mbox{\(\pm\)} one standard deviation and the horizontal line marks the
\mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} position-retention limit. Panel (c) gives the net
redundant displacement \mbox{\(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\)} of all four
settings, with the mean printed above each bar and error bars showing
\mbox{\(\pm\)} one standard deviation. Selected numerical values are discussed
below.

\subsection{Projected Null-Space Damping}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_damping}

Projected null-space damping reduced \mbox{\(E_N\)} from
\mbox{\(7.596^\circ\)} without null-space torque to \mbox{\(5.687^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)}, a reduction of
approximately \mbox{\(25\,\%\)}; panel~(a) of
\mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}} carries both traces. The net
redundant displacement decreased in
the same proportion, from \mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} to
\mbox{\(0.098\,\mathrm{rad}\)}, while the change in \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)}
across the disturbance interval became less negative, from
\mbox{\(-2.13\times10^{-3}\)} to \mbox{\(-1.26\times10^{-3}\)}. This
behaviour follows from the projected damping law of
\mbox{\Cref{eq:nullspace_damping_torque}}. The torque vanishes as the redundant
motion stops and therefore requests no return towards the initial
configuration.

\subsection{Singular-Value Conditioning}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_sigma}

Singular-value conditioning behaved differently from both the uncontrolled and
the damping-only settings. It was already active during the
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} preceding the disturbance and remained active
throughout it, so it did not act as a term applied after the redundant
configuration had been displaced. Both sigma-only settings ended the
disturbance interval with a net redundant displacement close to zero:
\mbox{\(0.3\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} at
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and
\mbox{\(-0.1\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}\)} at \mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}.
As panel~(c) of \mbox{\Cref{fig:results_nullspace_automatic}} shows, both
values are more than two orders of magnitude smaller than the
\mbox{\(0.131\,\mathrm{rad}\)} obtained without null-space torque. Over the
same interval \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} increased by approximately
\mbox{\(1.95\times10^{-5}\)} and \mbox{\(1.93\times10^{-5}\)}, whereas it
decreased by \mbox{\(2.13\times10^{-3}\)} without null-space torque. Under the
tested disturbance direction, the conditioning term therefore strongly
suppressed the resulting redundant displacement.

The two sigma magnitudes differed mainly in motion activity. At
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, the cumulative projected null-space
motion was \mbox{\(1.619^\circ\)}, compared with \mbox{\(0.283^\circ\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, a factor of about six, while both net
displacements remained near zero. The larger torque magnitude was associated
with greater switching activity near the locally preferred region. This is
consistent with the control law, because the conditioning term commands a fixed
torque magnitude along the selected direction and the selector
\mbox{\(s_\sigma\)} becomes zero only inside the comparison deadband. The
larger cumulative motion therefore indicates greater switching activity around
the same region rather than poorer disturbance rejection. The mean peak
Cartesian position error was \mbox{\(0.889\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at
\mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and \mbox{\(0.983\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at
\mbox{\(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The largest position error measured in the
complete pose-hold study was \mbox{\(1.304\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, below the
predefined \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} acceptance limit. Of the two tested
magnitudes, \mbox{\(1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} achieved comparable suppression of
net disturbance-induced displacement with substantially less redundant motion.

\subsection{Interpretation and Experimental Bounds}
\label{subsec:results_nullspace_bounds}

The pose-hold trials compare the four tested null-space settings under the
repeatable commanded disturbance. The two terms act on different quantities.
Projected damping dissipates redundant joint motion that is already present,
whereas the singular-value term drives the redundant configuration towards the
sampled neighbour with the larger \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} whether or not
such motion is present. The conditioning law is not dissipative: a disturbance
acting towards larger \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} would move the
configuration in the same direction as \mbox{\(\tau_\sigma\)} rather than
against it, so the rejection observed here is specific to the tested
disturbance direction. Both terms act through the null-space projector, so neither
contributes a first-order Cartesian wrench.

Several bounds apply to these results. Because the conditioning torque was
active before the disturbance, the sigma-only trials entered it from a redundant
configuration displaced by approximately
\mbox{\(0.013\,\mathrm{rad}\)} from the one at which the trials without
conditioning began. The four settings therefore compare the closed-loop
behaviour of the complete modes. They do not isolate an instantaneous opposing
torque at identical joint configurations. The surface-contact experiments use
both contributions together in the combined secondary-torque mode and therefore
do not separate their individual effects during contact. The geometric Jacobian
combines translational and rotational rows without a common length scale, so
the reported \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} is used only as a relative indicator
under the fixed Jacobian convention.
