\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\Added{This chapter defines the experimental system, the calibration
procedures, the surface-contact test matrix, and the evaluation quantities. The
surface-contact experiments varied the configured orientation offset, the
directional Cartesian stiffness, and the virtual \mbox{\abbr{CoC}}, while the
remaining experimental conditions were held constant. The primary outcome was
the end-effector rotation generated during Contact Establishment.}

\Added{A separate Cartesian pose-hold experiment evaluated projected null-space
damping and singular-value conditioning under a repeatable internally commanded
disturbance, referred to below as the virtual disturbance.}

\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:experimental_system}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Robot, Tool, and Surface}
\label{subsec:experimental_robot_tool_surface}

The experiments were performed with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika
Panda operated through the Franka Control Interface. Joint-torque commands were
transmitted through libfranka at the nominal control rate of
\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\), corresponding to a sampling period of
\(1\,\mathrm{ms}\). The real-time
controller, Cartesian impedance law, contact geometry, and state sequence are
described in \Cref{ch:implementation}.

The rectangular grinding tool was held by the Franka Hand using custom gripper
fingers. The tool face measured \(40\times120\,\mathrm{mm}\), with the
width along \(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and the length along \(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). Its
centre was located \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the
end-effector origin. The tool was clamped by custom pads screwed to
the gripper fingers.

\Added{The plane plate was mounted on a raised base board, and every
experiment used dry contact. Panel~(a) of
\mbox{\Cref{fig:experimental_setup_photo}} shows the physical arrangement.
Panel~(b) separates the configured surface reference, which is a controller
quantity, from the physical plane of the set-up.
\mbox{\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}} describes how the configured
surface reference and the tool normal were established separately.}

\subsection{Real-Time Computer and Software}
\label{subsec:experimental_software_computer}

The controller computer ran Ubuntu~20.04.3 with the
\texttt{5.9.1-rt20} Linux kernel and the \texttt{PREEMPT\_RT} patch. The
controller was compiled using \texttt{g++~9.3}, C++14, and optimisation level
\texttt{-O2}. Eigen~3.3.7 was used for matrix calculations and
libfranka~0.7.1 for communication with the robot.

Before the measurements, the computer--robot connection was checked with the
\texttt{communication\_test} program supplied with libfranka~0.7.1
\citep{libfrankaCommunicationTest}. The program reports the fraction of
control commands received by the robot, and issues a communication-quality
warning when the mean command-success rate falls below \(0.90\). The
experimental system passed this criterion, which confirms that the
communication quality met the libfranka requirement for operation through the
nominal \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\) interface.


\subsection{Operating and Safety Configuration}
\label{subsec:reproducibility_record}
\label{sec:experimental_monitoring_limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

Before torque control started, the acceleration and nominal joint-torque
collision thresholds were both set to \(140\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) for every
joint. The Cartesian thresholds were set to \(240\,\mathrm{N}\) for the
force channels and \(240\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) for the moment channels. These
values configured the robot-side collision and reflex monitoring and were kept
unchanged throughout the measurements.

The complete experiment-specific controller configuration is given in
\Cref{app:controller_parameters}.

\section{Surface-Reference and Tool-Normal Calibration}
\label{sec:surface_tool_calibration}
\label{sec:contact_geometry}
% ============================================================================

\Added{To establish the configured surface reference and tool normal, two
independent procedures were performed before the contact experiments.
Surface-reference construction determined \mbox{\(p_s\)}, \mbox{\(n_s\)}, and
\mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{surface}}\)} in the robot base frame, while tool-normal
calibration determined \mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\)} in end-effector
coordinates.}

The configured surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)} was obtained from a pose with
the tool face seated on the physical plate. It supplies the calibrated
surface reference used by the controller and the angular evaluation.
Panel~(b) of \mbox{\Cref{fig:experimental_setup_photo}} distinguishes this
reference from the physical plate orientation.

\subsection{Surface-Reference Construction}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

\Added{The complete tool face was seated on the physical plane, and one
end-effector pose was recorded. At that pose the tool face lay against the
plane, with \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} directed into it. The nominal
\mbox{\(-Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} axis transformed into the base frame therefore
defined the outward configured surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)}. The
transformed tool-face-centre offset defined the point \mbox{\(p_s\)} on the
configured reference plane. The surface tangents and \mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{surface}}\)}
were then constructed as in \mbox{\Cref{subsec:surface_task_frame}}. The
resulting configured surface normal corresponded to rotations of
\mbox{\(-1.585^\circ\)} about the base-frame \mbox{\(x\)}-axis and
\mbox{\(0.988^\circ\)} about the base-frame \mbox{\(y\)}-axis.
\mbox{\Cref{app:controller_parameters}} gives the complete numerical values.}

\subsection{Tool Normal Calibration}
\label{subsec:grinding_face_calibration}

\Added{The complete tool face was seated on the plane at three yaw
orientations. For each recorded pose, the physical tool-face normal corresponds
to the same direction in the robot base frame. A unit vector in end-effector
coordinates was therefore fitted so that its base-frame direction varied as
little as possible across the three poses, which yields
\mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\)}. The calibrated tool normal differed from the
nominal \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} direction by approximately
\mbox{\(1.56^\circ\)}. \mbox{\Cref{app:controller_parameters}} gives the
fitting relation and the numerical vector.}

% ============================================================================

controller parameters for configured orientation offsets about \(t_1\).

\section{Measured Angular Offset and Angular Error}
\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}
% ============================================================================

The configured angular offset \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}\)} sets the
pre-contact orientation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
At zero configured offset, the commanded tool face is parallel to the configured
surface reference. The orientation reached can differ from this command.
The experimental comparisons therefore use the measured pose-based angular
offset \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}\)} at contact entry.

The angular error \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{err},t_1}(t)\)} describes the tool-normal
orientation relative to the calibrated surface reference throughout Contact
Establishment. Both angular quantities use the inward configured surface normal
\mbox{\(-n_s\)} as their reference and are resolved about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
The measured angular offset is the value of this error at contact entry.

\Needspace{0.42\textheight}

for a schematic rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. The solid green and dashed blue
arrows show the calibrated tool normals at contact entry and contact end.
They are calculated from measured end-effector orientation.
Both angle arcs run from the red inward calibrated surface normal
\mbox{\(-n_s\)} to the respective tool normal. The common origin compares orientations.
The view places \mbox{\(t_1\)} out of the page, with positive rotation
anticlockwise by the right-hand rule.

\subsection{Measured Pose-Based Angular Offset}
\label{subsec:initial_angular_deviation}

At the start of Contact Establishment,
\mbox{\(t_{\mathrm{start}}\)}, the measured end-effector orientation gives
the calibrated tool-normal direction

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t_{\mathrm{start}})
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}(t_{\mathrm{start}})n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}.
\label{eq:achieved_tool_normal}
\end{equation}

The measured angular offset is represented by the rotation vector
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}\)}.
It describes the shortest rotation from the inward configured surface normal
\mbox{\(-n_s\)} to the entry tool-normal direction.
For \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}\neq\mathbf{0}\)}, this rotation satisfies

\begin{equation}
R\left(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}}{\lVert\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}\rVert},
\lVert\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}\rVert\right)(-n_s)
=n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t_{\mathrm{start}}).
\label{eq:initial_deviation_rotation}
\end{equation}

When the two directions coincide, \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}=\mathbf{0}\)}.
Its component about \mbox{\(t_1\)} is

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}=t_1^\top\theta_{\mathrm{meas}}.
\label{eq:pose_based_initial_deviation}
\end{equation}

The component \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}\)} identifies the angular
condition in every contact comparison, including the nominal zero-offset
condition. The direction \mbox{\(n_s\)} is fixed throughout each trial.

\subsection{Angular Error During Contact}
\label{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}

The angular error was calculated throughout Contact Establishment from the
measured end-effector orientation and the calibrated tool normal.
At time \mbox{\(t\)}, the tool-normal direction in the base frame is

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t)=R_{\mathrm{EE}}(t)n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}.
\label{eq:measured_tool_normal_time}
\end{equation}

The angular-error vector \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t)\)} represents the
shortest rotation from \mbox{\(-n_s\)} to \mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t)\)}.
For \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t)\neq\mathbf{0}\)}, it satisfies

\begin{equation}
R\left(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t)}{\lVert\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t)\rVert},
\lVert\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t)\rVert\right)(-n_s)
=n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t).
\label{eq:angular_error_rotation}
\end{equation}

The vector is zero when the normal directions coincide.
The component about the first surface tangent is

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{err},t_1}(t)=t_1^\top\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t),
\qquad
\theta_{\mathrm{err},t_1}(t_{\mathrm{start}})
=\theta_{\mathrm{meas},t_1}.
\label{eq:surface_resolved_angular_error}
\end{equation}

Positive and negative values follow the right-hand rule about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
A smaller magnitude indicates a smaller angular-error component relative to
the calibrated surface. A change through zero indicates that the component
has crossed the reference orientation. The complete normal-direction error
also contains the component about the other surface tangent.

The controller evaluated the normal-to-normal rotation from the measured
end-effector orientation during each control cycle. Its recorded vector points
from the tool normal towards \mbox{\(-n_s\)}.
The evaluation reverses this vector to obtain
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{err}}(t)\)} with the same direction convention as the
measured angular offset. \Cref{app:recorded_data} identifies the recorded fields.
The comparison values were extracted from the controller's per-trial reports
at the end of Contact Establishment, \mbox{\(t_{\mathrm{end}}\)}.
Those endpoint components were stored to \mbox{\(0.01^\circ\)}.
The arithmetic means and sample standard deviations were calculated from these
trial-level values. The time histories use the recorded samples from the
complete contact interval of each illustrated trial.

The rotational spring continues to use the controller orientation error
\mbox{\(e_R\)} relative to the held entry reference.
At contact entry, \mbox{\(e_R=\mathbf{0}\)} while the surface-relative angular
error can be non-zero.

\section{Cartesian Wrench Evaluation}
\label{sec:wrench_evaluation_method}
% ============================================================================

Two evaluations of the Cartesian wrench were carried out beside the main
experimental matrix. Both are supplementary to Cases~A--D and add no settings
to that matrix. The first compares the commanded wrench with the
model-estimated external wrench during contact establishment. The second
applies a defined translational perturbation and a defined rotational
perturbation, and compares the commanded wrench with the quasi-static
impedance prediction.

Both evaluations use the model-estimated external wrench supplied by libfranka.
It is obtained from the robot's dynamic model rather than from a force/torque
sensor, which is what the \mbox{\(\mathrm{est}\)} index records. The estimator
returns the spatial wrench acting on the stiffness frame \mbox{\(\{K\}\)},
expressed in the base frame, so its base-frame moment carries the lever from
the base origin to that frame. In the experimental configuration
\mbox{\({}^{\mathrm{EE}}T_K=I\)}, so \mbox{\(\{K\}\)} coincides with the TCP
and the moment is expressed about the TCP by subtracting that lever,

\[
m_{\mathrm{est,TCP}}=m_{\mathrm{est}}-p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\times f_{\mathrm{est}}.
\]

The evaluated components are the normal force and the moment about the first
surface tangent,

\[
F_{n,\mathrm{est}}=n_s^\top f_{\mathrm{est}},
\qquad
M_{t_1,\mathrm{est}}=t_1^\top m_{\mathrm{est,TCP}}.
\]

The commanded components \mbox{\(F_n=n_s^\top f\)} and
\mbox{\(M_{t_1}=t_1^\top m\)} are resolved on the same configured surface axes,
so command and estimate are compared at one reference point and in one frame.


\subsection{Commanded--Estimated Wrench Comparison}
\label{subsec:wrench_comparison_method}

The first evaluation used one trial of the TCP-centred positive-offset
condition, taken from the main contact matrix and evaluated without changing
any setting. Both wrenches were resolved over the complete
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} of Contact Establishment. The comparison is read over
the stationary interval from \mbox{\(4\)} to \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)}, in which
the press has settled and the references are constant. Each quantity is
reported as the mean over that interval, and the estimate is compared with the
command as a percentage of the commanded mean.


\subsection{Quasi-Static Plausibility Assessment}
\label{subsec:plausibility_method}

The second evaluation tested the implemented impedance relation directly,
against the stiffness values it was configured with. It ran in
Contact-Impedance Hold with the compliance centre at the TCP, so
\mbox{\(r_c=0\)} and no point-shift coupling acted, with the null-space torque
inactive and the configured damping in use. The evaluated stiffness entries
were \mbox{\(K_{p,n}=1000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} and
\mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}=15\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}.

Two perturbations were applied while the controller held its captured Cartesian
reference. The translational evaluation displaced the tool along the surface
normal and held the displaced position. The rotational evaluation rotated the
tool about \mbox{\(t_1\)} while the normal load remained applied. Both
perturbations were applied manually, so their achieved displacement and
rotation were measured from the recorded pose.

The translational and rotational evaluations are treated separately. For each
evaluation, the stationary condition immediately before the perturbation defines
the reference state. The translational evaluation therefore uses the unloaded
state as its reference, whereas the rotational evaluation uses the loaded state
before rotation. This removes the moment already present under the normal load
from the rotational comparison.

The quasi-static predictions follow from the configured stiffness and the change
in measured pose relative to the corresponding reference state,

\[
F_{n,\mathrm{qs}}=K_{p,n}\,n_s^\top\left(e_p-e_{p,0}\right),
\qquad
M_{t_1,\mathrm{qs}}=K_{R,t_1}\,t_1^\top\left(e_R-e_{R,0}\right),
\]

where \mbox{\(e_{p,0}\)} and \mbox{\(e_{R,0}\)} denote the pose-error values at
the respective reference states. Time is reset to \mbox{\(t=0\)} at the
beginning of each evaluation, and the reported values are obtained from the
stationary part of the corresponding response.

experiment therefore evaluated the terms individually and together under the
same repeatable virtual disturbance.

the stiffness and damping of \Cref{tab:experimental_hold_gains}. The

stated scalar value multiplied by the \(3\times3\) identity matrix. All
twelve trials in this comparison used fixed Cartesian damping.

conditioning alone, and both terms together. The enabled values were
\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\) and
\(k_\sigma=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The probe step, singular-value deadband,
and relative \abbr{SVD} threshold remained at \(0.08\,\mathrm{rad}\),
\(10^{-6}\), and \(10^{-4}\), respectively.
The combined-control trials were acquired in a later session with the same
controller revision and matching saved experimental settings.


\subsection{Commanded Disturbance}

The disturbance was implemented as the joint-torque equivalent of a virtual
point force applied at a selected location on link~3. In the link frame, the
point was

\begin{equation}
r_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.200
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_point}
\end{equation}

At the initial posture \(q_{\mathrm{init}}\), the structural null direction
\(v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) is a joint-motion direction that preserves the
end-effector pose to first order, and the translational point Jacobian
\(J(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) maps it to the corresponding velocity of the
selected link point. The fixed base-frame force direction was chosen along that
velocity,

\begin{equation}
u_f
=
\frac{J(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})}
{\lVert J(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\rVert_2}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}
\end{equation}

\Added{This choice aligns the virtual force with the motion of the selected
link point produced by the initial structural null direction, so the
disturbance excites the redundant configuration while the pose-hold impedance
opposes its Cartesian component.}

The commanded force magnitude was \(F_{\mathrm{dist}}=20\,\mathrm{N}\). The virtual force
and its equivalent joint torque were

\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)=F_{\mathrm{dist}}s_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)u_f,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)
=
J(q(t))^\top f_{\mathrm{dist}}(t).
\label{eq:nullspace_automatic_disturbance}
\end{equation}

Here \(u_f\) is the unit force direction defined in
\Cref{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}, and \(s_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)\in[0,1]\) is a
dimensionless time-scaling function that switches the commanded virtual
disturbance smoothly on and off. It was defined as

\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,
& t<5\,\mathrm{s},\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1}{2}\left[
1-\cos\!\left(\pi\dfrac{t-5\,\mathrm{s}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}\right)
\right],
& 5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<7\,\mathrm{s},\\[3mm]
1,
& 7\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<8\,\mathrm{s},\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1}{2}\left[
1+\cos\!\left(\pi\dfrac{t-8\,\mathrm{s}}{1\,\mathrm{s}}\right)
\right],
& 8\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<9\,\mathrm{s},\\[3mm]
0,
& t\geq9\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_scale}
\end{equation}

The disturbance increased smoothly from zero to the full commanded
magnitude between \(5\) and \(7\,\mathrm{s}\), remained at full magnitude
until \(8\,\mathrm{s}\), and decreased smoothly to zero by
\(9\,\mathrm{s}\). The equivalent disturbance torque was bounded by

\[
\lVert\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\rVert_2\leq3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\]

\Added{The controller generates a repeatable joint-torque excitation by mapping
the commanded point force through \mbox{\(J^\top\)}. Its Cartesian component
is opposed by the pose-hold impedance, and
\mbox{\Cref{eq:nullspace_cartesian_retention_criterion}} evaluates the resulting
position retention.}

In every trial the selected null-space law was active from the start of the
pose-hold state. The first \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) formed a
pre-disturbance settling interval, during which the conditioning torque of the
singular-value-conditioning settings could already move the redundant configuration. The
disturbance then acted from \(5\) to \(9\,\mathrm{s}\), followed by an
observation interval until the end of the trial. Each selected secondary term
remained active from state entry through the complete trial. The
singular-value-conditioning torque acted before, during, and after the
disturbance interval.


\subsection{Evaluation Quantities}

All quantities in this subsection are evaluated over the interval in which
the virtual disturbance acts, \(t\in[5,9]\,\mathrm{s}\). For the time-history
plots, elapsed time from disturbance onset is \(t_d=t-5\,\mathrm{s}\),
so the evaluated interval is displayed from \(0\) to \(4\,\mathrm{s}\).

The null-space
controller is intended to modify the redundant joint configuration while
preserving the Cartesian pose. The Cartesian position error evaluates
TCP position retention during the disturbance. A
deviation below \(2\,\mathrm{mm}\) is treated as acceptable position
retention, so a trial satisfies the criterion when

\begin{equation}
\max_{5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t\leq9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert e_p(t)\rVert_2
<2\,\mathrm{mm},
\label{eq:nullspace_cartesian_retention_criterion}
\end{equation}

where \(e_p(t)\) is the Cartesian position-error vector.

The redundant joint motion is evaluated from the projected joint velocity
defined in \Cref{eq:nullspace_velocity_projector}. The vector
\(\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}\in\mathbb{R}^7\) has units of
\(\mathrm{rad/s}\) and represents the component of the measured joint
velocity that lies in the instantaneous kinematic null space.

The cumulative joint motion is

\begin{equation}
E_N
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}(t)\rVert_2\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_excursion_metric}
\end{equation}

Taking the norm before integration makes motion in opposite directions
accumulate. The cumulative projected joint motion \(E_N\) evaluates motion
across all seven joints and is particularly relevant to the damping
comparison. Its unit is radians, and the values are expressed in degrees. The
cumulative time history evaluates the same integral from disturbance onset to
each displayed instant.

The conditioning indicator \(\sigma_{\min}(t)\) was evaluated throughout
the disturbance interval. Its time history shows how the local Jacobian
conditioning changes while the disturbance acts. Each trial was also
characterised by its lowest value over the interval,
\[
\min_{5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t\leq9\,\mathrm{s}}\sigma_{\min}(t).
\]
The minimum is calculated for each trial before the three-trial mean and
sample standard deviation are formed.

The measured joint angle \(q_1\) was also evaluated to compare the
joint-angle histories. Joint 1 received the largest peak absolute component of
the equivalent disturbance torque \(\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\) in each of the
twelve selected trials. Its angle was therefore used to illustrate the
temporal response, while \(E_N\) provides the overall motion measure across all
seven joints. The angle was sampled at a nominal \(20\,\mathrm{Hz}\), using
the measured timestamps. The joint-angle change \(\Delta q_1\) was calculated
relative to disturbance onset:
\[
\Delta q_1(t_d)=q_1(t_d+5\,\mathrm{s})-q_1(5\,\mathrm{s}).
\]
The values are expressed in degrees. Each plotted curve is the mean of
three trials at common measured timestamps. Shaded bands show one sample
standard deviation. No interpolation or smoothing was applied.
Subtracting each trial's onset angle preserves positive and negative motion
in \(\Delta q_1\). A joint can move away from its onset angle and later return
close to it while \(E_N\) remains positive. A near-zero final \(\Delta q_1\)
establishes only that joint 1 returned near its onset angle; it does not
establish that every joint returned or that little cumulative motion occurred.
Both panels cover the full disturbance interval.
