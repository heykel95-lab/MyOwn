\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}
\Revised{This chapter presents the experimental configuration, calibration procedure,
controller parameters, test sequence, and evaluation quantities used to
characterise the contact-induced alignment of the grinding tool. The main test
series consists of surface-contact measurements in which the commanded tool orientation offset,
directional Cartesian stiffness, and virtual centre of compliance \mbox{\(p_c\)} were varied
under otherwise matched conditions. Complementary Cartesian pose-hold trials
quantified the two null-space torque \mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}\)} contributions under a repeatable
internally commanded disturbance.}


\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental_design}
\label{sec:experimental_question}

\Revised{The surface-contact investigation follows one comparison chain.
Case~D establishes the zero-lever baseline. Cases~E, G, F, and~H then vary the
lever direction and sign, axial position, definition frame, and commanded
offset magnitude, respectively. Case~C tests the resulting direction-selection
rule at four commanded rotation directions. Cases~B and~A finally vary the
cross-axis translational stiffness and command-axis rotational stiffness.
\mbox{\Cref{tab:experimental_question_map}} summarises this structure before
the complete parameter matrix.}

\Revised{Case~D places the centre on the TCP, so the press makes no moment
through an offset and what acts is the contact alone. It is measured on both
surface tangents and at both signs of the commanded offset, because the
grinding face is three times longer across one tangent than the other and the
two need not behave alike.}

\Revised{Case~E displaces the centre along the tangent perpendicular to the
commanded rotation axis, seven positions from \mbox{\(-40\)} to
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}, at every combination of tangent and sign. That
grid is what settles whether the assisting direction follows the sign of the
commanded offset. Case~G moves the
centre along the tool axis, the direction a first-order coupling model leaves
without effect. Case~F then names the same lever in surface coordinates instead
of end-effector coordinates, so the definition frame is separated from the
offset itself. Case~H halves the commanded offset at two of the Case-E
positions. Case~C subsequently commands four rotation directions in the
tangent plane and compares a direction-selected lever with a fixed lever.}

\Revised{The two stiffness cases retain the zero-lever baseline. Case~B varies
the translational stiffness along the tangent perpendicular to the commanded
rotation axis. Case~A then varies the rotational stiffness about the commanded
rotation axis.}

\Revised{The commanded angular offset defines the orientation target before
set-up; the measured initial EE-inferred angle is retained separately because
finite tracking error remains. The rotational stiffness determines the resistance to
rotation about the tilted tangent, whereas the translational stiffness
perpendicular to that tangent affects the translational response coupled to the
alignment motion. Shifting the Cartesian impedance from a selected
centre of compliance \mbox{\(p_c\)} to the TCP introduces translation--rotation coupling and
therefore changes the moment produced by the commanded press.}

\Revised{The surface plane and the grinding-face direction were calibrated
independently and used to define the surface and tool geometry throughout the
measurements. The rotational stiffness about \mbox{\(t_1\)} and \mbox{\(t_2\)} was varied
separately, while the orthogonal rotational-stiffness entry remained at
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}. This arrangement allowed the response associated with each
surface tangent \mbox{\(t_1\)} or \mbox{\(t_2\)} to be evaluated independently.}

\Revised{Unless stated otherwise for a particular case, the reported set-up
phase used the surface-frame matrices \mbox{\(K_{p,\mathrm{set}}\)} and
\mbox{\(K_{R,\mathrm{set}}\)} defined in
\mbox{\cref{eq:experimental_setup_translational_stiffness,eq:experimental_setup_rotational_stiffness}}.
Both matrices use the coordinate order \mbox{\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\)}. The fixed
phase, damping, null-space, and trajectory parameters are specified in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_controller_parameters}}.}

\Revised{Neither matrix is a neutral starting point. The entries were chosen
for the roles they play during set-up, and the choice is stated here because it
bounds what varying them could show. The controller regulates pose and not
force: the commanded wrench is \mbox{\(K\,\Delta x+D\,\Delta\dot x\)}
throughout, with no force target and no force feedback anywhere in the loop.
Whatever load the tool carries is a consequence of a commanded displacement
acting through a stiffness.}

\Revised{The normal translational entry is the compliant one,
\mbox{\(350\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} against \mbox{\(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} in the
plane. The press reference is ramped past the surface and the surface stops the
tool, so the load that arises is that entry times the penetration the contact
refuses. A stiff normal entry would turn a small geometric error into a large
load, which is what makes the compliant choice the workable one. The stiff
in-plane entries hold the contact point where the geometry places it instead of
letting it slide.}

\Revised{The rotational entries are chosen the other way round. Those about the
two surface tangents are nearly free at \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)},
because turning onto the surface is the motion the set-up phase exists to
permit and a stiff entry there would hold the tool at the attitude it arrived
with. The entry about the surface normal is stiff at
\mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}, because rotation about the tool axis
contributes nothing to alignment.}

\Revised{Two consequences follow for the experiments. The stiffness variations
of Cases~A and~B ask how much the chosen values matter rather than search for
better ones, and their outcome supports the choice: raising the rotational
entry about the commanded tangent reduced the signed set-up rotation. The entry
about the surface normal was not varied, because the alignment error carries no
component about that axis by construction and the measured rotation about it
stayed below \mbox{\(0.71^\circ\)} in every reported run, leaving that entry
little to act on.}

\Revised{The normal translational entry was not varied either. Raising it would
raise the press and, through the compliance-centre offset, the moment the press
makes. Whether that improves the alignment is not established here, and the
reported measurements give reason for doubt: across Cases~D to~H the
model-estimated normal load lay between \mbox{\(76\)} and
\mbox{\(85\,\mathrm{N}\)}, while the signed set-up rotation varied from
\mbox{\(-11.30\)} to \mbox{\(+7.87^\circ\)}. Normal load alone therefore does
not explain the response over this range, and a larger value cannot be assumed
to help.}

\Revised{The archive contains \mbox{\(171\)} surface-contact run directories
distributed across \mbox{\(57\)} parameter settings. One directory,
\mbox{\texttt{P2\_t1\_pos\_m040/r02}}, contains neither a CSV log nor a set-up
report. The quantitative evaluation therefore uses \mbox{\(170\)} contact runs;
that setting has two repetitions, while every other setting has three. Within
each controlled comparison, only the stated parameter was changed. The
remaining gain entries, calibrated geometry, phase sequence, and evaluation
procedure were held constant.}
\Revised{The principal controller-response quantity was the signed set-up
rotation \mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set},t_i}\)} about the commanded surface
tangent. TCP height above the calibrated plane provided the separate
geometry-based flatness classification. The EE-inferred alignment angle,
contact-point and TCP displacements, commanded wrench, and model-estimated
normal load were retained as supporting quantities. Their definitions and
treatment across repeated runs are presented in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}}.}
\Revised{The surface-contact measurements and Cartesian pose-hold trials formed
complementary data sets. Contact trials measured the set-up response with projected
damping and singular-value conditioning as the active null-space terms. The pose-hold trials in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:nullspace_hold_study}} quantified projected damping and
singular-value conditioning under a repeatable internally commanded disturbance.}

\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:experimental_system}


\subsection{Robot, Tool, and Surface}
\label{subsec:experimental_robot_tool_surface}

\Revised{The experiments were performed with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika
Panda operated through the Franka Control Interface. Joint-torque commands were
transmitted through libfranka at the nominal sampling period \mbox{\(T_s\)}:}

\begin{equation}
T_s=1\,\mathrm{ms},
\label{eq:sampling_time_contact}
\end{equation}

\Revised{corresponding to a nominal control rate of \mbox{\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\)}. The real-time
controller and its phase sequence are described in
\mbox{\Cref{ch:implementation}}.}

\Revised{The rectangular grinding tool was held by the Franka Hand using custom gripper
fingers. Its grinding face measured \mbox{\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} along
\mbox{\(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} and \mbox{\(120\,\mathrm{mm}\)} along \mbox{\(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}. The
centre of the grinding face was located \mbox{\(20\,\mathrm{mm}\)} along
\mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} from the reported end-effector origin. The complete
calibrated tool geometry is presented in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}}.}

\Revised{The pads holding the tool were screwed to the gripper fingers. Clearance in
that screwed joint permitted a small relative rotation of the tool about
\mbox{\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}.}

\Revised{The grinding-face direction was reconstructed from the measured end-effector
orientation and its calibrated direction in the end-effector frame. This
frame-consistent metric quantifies the combined robot--gripper--tool response.
The approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} relative tool rotation about
\mbox{\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} defines the mount-related contribution to its uncertainty.}

\Revised{The surface plate was mounted on a raised base board. All experiments used dry
contact between the grinding face and the plate. The controller command followed
the calibrated geometry and Cartesian impedance law. The surface plane and
grinding-face direction were calibrated separately before the experimental
campaign, as described in \mbox{\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}}.}

\Revised{During approach, the controller determines which point or edge of the
rectangular grinding face lies furthest in the descent direction. This point
or edge is used for the geometric clearance calculation and the subsequent
set-up motion. Its position was calculated from the measured end-effector pose
and the calibrated tool geometry.}

\subsection{Software and Real-Time Computer}
\label{subsec:experimental_software_computer}

\Revised{The controller computer ran Ubuntu~20.04.3 with the
\mbox{\texttt{5.9.1-rt20}} Linux kernel and the \mbox{\texttt{PREEMPT\_RT}} patch. The
controller was compiled using \mbox{\texttt{g++~9.3}}, the C++14 language standard,
and the \mbox{\texttt{-O2}} optimisation level. Eigen~3.3.7 and libfranka~0.7.1 were
used for the matrix calculations and communication with the robot.}

\Revised{Before the contact measurements, the real-time computer and robot network
were checked with the \mbox{\texttt{communication\_test}} program supplied with
libfranka~0.7.1 \mbox{\citep{libfrankaCommunicationTest}}. The program reports the
fraction of control commands received by the robot and issues its lower
communication-quality warning below an average success rate of \mbox{\(0.90\)}.
The experimental setup passed this criterion.}

\Revised{The controller operated through the libfranka joint-torque interface. At each
control cycle, the measured robot state and the quantities provided by the
robot model were used to calculate the commanded joint torque
\mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{cmd}}\)}. The
implementation of the real-time callback is documented in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:real_time_control_loop}}.}

\Revised{Each contact run followed the implemented sequence of tool orientation,
surface approach, set-up, and grinding. Manual guidance supported preparation,
while Cartesian pose hold provided the complementary null-space trials.
Projected damping supplied the null-space contribution throughout the contact
sequence.}


\subsection{Recorded Experimental Quantities}
\label{subsec:experimental_recorded_quantities}

\Revised{The quantities required for the subsequent evaluation were written to the
controller's in-memory logging buffer during each run and exported to a CSV
file after torque control had ended. The recorded quantities included:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \Revised{the measured end-effector position and orientation;}
    \item \Revised{the measured joint position \mbox{\(q\)} and velocity
          \mbox{\(\dot q\)};}
    \item \Revised{the commanded Cartesian reference and Cartesian error \mbox{\(e\)};}
    \item \Revised{the position of the point or edge of the grinding face used for the
          set-up motion;}
    \item \Revised{the active controller phase and null-space mode;}
    \item \Revised{the commanded Cartesian wrench \mbox{\(F\)} and final joint-torque
          command \mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{cmd}}\)}; and}
    \item \Revised{the model-estimated external force \mbox{\(\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}\)} and
          moment reported by libfranka.}
\end{itemize}

\Revised{The commanded wrench \mbox{\(F\)} and the model-estimated external wrench were recorded as
separate quantities. After the clearance-transition reference was subtracted,
the libfranka estimate provided a common supporting load signal across matched runs. The complete
logging implementation and CSV structure are described in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:recorded_signals}}.}

\section{Surface and Tool Calibration}
\label{sec:surface_tool_calibration}
\label{sec:contact_geometry}

\Revised{The surface plane and the direction normal to the grinding face were
calibrated independently before the contact experiments. The surface
calibration provided the point, normal direction, and tangent directions used
by the controller. The tool calibration provided the grinding-face direction
expressed in the end-effector frame.}

\Revised{The calibrated quantities were written to the controller parameter files and
were subsequently used for approach, clearance calculation, commanded tool
orientation, gain orientation, compliance-centre placement, and evaluation of
the experimental results.}

\Revised{The two calibration chains were independent during estimation and were
combined only when the run-time tool direction was evaluated against the
surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)}. Their fit and validation stages are summarised in
\mbox{\cref{fig:calibration_flow}}.}

\subsection{Surface-Plane Calibration}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

\Revised{Four positions distributed over the plate were probed with the same
marked \mbox{\(+X_{\mathrm{EE}},+Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} corner of the grinding face.
The first three corner positions, \mbox{\(P_1\)}--\mbox{\(P_3\)}, determined the
plane. The fourth, \mbox{\(P_4\)}, was excluded from the fit and provided an
independent geometric check.}

\Revised{At each position \mbox{\(i\)}, the read-only capture utility obtained the
measured end-effector position \mbox{\(p_{\mathrm{EE},i}\)} and orientation
\mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{EE},i}\)}. The fixed end-effector-frame probe offset
\mbox{\(r_{\mathrm{probe,EE}}\)} was the grinding-face centre plus the positive
half-width and half-length vectors:}

\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{probe,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.020\\
0.060\\
0.020
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:plane_probe_corner_offset}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The base-frame corner coordinate \mbox{\(p_{\mathrm{cal},i}\)} stored for the plane fit was
therefore}

\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{cal},i}
=
p_{\mathrm{EE},i}
+R_{\mathrm{EE},i}r_{\mathrm{probe,EE}}.
\label{eq:plane_probe_corner_position}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The plane used by the controller is represented by}

\begin{equation}
n_s^\top(p-p_s)=0,
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_plane}
\end{equation}

\Revised{where \mbox{\(p_s\)} is one point on the plane and \mbox{\(n_s\)} is its unit normal,
expressed in the robot base frame.}

\Revised{The final calibrated plane point stored in
the controller parameters was}

\begin{equation}
p_s
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.5153\\
-0.1072\\
0.0031
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_point}
\end{equation}

\Revised{This point is the centroid of the three fitted corner coordinates, not a
captured grinding-face-centre position.}

\Revised{The controller stores the surface orientation through rotations
\mbox{\(\alpha_s\)} about the base \mbox{\(x\)}-axis and
\mbox{\(\beta_s\)} about the base \mbox{\(y\)}-axis. With
\mbox{\(e_z\)} denoting the base-frame \mbox{\(z\)}-axis unit vector, the corresponding
normal is calculated from}

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
R_y(\beta_s)R_x(\alpha_s)e_z
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin(\beta_s)\cos(\alpha_s)\\
-\sin(\alpha_s)\\
\cos(\beta_s)\cos(\alpha_s)
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:implemented_surface_normal_angles}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The calibrated angles in the final controller configuration were}

\begin{equation}
\alpha_s=-1.585^\circ,
\qquad
\beta_s=+0.988^\circ.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tilt_angles}
\end{equation}

\Revised{These values give the base-frame surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)}}

\begin{equation}
n_s
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
0.017245\\
0.027663\\
0.999469
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_s\|=1.
\label{eq:surface_normal}
\end{equation}

\Revised{This three-point physical-plane fit was copied into the controller
configuration. The same normal \mbox{\(n_s\)} therefore defined the surface
frame, the controller geometry, and the alignment values reported in
\mbox{\Cref{ch:results_discussion}}. No separate evaluation normal was used.}

\Revised{The plane-validation residual \mbox{\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}\)} is the
perpendicular distance of the independently probed fourth point
\mbox{\(p_{\mathrm{cal},4}\)}
from the calibrated plane:}

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
\left|
n_s^\top(p_{\mathrm{cal},4}-p_s)
\right|.
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_residual}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The resulting validation residual was}

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
0.82\,\mathrm{mm}.
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_value}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The plane calibration determines the surface point and normal. Its
in-plane orientation is fixed separately by the configured reference direction
\mbox{\(a_s=e_x\)}, which is projected into the calibrated plane and normalised:}

\begin{equation}
t_1
=
\frac{
    (I-n_sn_s^\top)a_s
}{
    \left\|
        (I-n_sn_s^\top)a_s
    \right\|
}.

\subsection{Grinding-Face Calibration}
\label{subsec:grinding_face_calibration}

\Revised{Four end-effector orientations,
\mbox{\(T_{\mathrm{cal},1}\)}--\mbox{\(T_{\mathrm{cal},4}\)}, were captured
with the complete grinding face seated flat on the validated plane. The face
remained flat while its yaw about the plane normal was changed by approximately
\mbox{\(20^\circ\)}--\mbox{\(45^\circ\)} between samples. The first three rotations
were used for estimation, and the fourth was held out for validation.}

\Revised{With only the yaw changed, the end-effector-frame grinding-face normal
\mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} should map to one invariant base-frame direction
\mbox{\(\bar n_B\)}. The matrices \mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}\)} are the
rotational blocks of the captured poses \mbox{\(T_{\mathrm{cal},i}\)}:}

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{cal},1}n_{\mathrm{EE}}
\approx
R_{\mathrm{cal},2}n_{\mathrm{EE}}
\approx
R_{\mathrm{cal},3}n_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
\bar n_B.
\label{eq:tool_axis_invariant_direction}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The estimator formed the mean rotation matrix
\mbox{\(\bar R=(R_{\mathrm{cal},1}+R_{\mathrm{cal},2}+R_{\mathrm{cal},3})/3\)}
and applied the
singular-value decomposition \mbox{\(\bar R=U\Sigma V^\top\)}. The matrix
\mbox{\(U\)} contains the left singular vectors. The matrix \mbox{\(\Sigma\)}
contains the singular values, and \mbox{\(V\)} contains the right singular
vectors. The dominant
right singular vector gave \mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}, while the corresponding
left singular vector gave \mbox{\(\bar n_B\)}. The sign was selected toward
nominal \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}. The final controller configuration was}

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.026387057\\
-0.006678514\\
0.999629492
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_{\mathrm{EE}}\|=1,
\label{eq:calibrated_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

\Revised{expressed in the end-effector frame. The calibrated grinding-face normal
differs from nominal \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} by approximately
\mbox{\(1.56^\circ\)}. The calibration does not by itself identify the physical
source of this offset. The effective value above was stored with every
reported run and is therefore the value used in the evaluation.}

\Revised{During each controller cycle, the grinding-face direction in the robot base
frame is calculated from the measured end-effector orientation:}

\begin{equation}
n_T
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}n_{\mathrm{EE}}.
\label{eq:grinding_face_direction_base}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The direction \mbox{\(n_T\)} is therefore calculated from the measured end-effector
orientation and the calibrated grinding-face direction. This reconstruction
provides the grinding-face direction used throughout the evaluation.}

\Revised{The commanded flat orientation turns the grinding face toward the calibrated
surface according to}

\begin{equation}
R_d n_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
-n_s.
\label{eq:implemented_tool_axis_target}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The negative sign follows from the axis-direction sign \mbox{\(s_a\)}:}

\begin{equation}
s_a=-1,
\end{equation}

\Revised{which directs the positive grinding-face axis opposite the outward surface
normal \mbox{\(n_s\)}. The commanded spin about the desired grinding-face axis,
measured from \mbox{\(t_1\)}, was \mbox{\(90^\circ\)} for all reported runs. For
the flat orientation, the grinding-face axis is antiparallel to
\mbox{\(n_s\)}, and the projected long face axis is consequently aligned with
the \mbox{\(t_2\)} line.}

\Revised{The parallel-jaw mounting permitted approximately
\mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of relative tool rotation about \mbox{\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}.
This relative motion contributes to the uncertainty of the alignment
calculated from the end-effector orientation.}

mounting axes in the surface plane.}

\Revised{The grinding-face contact-point selection tolerance

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\Revised{Corners whose heights differ by no more than \mbox{\(\varepsilon_g\)}

\Revised{In the following sections, this geometry-based reference is called the point
or edge of the grinding face used for the set-up motion. Its position is used

\Revised{The point or edge is obtained entirely from the measured end-effector pose,
the calibrated grinding-face geometry, and the descent direction. The
controller therefore uses a geometric surface reference rather than a
separately measured physical contact position. The implementation of the
corner transformation and geometric selection is described in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:surface_task_plane}}.}

against a commanded penetration, so a condition in which the tool rotates far
presents a different contact geometry and settles a few newtons lower. The
alignment comparisons therefore rest on a common commanded press rather than
on an identical measured one.}

\Revised{The alignment criterion is evaluated while the phase runs but never
ends it. A deviation below \mbox{\(2^\circ\)} held for \mbox{\(0.3\,\mathrm{s}\)}
marks the run as aligned and records the time; the crossing of half the
deviation present at set-up entry is recorded separately, together with the
smallest deviation reached and when. The last two are defined for every run,
including those that never align, so a condition that fails is kept with its
own numbers rather than left empty.}

\Revised{The individual variations are listed in
\mbox{\cref{tab:experimental_test_cases}}. Case~E sampled seven tangential
positions from \mbox{\(-40\)} to \mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. Case~G sampled
six non-zero tool-axis positions over the same range and used the Case-E
zero-position run as its reference. Case~H compared \mbox{\(0\)} and
\mbox{\(+40\,\mathrm{mm}\)} at \mbox{\(+5^\circ\)} with the corresponding
\mbox{\(+10^\circ\)} Case-E settings.}


\subsection{Null-Space Settings for the Contact Measurements}
\label{subsec:nullspace_parameter_rationale}

\Revised{Projected damping and singular-value conditioning formed the active
null-space configuration throughout the surface-contact measurements. This
held the redundant-motion policy constant across Cases~A to~H. The gains were}

\begin{equation}
d_{\mathrm{null}}
=
2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad},
\qquad
k_\sigma
=
1\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\label{eq:experimental_contact_nullspace_gains}
\end{equation}

\Revised{Mode~3 adds the projected damping torque to the singular-value
conditioning torque. Their definitions are given in
\mbox{\Cref{sec:nullspace_svd}}. The contact measurements therefore establish
alignment performance with both terms active; they do not isolate their
individual effects. Matched pose-hold trials provide that separate comparison
under a repeatable disturbance.}

\Revised{The recorded parameters confirmed one phase configuration throughout
all reported contact runs: surface-frame translation,
\mbox{\(K_{p,n}=350\,\mathrm{N/m}\)} toward \mbox{\(+0.240\,\mathrm{m}\)},
a \mbox{\(5.0\,\mathrm{s}\)} set-up timeout, a moment threshold the contact
never reached, a disabled pre-set-up gate, and null-space mode~3. The detailed
values remain collected in
\mbox{\cref{tab:experimental_phase_parameters,tab:active_phase_gains,tab:nullspace_safety_parameters}}.}
\section{Contact Experiment Procedure}
\label{sec:contact_experimental_procedure}
\label{sec:experimental_procedure_contact}

\Revised{Before each run, the active parameter set was recorded and the robot was moved
to the configured initial joint posture. Damped manual guidance could be used
to adjust this posture, after which the selected start pose was captured. The
tool was held by the Franka Hand using the configured grasp width of
\mbox{\(66\,\mathrm{mm}\)} and a grasping force of \mbox{\(60\,\mathrm{N}\)}. The seating
was repeated in the same manner for every measurement, while the approximately
\mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} rotational play about \mbox{\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} remained part of
the physical mounting condition.}

\Revised{The calibrated surface frame and grinding-face geometry were constructed
before torque control began. The active Cartesian gains, damping settings,
null-space parameters, and compliance-centre definition were then loaded, and
the robot-side collision behaviour was configured.}

\Revised{During tool orientation, the TCP position was held while the calibrated
grinding-face direction was rotated toward \mbox{\(-n_s\)}, including the commanded
angular offsets of the active case. The controller remained in this phase for
at least \mbox{\(1.0\,\mathrm{s}\)} and continued to surface approach when the
remaining tool-axis and normal-axis spin errors were at most
\mbox{\(0.016\,\mathrm{rad}\)} and \mbox{\(0.009\,\mathrm{rad}\)}, respectively.
The phase was additionally bounded by an \mbox{\(8.0\,\mathrm{s}\)} timeout and a
\mbox{\(20^\circ\!/\mathrm{s}\)} orientation-reference rate.}

\Revised{Surface approach moved the Cartesian reference along the descent direction at
\mbox{\(0.1\,\mathrm{m/s}\)}, with a maximum commanded travel of
\mbox{\(0.640\,\mathrm{m}\)}. During this motion, the controller continuously
calculated the point or edge of the rectangular grinding face that approached
the surface first. The geometric transition was reached when the leading
corner height was at most \mbox{\(20\,\mathrm{mm}\)} above the calibrated plane.
Corner heights agreeing within \mbox{\(0.1\,\mathrm{mm}\)} were averaged according
to the geometric selection described in
\mbox{\Cref{subsec:calibrated_contact_geometry}}.}

\Revised{At the clearance transition, the controller stored the TCP pose, the
orientation reference, the point or edge of the grinding face used for the
set-up motion, its projection onto the plane, and the model-estimated external
force \mbox{\(\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}\)} and moment as reference values. The
disabled pre-set-up gate allowed the set-up phase to begin directly. This transition was defined by the
calibrated geometry; the model-estimated force \mbox{\(\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}\)} was recorded for evaluation but
did not determine the transition.}

\Revised{During set-up, the signed press coordinate moved from its captured
value, approximately \mbox{\(-0.020\,\mathrm{m}\)}, toward
\mbox{\(+0.240\,\mathrm{m}\)} at \mbox{\(0.080\,\mathrm{m/s}\)} along
the descent direction. Cases~A, B and~D
used the decoupled impedance, whereas Cases~C and~E to~H applied the
point-shifted
\mbox{\(6\times6\)} stiffness and damping matrices associated with the selected
centre of compliance \mbox{\(p_c\)}. The orientation stored at the clearance transition
remained the rotational reference, so the alignment motion arose from the
contact response rather than from a time-varying tipping command.}

\Revised{After the minimum set-up time of \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{s}\)}, the phase could end when
the norm of the model-estimated external-moment change from the clearance transition reached
\mbox{\(150\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The independent \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} timeout provided
the second termination condition. The final signed coordinate is a virtual
Cartesian spring reference and therefore describes the controller command
rather than a measured penetration depth.}

\Revised{Every reported experimental set-up phase ended at the
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} timeout; the \mbox{\(150\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} moment-change threshold
was not reached. In this campaign, the threshold therefore functioned as an
exceptional alternative bound rather than the routine set-up termination
criterion.}

\Revised{The enabled pre-grind gate then held the reached pose while the coupled
set-up impedance remained active. The reported runs were ended at this gate;
operator confirmation of the grinding transition was not given. Consequently,
the archived contact data contain the orientation, approach, and set-up phases,
but no grinding phase or tangential sweep.}

\Revised{The operator ended the run through the terminal interface. The buffered data
were then written to the run-specific CSV file, and the stored parameter set
was included with the measurement. Between the three repetitions of one
setting, the calibrated geometry, fixed gain entries, phase timing, and
procedure remained unchanged. Only the parameter assigned to the corresponding
case was modified.}


\section{Evaluation Metrics and Repeated-Run Treatment}
\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}

\subsection{EE-Inferred Alignment Diagnostic}
\label{subsec:experimental_alignment_change}

\Revised{The grinding-face direction used in the evaluation was calculated from the
measured end-effector orientation and the calibrated direction expressed in
the end-effector frame:}

\begin{equation}
n_T
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}n_{\mathrm{EE}}.
\label{eq:evaluation_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The alignment angle \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\)} measures the angular
difference between this direction and the calibrated surface:}

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(-n_T^\top n_s\right).
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The sign convention follows the orientation target
\mbox{\(R_d n_{\mathrm{EE}}=-n_s\)}. The angle is calculated from the measured
end-effector orientation and the calibrated tool geometry. Relative rotation
of the tool in the gripper therefore contributes to its uncertainty.}

\Revised{The alignment improvement \mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\)} is the
initial alignment angle \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\)} minus the final alignment
angle \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\)}:}

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\theta_{\mathrm{align,before}}
-
\theta_{\mathrm{align,after}},
\label{eq:alignment_error_improvement}
\end{equation}

\Revised{where the initial value is taken at the clearance transition and the final
value at the end of set-up. Positive values represent a reduction of the
angular difference between the grinding face and the calibrated surface.}


\subsection{Primary Set-Up Rotation, Displacement, and Load}
\label{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}

\Revised{The finite set-up rotation vector \mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}\)} was
evaluated from}

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}
=
\phi_{\mathrm{set}}u_{\mathrm{set}}.
\label{eq:contact_induced_rotation}
\end{equation}

\Revised{Here \mbox{\(\phi_{\mathrm{set}}\)} and \mbox{\(u_{\mathrm{set}}\)} are the rotation angle
and the unit rotation axis of the relative rotation
\mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{after}}R_{\mathrm{before}}^\top\)}, obtained from its trace and its
skew-symmetric part exactly as in \mbox{\Cref{eq:angle_from_trace}} and
\mbox{\Cref{eq:rotation_axis_from_matrix}}. The vector is expressed in the robot base
frame. Its magnitude is the angle turned during set-up, in radians, and its
direction is the axis about which that rotation occurred.}

\Revised{The surface-resolved set-up rotation
\mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}^{S}\)} was obtained from}

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}^{S}
=
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}.
\label{eq:surface_resolved_setup_rotation}
\end{equation}

\Revised{This representation separates the rotations about \mbox{\(t_1\)}, \mbox{\(t_2\)}, and
\mbox{\(n_s\)} and permits direct comparison with the commanded rotation direction.}

\Revised{Let \mbox{\(p_g\)} denote the position of the point or edge of the grinding face used
for the set-up motion. Its displacement vector \mbox{\(\Delta p_g\)} and
displacement magnitude \mbox{\(s_g\)} were}

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_g
=
p_{g,\mathrm{after}}-p_{g,\mathrm{before}},
\qquad
s_g
=
\lVert\Delta p_g\rVert.
\label{eq:contact_feature_displacement}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The TCP displacement vector \mbox{\(\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\)} and its
magnitude \mbox{\(s_{\mathrm{TCP}}\)} were evaluated in the same form:}

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}
=
p_{\mathrm{TCP,after}}-p_{\mathrm{TCP,before}},
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{TCP}}
=
\lVert\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\rVert.
\label{eq:tcp_displacement_metric}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The surface-frame components of both displacement vectors were recorded to
show whether the angular change was accompanied by normal motion or tangential
sliding.}

\Revised{The model-estimated external force \mbox{\(\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}\)} was reported
by libfranka. The clearance-transition reference \mbox{\(\hat f_{\mathrm{ref}}\)}
was subtracted from it. The normal-force magnitude \mbox{\(F_n\)} was}

\begin{equation}
F_n
=
\left|
n_s^\top
\left(
\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}-\hat f_{\mathrm{ref}}
\right)
\right|.
\label{eq:model_estimated_normal_load}
\end{equation}

\Revised{This change in the robot-model force estimate provided the supporting load
quantity for matched-run comparisons. Its common calculation preserved a
consistent load basis across all contact settings.}

\Revised{The commanded normal force and commanded TCP moment used in the
controller-response plots were instead obtained from the commanded wrench
\mbox{\(F=[f^\top,m^\top]^\top\)}:}

\begin{equation}
F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}=n_s^\top f,
\qquad
M_{t_i,\mathrm{cmd}}=t_i^\top m.
\label{eq:commanded_surface_wrench_components}
\end{equation}

\Revised{Thus \mbox{\(F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}\)} is a signed command, whereas
\mbox{\(F_n=|n_s^\top\Delta\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}|\)} is the positive magnitude
of a model-estimated force change. With \mbox{\(n_s\)} directed out of the
plate, a steady press gives
\mbox{\(n_s^\top\Delta\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}\approx F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}<0\)}, or
equivalently \mbox{\(F_n\approx-F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}\)}. This approximate
agreement is an equilibrium check; it does not make the measured and commanded
signals identical.}

\Revised{The controller orientation error \mbox{\(e_R\)} is referenced to the orientation
stored at the clearance transition. It therefore describes deviation from the
held controller reference, while \mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\)} describes the
relationship between the grinding face and the calibrated surface. The latter
is an EE-inferred geometric quantity and is reported separately from the
controller response. Across Cases~A to~H, the principal response metric is the
signed component of \mbox{\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}^S\)} about the
commanded rotation axis. TCP height provides the separate geometry-based
classification of whether the grinding face ended flat.}


\subsection{Treatment of Repeated Runs}
\label{subsec:experimental_repeated_run_treatment}
\label{sec:evaluation_criteria_contact}

\Revised{Each complete parameter setting was repeated three times. For a
measured quantity \mbox{\(y\)}, the repetition value \mbox{\(y_j\)} carries
the index \mbox{\(j\)} and \mbox{\(N_y\)} is the number of usable repetitions.
The arithmetic mean \mbox{\(\bar y\)} was}

\begin{equation}
\bar y
=
\frac{1}{N_y}
\sum_{j=1}^{N_y}y_j.
\label{eq:experimental_sample_mean}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The sample standard deviation \mbox{\(s_y\)} was}

\begin{equation}
s_y
=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{N_y-1}
\sum_{j=1}^{N_y}
\left(y_j-\bar y\right)^2
}.
\label{eq:experimental_sample_standard_deviation}
\end{equation}

\Revised{Cases~A and~B compare the signed set-up rotation with the varied
rotational or cross-axis translational stiffness. Case~C compares
direction-selected and fixed levers across four commanded rotation directions.
The EE-inferred alignment angle, TCP-height flatness, displacements, and
model-estimated normal load provide supporting quantities.}

\Revised{Case~D provides the reference response to a commanded orientation offset without a
lever. Cases~E and~F compare that response with a tangential lever named in the
end-effector frame and in the surface frame. Cases~G and~H evaluate the
signed set-up rotation as a function of tool-axis centre position and
commanded orientation-offset magnitude.}

\Revised{The comparison was restricted to settings with matching calibrated geometry,
phase timing, fixed gains, and null-space parameters. Differences were
interpreted together with the within-setting variation and the zero-tilt
reference reported in \mbox{\Cref{sec:results_repeatability}}. The largest response
among the sampled levels is reported as the best tested magnitude.}


\section{Secondary Null-Space Pose-Hold Study}
\label{sec:nullspace_hold_study}
\label{subsec:nullspace_automatic_method}

\Revised{Matched Cartesian pose-hold trials quantified the two null-space torque
\mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}\)} contributions. The pose-hold controller maintained the
initial TCP pose, while an internally commanded joint torque
\mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\)} represented the
effect of a point force applied at a specified robot-link location. This
repeatable software command provided a common disturbance input for all
settings.}

\Revised{The force point was assigned to link~3 at the link-frame offset
\mbox{\(r_p\)}:}

\begin{equation}
r_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.200
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_point}
\end{equation}

\Revised{At the initial posture \mbox{\(q_{\mathrm{init}}\)}, the structural null direction
\mbox{\(v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\)} and the translational point Jacobian
\mbox{\(J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})\)} defined the base-frame
unit force direction \mbox{\(u_f\)}:}

\begin{equation}
u_f
=
\frac{
J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})
}{
\lVert J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\rVert_2
}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The direction was held constant throughout the trial. The commanded
disturbance-force magnitude was \mbox{\(F_d=20\,\mathrm{N}\)}. The disturbance
force \mbox{\(f_d(t)\)} was mapped by the current point Jacobian to the disturbance
torque \mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)\)}:}

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)
=
J_p(q(t))^\top f_d(t),
\qquad
f_d(t)
=
F_d s_d(t)u_f.
\label{eq:nullspace_automatic_disturbance}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The scale \mbox{\(s_d(t)\)} increased from zero to one through a half-cosine between
\mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} and \mbox{\(7\,\mathrm{s}\)}, remained at one until
\mbox{\(8\,\mathrm{s}\)}, and returned to zero during the following second. The
joint-torque-equivalent disturbance was limited to}

\begin{equation}
\lVert\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\rVert_2
\leq
3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{equation}

\Revised{Four null-space settings were tested with three repetitions each. The first
setting disabled the null-space torque \mbox{\(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}\)}. The second used projected damping with
\mbox{\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)}. The remaining settings used
singular-value conditioning with \mbox{\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} and
\mbox{\(k_\sigma=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, respectively. The SVD probe step, sign
deadband, and relative singular-value threshold remained at
\mbox{\(0.08\,\mathrm{rad}\)}, \mbox{\(10^{-6}\)}, and \mbox{\(10^{-4}\)}.}

\Revised{All twelve runs used the fixed pose-hold damping matrices
\mbox{\(D_p=50I_3\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}\)} and
\mbox{\(D_R=8I_3\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)}. The archived configuration used these
fixed matrices for pose hold.}

\Revised{The predefined task-retention limit was a peak Cartesian position error
\mbox{\(e_p\)} of
\mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The integrated projected joint motion
\mbox{\(E_N\)} measured the null-space motion during the disturbance:}

\begin{equation}
E_N
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert N_q(q)\dot q\rVert_2
\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_excursion_metric}
\end{equation}

\Revised{The integral was converted to degrees for comparison with the angular
quantities reported elsewhere. Singular-value conditioning was evaluated from
the change in the smallest singular value \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} between the initial and final
states. Cartesian task retention was evaluated from the peak position-error
norm during the pose hold. The acceptance outcome and measured differences
between the four settings are reported in the results chapter.}

\Revised{The disturbance is the torque equivalent of a commanded point force. It
provides a repeatable controller input for comparing the four null-space
settings.}


\section{Robot-Side Monitoring and Procedural Limits}
\label{sec:experimental_monitoring_limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

\Revised{Before torque control started, the robot-side collision behaviour was
configured with equal acceleration and nominal joint-torque thresholds of
\mbox{\(140\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}. The six Cartesian force and moment components used
equal thresholds of \mbox{\(240\,\mathrm{N}\)} or
\mbox{\(240\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)}, according to the component type. These values define
the conditions for the Franka reflex response.}

\Revised{The joint-torque callback returned the calculated controller command directly
to libfranka. Robot-side monitoring supplied the reflex mechanism through the
configured joint and Cartesian collision thresholds. These values document the
operating configuration used throughout the measurements.}

\Revised{The motion sequence added several procedural limits. Surface approach was
bounded to \mbox{\(0.640\,\mathrm{m}\)}, and the transition to set-up occurred at a
geometrically defined clearance of \mbox{\(20\,\mathrm{mm}\)}. The pre-set-up
gate was disabled, so the controller entered set-up directly at this
transition. Set-up was bounded by a \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{s}\)} timeout and by the alternative
\mbox{\(150\,\mathrm{N\,m}\)} change in the norm of the model-estimated external
moment after the minimum phase time.}

\Revised{The set-up stiffness and signed press reference also define a command
scale. For a completely blocked \mbox{\(0.260\,\mathrm{m}\)} reference change along the
calibrated normal, the blocked-reference normal-force scale
\mbox{\(F_{n,\mathrm{block}}\)} follows from
\mbox{\Cref{eq:experimental_setup_translational_stiffness}}:}

\begin{equation}
F_{n,\mathrm{block}}
=
0.260\,
n_s^\top K_{p,\mathrm{set}}n_s
=
91.0\,\mathrm{N},
\label{eq:experimental_blocked_normal_force_scale}
\end{equation}

\Revised{This value describes the virtual elastic command under a fully blocked
reference. The actual robot motion reduces the tracking error, and the value is
therefore a command-scale bound rather than a measured contact load.}

\Revised{The set-up moment threshold was applied to the change in libfranka's
model-estimated external moment after subtraction of the value stored at the
clearance transition. Libfranka reports this wrench as acting on stiffness
frame~K, with its components expressed in the base frame. The threshold
therefore terminated no run, and
set-up ended on its timeout throughout. It served as a controller termination
condition rather than a calibrated contact-force or contact-moment boundary.
The timeout, collision configuration, logged torque command, displacement, and
model-estimated wrench were considered together when assessing each run.}
