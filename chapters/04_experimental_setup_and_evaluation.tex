\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

This chapter describes how the controller developed in \Cref{ch:implementation}
was evaluated experimentally. The methodology is organised in the order in
which the experiments can be reproduced: the physical system is introduced
first, followed by the surface and tool calibration, the common contact-test
configuration and procedure, the parameter matrix for Cases~A to~H, the
quantities used for evaluation, and the separate Cartesian pose-hold study for
the null-space controller.

The main data set consists of surface-contact measurements in which the
commanded tool orientation offset, directional Cartesian stiffness, and virtual
centre of compliance \(p_c\) were varied under otherwise matched conditions.
The primary controller-response quantity is the signed end-effector rotation
during set-up. The alignment angle calculated from the end-effector pose,
TCP-height flatness classification, displacement, commanded wrench, and
model-estimated external load are retained as supporting quantities. A second,
independent experiment evaluates the null-space terms under a repeatable
internally commanded disturbance.

\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:experimental_system}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Robot, Tool, and Surface}
\label{subsec:experimental_robot_tool_surface}

The experiments were performed with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika
Panda operated through the Franka Control Interface. Joint-torque commands were
transmitted through libfranka at the nominal control rate of
\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\), corresponding to a sampling period of
\(1\,\mathrm{ms}\). The real-time
controller, Cartesian impedance law, contact geometry, and phase sequence are
described in \Cref{ch:implementation}.

The rectangular grinding tool was held by the Franka Hand using custom gripper
fingers. The tool face measured \(40\times120\,\mathrm{mm}\), with the
width along \(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and the length along \(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). Its
centre was located \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the
reported end-effector origin. The pads holding the tool were screwed to the
gripper fingers. The mounting allowed approximately \(\pm2^\circ\) of unloaded
relative rotation about \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). This observation is treated as a
mounting characteristic, not as a calibrated uncertainty bound under contact
load.

The surface was a plane plate mounted on a raised base board. All reported
measurements used dry contact. The physical surface orientation and the
tool normal were calibrated separately before the measurements, as
described in \Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}.

\subsection{Real-Time Computer and Software}
\label{subsec:experimental_software_computer}

The controller computer ran Ubuntu~20.04.3 with the
\texttt{5.9.1-rt20} Linux kernel and the \texttt{PREEMPT\_RT} patch. The
controller was compiled using \texttt{g++~9.3}, C++14, and optimisation level
\texttt{-O2}. Eigen~3.3.7 was used for matrix calculations and
libfranka~0.7.1 for communication with the robot.

Before the measurements, the computer--robot connection was checked with the
\texttt{communication\_test} program supplied with libfranka~0.7.1
\citep{libfrankaCommunicationTest}. The program reports the fraction of
control commands received by the robot and issues its lower
communication-quality warning below an average command-success rate of
\(0.90\). The experimental system passed this criterion. This check verifies
communication quality for operation through the nominal \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\)
interface; it is not a worst-case execution-time or scheduling-jitter
measurement.


\subsection{Operating and Safety Configuration}
\label{subsec:reproducibility_record}
\label{sec:experimental_monitoring_limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

Before torque control started, the robot-side collision behaviour was
configured with equal acceleration and nominal joint-torque thresholds of
\(140\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The six Cartesian force and moment channels used
thresholds of \(240\,\mathrm{N}\) or \(240\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), according to the
component type. These thresholds supplied the Franka reflex mechanism and were
kept unchanged throughout the reported measurements.

The complete run-specific controller configuration is given in
\Cref{app:controller_parameters}.

\section{Surface and Tool Calibration}
\label{sec:surface_tool_calibration}
\label{sec:contact_geometry}
% ============================================================================

The controller requires the physical plane and the tool normal in
consistent coordinate frames. These quantities were estimated independently.
The surface calibration determined a point on the plane, its normal, and the
two surface tangents. The tool calibration determined that normal in
end-effector coordinates. The calibrated values were then used by both the
controller and the evaluation.

\subsection{Surface-Plane Calibration}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

Four positions distributed over the plate were probed with the same marked
\(+X_{\mathrm{EE}},+Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\) corner of the tool face. The first
three points, \(P_1\)--\(P_3\), defined the plane; the fourth point \(P_4\) was
excluded from the fit and used for validation.

For each capture \(i\), the measured end-effector position
\(p_{\mathrm{EE},i}\) and orientation \(R_{\mathrm{EE},i}\) were combined with
the fixed end-effector-frame corner offset

\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{probe,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.020\\
0.060\\
0.020
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:plane_probe_corner_offset}
\end{equation}

The corresponding base-frame corner position was

\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{cal},i}
=
p_{\mathrm{EE},i}+R_{\mathrm{EE},i}r_{\mathrm{probe,EE}}.
\label{eq:plane_probe_corner_position}
\end{equation}

The calibrated plane is represented by

\begin{equation}
n_s^\top(p-p_s)=0,
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_plane}
\end{equation}

where \(p_s\) is a point on the plane and \(n_s\) is the unit outward surface
normal. The fitted plane point stored in the controller was

\begin{equation}
p_s
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.5153\\
-0.1072\\
0.0031
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_point}
\end{equation}

This point is the centroid of the three fitted corner coordinates. The
controller stores the plane orientation through rotations \(\alpha_s\) about
the base \(x\)-axis and \(\beta_s\) about the base \(y\)-axis. With \(e_z\)
the base-frame \(z\)-axis,

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
R_y(\beta_s)R_x(\alpha_s)e_z
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin(\beta_s)\cos(\alpha_s)\\
-\sin(\alpha_s)\\
\cos(\beta_s)\cos(\alpha_s)
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:implemented_surface_normal_angles}
\end{equation}

The calibrated angles were

\begin{equation}
\alpha_s=-1.585^\circ,
\qquad
\beta_s=+0.988^\circ,
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tilt_angles}
\end{equation}

which give

\begin{equation}
n_s
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
0.017245\\
0.027663\\
0.999469
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_s\|=1.
\label{eq:surface_normal}
\end{equation}

The held-out point provided an independent geometric check. Its perpendicular
residual from the calibrated plane was

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
\left|n_s^\top(p_{\mathrm{cal},4}-p_s)\right|,
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_residual}
\end{equation}

with

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}=0.82\,\mathrm{mm}.
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_value}
\end{equation}

The in-plane orientation was fixed by projecting the base-frame reference
direction \(a_s=e_x\) onto the calibrated plane:

\begin{equation}
t_1
=
\frac{(I-n_sn_s^\top)a_s}
{\left\|(I-n_sn_s^\top)a_s\right\|}.
\label{eq:calibrated_first_surface_tangent}
\end{equation}

The second tangent completes the right-handed frame,

\begin{equation}
t_2=n_s\times t_1.
\label{eq:calibrated_second_surface_tangent}
\end{equation}

For the calibrated plane,

\begin{equation}
t_1
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.999851\\
-0.000477\\
-0.017238
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
t_2
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.000000\\
 0.999618\\
-0.027667
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tangent_values}
\end{equation}

The surface frame used throughout the contact measurements is therefore

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{surface}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_1 & t_2 & n_s
\end{bmatrix}
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.999851 & 0.000000 & 0.017245\\
-0.000477 & 0.999618 & 0.027663\\
-0.017238 &-0.027667 & 0.999469
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:surface_task_frame}
\end{equation}

All surface-resolved quantities use the coordinate order
\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\).


\subsection{Tool Normal Calibration}
\label{subsec:grinding_face_calibration}

The tool normal in the end-effector frame cannot be taken from the
nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) axis alone because the physical tool and its mount
introduce geometric offsets. To identify this direction, the complete tool
face was seated flat on the calibrated plane at four different yaw angles. The
face normal in the base frame remains unchanged when only the yaw is varied;
therefore the end-effector-frame direction that maps to the same base-frame
direction across the captures identifies the tool normal.

The first three captured rotations were used for estimation and the fourth was
retained for validation. With \(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}\) the rotational part of the
captured pose,

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{cal},1}n_{\mathrm{EE}}
\approx
R_{\mathrm{cal},2}n_{\mathrm{EE}}
\approx
R_{\mathrm{cal},3}n_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
\bar n_B.
\label{eq:tool_axis_invariant_direction}
\end{equation}

The estimator formed
\(\bar R=(R_{\mathrm{cal},1}+R_{\mathrm{cal},2}+R_{\mathrm{cal},3})/3\) and
applied the singular-value decomposition \(\bar R=U\Sigma V^\top\). The
corresponding dominant singular-vector pair provided the invariant base-frame
direction and the tool normal in the end-effector frame. The sign
was chosen toward nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\). The final value used in every
reported run was

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.026387057\\
-0.006678514\\
0.999629492
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_{\mathrm{EE}}\|=1.
\label{eq:calibrated_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

It differs from nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) by approximately \(1.56^\circ\).
The calibration identifies the effective geometric direction used by the
controller and evaluation; it does not determine the physical source of this
offset.

This calibrated direction is the one used by both the controller and the
evaluation. The flat orientation target is the orientation that maps it onto
the inward surface normal, so the target sign is \(s_a=-1\). The commanded spin about the desired
tool axis, measured from \(t_1\), was \(90^\circ\) for all reported
runs. In the flat target orientation the projected long face axis is aligned
with the \(t_2\) line.

\end{figure}


\subsection{Calibrated Experimental Geometry}
\label{subsec:calibrated_contact_geometry}

The calibrated tool face is represented in end-effector coordinates by
its centre offset

\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{face,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.020
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m},
\label{eq:grinding_face_center_ee}
\end{equation}

and the half-width and half-length vectors

\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{width,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.020\\
0\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{length,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0.060\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:grinding_face_half_dimensions}
\end{equation}

These calibrated offsets, together with a feature-selection tolerance of
\(\varepsilon_g=0.1\,\mathrm{mm}\), were used by the tool-point selection

and averaged. The present chapter records the calibrated geometry; the
selection algorithm that consumes it belongs to \Cref{ch:implementation}.

\section{Common Contact-Test Configuration and Procedure}
\label{sec:experimental_controller_parameters}
\label{sec:cartesian_stiffness_selection}

All surface-contact cases used the same calibrated geometry, phase sequence,
normal press trajectory, fixed gain entries, and null-space configuration
except where a parameter was explicitly varied. This section defines that
common experimental basis before the individual cases are introduced in

\subsection{Common Cartesian Gains and Damping}
\label{subsec:initial_stiffness_damping_values}
\label{subsec:experimental_inertia_scaled_damping}

The phase-dependent gains are diagonal in the calibrated surface frame
\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\) before any compliance-centre shift.
\Cref{tab:experimental_common_gains} gives the entries used by every reported
contact run, together with the damping ratio \(\zeta\) of the corresponding
phase. The configured grinding phase carried the set-up entries unchanged. The
pre-set-up gate was configured with the same stiffness as set-up and was
disabled in the reported runs, so it did not act as a separate phase.

During orientation and approach the translational stiffness was isotropic and
the rotational stiffness was higher about the surface tangents. In set-up the
lower normal entry limits the elastic normal-force increase for a given normal
position error, while the higher tangential entries restrict motion in the
surface plane. The low rotational stiffnesses about \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) permit
contact-induced alignment rotation, and the higher entry about \(n_s\) mainly
resists in-plane spin of the tool face. Because the set-up translational
stiffness \(K_{p,\mathrm{set}}\) is diagonal in the surface frame, the effective
normal stiffness \(K_{p,n}=n_s^\top K_{p,\mathrm{set}}n_s\) equals the tabulated
normal entry of \(350\,\mathrm{N/m}\).

The directional damping coefficients were calculated from the Cartesian inertia
and the active stiffness at the tabulated damping ratio, as described in
\Cref{subsec:inertia_scaled_damping_calculation}. Detailed fallback damping
values, used only if the inertia-based calculation could not be applied, are
provided in \Cref{app:controller_parameters}.

Before each run, the active parameter set was loaded and the robot was moved to
the configured initial joint posture. The calibrated geometry, the phase gains
and damping, and the compliance-centre definition were prepared before the
torque callback started.

Approach ended when the selected tool point reached \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\)
above the calibrated plane. That transition was
defined by geometry rather than by a force threshold. At the same instant the
controller stored the TCP pose, the orientation reference, the selected tool
point and its projection onto the plane, and the
model-estimated external wrench, which serve as the references for the
evaluation quantities of \Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}.

The set-up press endpoint of \(+0.240\,\mathrm{m}\) is a Cartesian spring
reference beyond the calibrated plane and is not interpreted as a physical
penetration of the same magnitude. The reference reached it after approximately
\(3.25\,\mathrm{s}\) and was then held. Throughout set-up the rotational
reference remained the orientation captured at the clearance transition, so the
measured alignment rotation is a response to contact rather than the tracking
of a time-varying orientation command. In Cases~A, B, and~C the decoupled
Cartesian impedance was active during set-up, and in Cases~D to~H the
point-shifted \(6\times6\) impedance associated with the selected centre of
compliance.

Every reported contact run ended at the \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) set-up timeout, and
the moment-change threshold was reached in none of them. The pre-grind gate
then held the reached pose. No run entered the configured grinding sweep, so
the archived data cover orientation, approach, and set-up only.

The set-up stiffness and commanded press range also define a virtual elastic
command scale. For a completely blocked \(0.260\,\mathrm{m}\) reference change
along the calibrated normal,

\begin{equation}
F_{n,\mathrm{block}}
=
0.260\,n_s^\top K_{p,\mathrm{set}}n_s
=
91.0\,\mathrm{N}.
\label{eq:experimental_blocked_normal_force_scale}
\end{equation}

This value is a command-scale bound for a fully blocked virtual spring and not
a measured contact load.


\subsection{Common Null-Space Configuration}
\label{subsec:nullspace_parameter_rationale}

Projected null-space damping and singular-value conditioning were active
together throughout the surface-contact measurements. Keeping this secondary
controller fixed prevents changes in the null-space policy from becoming an
additional variable across Cases~A to~H. The gains were

\begin{equation}
d_{\mathrm{null}}
=
2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad},
\qquad
k_\sigma
=
1\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\label{eq:experimental_contact_nullspace_gains}
\end{equation}

This is the combined secondary-torque mode of the implementation described in
\Cref{sec:nullspace_conditioning_implementation}. The contact measurements
therefore evaluate the surface-alignment controller with both null-space terms
active. Their individual effects are examined separately in the pose-hold
experiment of \Cref{sec:nullspace_hold_study}.


\subsection{Repetitions and Data Recording}
\label{subsec:experimental_recorded_quantities}

The real-time logging buffer recorded the measured robot state, Cartesian
reference and error, selected tool point, active control phase,
commanded Cartesian wrench, final commanded joint torque, null-space mode, and
libfranka model-estimated external wrench. The complete signal list and CSV
structure are given in \Cref{sec:recorded_signals} and
\Cref{app:recorded_data}.

Three repetitions were scheduled for each parameter setting, giving \(171\)
surface-contact runs across \(57\) settings. One run of one Case-D setting was
not recorded completely and was therefore excluded. The quantitative evaluation
uses the remaining \(170\) contact runs. That setting has two usable
repetitions; every other setting has three. Within each controlled comparison, the calibrated geometry,
common phase parameters, fixed gains, and null-space settings remained
unchanged. Only the parameter assigned to the corresponding case was varied.

The run counts in Table~\ref{tab:experimental_test_cases} include the three scheduled
repetitions for each newly recorded setting. Shared reference conditions are
identified in the table and were not recorded or counted twice.

In Case~F the centre of compliance was displaced along the tool axis. An axial
displacement produces no tangential lever component when the tool axis is
exactly parallel to the normal press direction. At finite inclination a small
tangential component remains, so the case tests the sensitivity of a nominally
weak direction rather than an expected effect.


\subsection{Case-H Direction and Lever Definition}
\label{subsec:impedance_around_pole}

The compliance-centre lever follows the convention

\begin{equation}
r_c=p_{\mathrm{TCP}}-p_c.
\label{eq:experimental_compliance_lever}
\end{equation}

This is the shift of the impedance reference point. It is not the offset
between the TCP and the physical contact point, which follows from the
tool geometry of \Cref{subsec:calibrated_contact_geometry}. The two
are separate quantities and are reported as such, as set out in
\Cref{sec:coupled_pole_stiffness}.

A tool-fixed lever is constant in end-effector coordinates and therefore
rotates in the base frame with the end effector. A surface-fixed lever is
constant in the surface frame \([t_1,t_2,n_s]\). In both cases, the active
lever is transformed to base coordinates during each control cycle before the
stiffness and damping matrices are shifted to the TCP.

For a commanded rotation represented by surface components
\((\theta_{t_1},\theta_{t_2})\), the direction-selected tangential lever is

\begin{equation}
r_{c,t}
=
\frac{\rho_c}{\sqrt{\theta_{t_1}^{2}+\theta_{t_2}^{2}}}
\left(\theta_{t_2}t_1-\theta_{t_1}t_2\right),
\label{eq:experimental_general_lever_rule}
\end{equation}

where \(\rho_c>0\) is the selected lever magnitude. Case~H used
\(\rho_c=40\,\mathrm{mm}\). The selected lever lies perpendicular to the
commanded rotation direction in the tangent plane, and its sign is chosen so
that the press-induced coupling moment acts in the required alignment
direction. It therefore changes with the commanded rotation direction, whereas
the fixed comparison retains the lever selected for the \(t_1\) command.

\section{Evaluation Quantities}
\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}
% ============================================================================

The evaluation separates the controller response from geometric quantities
that depend on the calibrated tool model. The signed set-up rotation is the
primary comparison quantity for Cases~A to~H. The alignment angle, TCP-height
classification, displacements, commanded wrench, and model-estimated normal
load provide complementary information and are not treated as interchangeable
measurements of the same physical quantity.


\subsection{Signed Set-Up Rotation}
\label{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}

The finite end-effector rotation between the clearance transition and the end
of set-up is written as

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}
=
\phi_{\mathrm{set}}u_{\mathrm{set}}.
\label{eq:contact_induced_rotation}
\end{equation}

Here \(\phi_{\mathrm{set}}\) and \(u_{\mathrm{set}}\) are the angle and unit
axis of the relative rotation
\(R_{\mathrm{after}}R_{\mathrm{before}}^\top\), evaluated using the same
finite-rotation relations as in
\Cref{eq:angle_from_trace,eq:rotation_axis_from_matrix}. The vector is expressed
in the robot base frame.

For comparison with the commanded surface directions, it is resolved in the
surface frame:

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}^{S}
=
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}.
\label{eq:surface_resolved_setup_rotation}
\end{equation}

The signed component along the commanded rotation direction is the primary
controller-response metric. A positive value denotes rotation in the intended
alignment direction. This quantity is calculated from end-effector motion and
does not require the calibrated tool normal \(n_{\mathrm{EE}}\).


\subsection{Alignment Angle and Flatness Classification}
\label{subsec:experimental_alignment_change}

The tool normal used for the alignment calculation is obtained
from the measured end-effector orientation and the calibrated direction in the
end-effector frame:

\begin{equation}
n_T=R_{\mathrm{EE}}n_{\mathrm{EE}}.
\label{eq:evaluation_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

The alignment angle is

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(-n_T^\top n_s\right).
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

The negative sign follows the flat target
\(R_dn_{\mathrm{EE}}=-n_s\). The angle describes the tool alignment
calculated from the end-effector pose under the assumption that the calibrated
relation between the tool and end effector remains fixed. Relative motion in
the tool mounting is not captured by this calculation.

The change over set-up is reported as

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\theta_{\mathrm{align,before}}
-
\theta_{\mathrm{align,after}},
\label{eq:alignment_error_improvement}
\end{equation}

so a positive value means that the calculated angular difference decreased.
The before value is taken at the clearance transition and the after value at
the end of set-up.

A separate geometry-based flatness classification is obtained from the final
TCP height relative to the calibrated plane and the height expected from the
calibrated rectangular tool geometry when the tool face is flat. This
classification uses the end-effector position and calibrated geometry; it is
not a direct angular measurement of the physical tool face. The alignment
angle and the TCP-height classification are therefore reported separately from
the primary set-up rotation.


\subsection{Displacement and Wrench Quantities}

Let \(p_T\) denote the tool point selected at the clearance transition. Its displacement and magnitude are

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_T
=
p_{T,\mathrm{after}}-p_{T,\mathrm{before}},
\qquad
s_T=\lVert\Delta p_T\rVert.
\label{eq:contact_feature_displacement}
\end{equation}

The TCP displacement is evaluated in the same form:

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}
=
p_{\mathrm{TCP,after}}-p_{\mathrm{TCP,before}},
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{TCP}}=\lVert\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\rVert.
\label{eq:tcp_displacement_metric}
\end{equation}

Both displacement vectors were additionally resolved in the surface frame to
separate tangential and normal motion.

The supporting normal-load quantity is derived from the change in the
libfranka model-estimated external force relative to the value stored at the
clearance transition:

\begin{equation}
F_n
=
\left|
n_s^\top\left(\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}-\hat f_{\mathrm{ref}}\right)
\right|.
\label{eq:model_estimated_normal_load}
\end{equation}

This is a model-estimated force change, not an independently measured contact
force. The commanded normal force and commanded TCP moment used in the
controller-response plots are instead obtained from
\(F=[f^\top,m^\top]^\top\):

\begin{equation}
F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}=n_s^\top f,
\qquad
M_{t_i,\mathrm{cmd}}=t_i^\top m.
\label{eq:commanded_surface_wrench_components}
\end{equation}

Thus \(F_{n,\mathrm{cmd}}\) is a signed command, while \(F_n\) is the positive
magnitude of a model-estimated change. During a quasi-static press the two are
expected to have approximately opposite magnitude under the chosen sign
convention, but they remain distinct controller signals.


\subsection{Treatment of Repeated Runs}
\label{subsec:experimental_repeated_run_treatment}
\label{sec:evaluation_criteria_contact}

Repeated settings are reported using the arithmetic mean and the sample
standard deviation over their usable repetitions.

The within-setting standard deviations are reported with the corresponding
results in \Cref{ch:results_discussion}. Comparisons are restricted to settings
with the same calibrated geometry, common phase timing, fixed gain entries, and
null-space configuration.

\section{Secondary Null-Space Pose-Hold Experiment}
\label{sec:nullspace_hold_study}
\label{subsec:nullspace_automatic_method}
% ============================================================================

The surface-contact measurements keep projected damping and singular-value
conditioning active together. A separate Cartesian pose-hold experiment was
therefore used to compare their individual effects under a repeatable
internally commanded disturbance.


\subsection{Experimental Configuration}

The pose-hold controller maintained the initial TCP pose with the gains of
\Cref{tab:experimental_hold_gains}. Every entry was isotropic, so each matrix
is the stated value times the identity. Unlike the contact-sequence phases, the
twelve pose-hold trials used fixed Cartesian damping rather than inertia-scaled
damping.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Isotropic Cartesian gains of the pose-hold trials.}
\label{tab:experimental_hold_gains}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & & \textbf{Value}\\
\midrule
Translational stiffness & \(K_{p,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)\\
Rotational stiffness    & \(K_{R,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)\\
Translational damping   & \(D_{p,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(50\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}\)\\
Rotational damping      & \(D_{R,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(8\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Four null-space settings were tested with three repetitions each: no null-space
torque, projected damping with
\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\), sigma conditioning with
\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), and sigma conditioning with
\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The probe step, sigma deadband, and relative
SVD threshold remained at \(0.08\,\mathrm{rad}\), \(10^{-6}\), and
\(10^{-4}\), respectively.


\subsection{Commanded Disturbance}

The disturbance represents the joint-torque equivalent of a commanded point
force acting at a specified location on link~3. The point was defined by the
link-frame offset

\begin{equation}
r_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.200
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_point}
\end{equation}

At the initial posture \(q_{\mathrm{init}}\), the structural null direction
\(v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) and the translational point Jacobian
\(J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) define the fixed base-frame force direction

\begin{equation}
u_f
=
\frac{J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})}
{\lVert J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\rVert_2}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}
\end{equation}

The commanded force magnitude was \(F_d=20\,\mathrm{N}\). The force waveform
and corresponding equivalent joint torque were

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)
=
J_p(q(t))^\top f_d(t),
\qquad
f_d(t)=F_d s_d(t)u_f.
\label{eq:nullspace_automatic_disturbance}
\end{equation}

The scale \(s_d(t)\) increased from zero to one through a half-cosine from
\(5\) to \(7\,\mathrm{s}\), remained at one until \(8\,\mathrm{s}\), and
returned to zero during the following second. The equivalent disturbance was
limited to

\[
\lVert\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\rVert_2\leq3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\]

The disturbance is internal to the controller and therefore provides a
repeatable comparison input; it is not a separately applied physical force.


\subsection{Evaluation Quantities}

The predefined Cartesian task-retention criterion was a peak position-error
norm below \(2\,\mathrm{mm}\). Redundant motion was quantified by integrating
the norm of the projected joint velocity over the disturbance interval,

\begin{equation}
E_N
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert N_q(q)\dot q\rVert_2\,\mathrm{d}t,
\label{eq:nullspace_excursion_metric}
\end{equation}

and the result was converted to degrees for reporting. Singular-value
conditioning was evaluated from the final change in \(\sigma_{\min}\), while
Cartesian task retention was evaluated from the peak position-error norm. The
corresponding results are presented in \Cref{ch:results_discussion}.
