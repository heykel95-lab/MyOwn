\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\Added{This chapter defines the experimental system, the calibration
procedures, the surface-contact test matrix, and the evaluation quantities. The
surface-contact experiments varied the configured orientation offset, the
directional Cartesian stiffness, and the virtual \mbox{\abbr{CoC}}, while the
remaining experimental conditions were held constant. The primary outcome was
the end-effector rotation generated during Contact Establishment.}

\Added{A separate Cartesian pose-hold experiment evaluated projected null-space
damping and singular-value conditioning under a repeatable internally commanded
disturbance, referred to below as the virtual disturbance.}

\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:experimental_system}

\subsection{Robot, Tool, and Surface}
\label{subsec:experimental_robot_tool_surface}

The experiments were performed with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika
Panda operated through the Franka Control Interface. Joint-torque commands were
transmitted through libfranka at the nominal control rate of
\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\), corresponding to a sampling period of
\(1\,\mathrm{ms}\). The real-time
controller, Cartesian impedance law, contact geometry, and state sequence are
described in \Cref{ch:implementation}.

The rectangular grinding tool was held by the Franka Hand using custom gripper
fingers. The tool face measured \(40\times120\,\mathrm{mm}\), with the
width along \(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and the length along \(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). Its
centre was located \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the
end-effector origin. The tool was clamped by custom pads screwed to
the gripper fingers.

\Added{The plane plate was mounted on a raised base board, and every
experiment used dry contact. Panel~(a) of
\mbox{\Cref{fig:experimental_setup_photo}} shows the physical arrangement.
Panel~(b) separates the configured surface reference, which is a controller
quantity, from the physical plane of the set-up.
\mbox{\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}} describes how the configured
surface reference and the tool normal were established separately.}

\subsection{Real-Time Computer and Software}
\label{subsec:experimental_software_computer}

The controller computer ran Ubuntu~20.04.3 with the
\texttt{5.9.1-rt20} Linux kernel and the \texttt{PREEMPT\_RT} patch. The
controller was compiled using \texttt{g++~9.3}, C++14, and optimisation level
\texttt{-O2}. Eigen~3.3.7 was used for matrix calculations and
libfranka~0.7.1 for communication with the robot.

Before the measurements, the computer--robot connection was checked with the
\texttt{communication\_test} program supplied with libfranka~0.7.1
\citep{libfrankaCommunicationTest}. The program reports the fraction of
control commands received by the robot, and issues a communication-quality
warning when the mean command-success rate falls below \(0.90\). The
experimental system passed this criterion, which confirms that the
communication quality met the libfranka requirement for operation through the
nominal \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\) interface.


\subsection{Operating and Safety Configuration}
\label{subsec:reproducibility_record}
\label{sec:experimental_monitoring_limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

Before torque control started, the acceleration and nominal joint-torque
collision thresholds were both set to \(140\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) for every
joint. The Cartesian thresholds were set to \(240\,\mathrm{N}\) for the
force channels and \(240\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) for the moment channels. These
values configured the robot-side collision and reflex monitoring and were kept
unchanged throughout the measurements.

The complete experiment-specific controller configuration is given in
\Cref{app:controller_parameters}.

\section{Surface-Reference and Tool-Normal Calibration}
\label{sec:surface_tool_calibration}
\label{sec:contact_geometry}
% ============================================================================

\Added{The configured angular offset used in the surface-contact experiments
was introduced through the commanded pre-contact orientation. With
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}=0\)}, the controller commands the tool
face parallel to the configured surface reference. A non-zero
\mbox{\(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}\)} deliberately inclines the tool about
\mbox{\(t_1\)} before contact. Because the orientation actually reached can
differ slightly from this command, the achieved pose-based initial angular
offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)} is calculated from the measured tool
orientation at contact entry relative to the configured surface reference. It
therefore represents the actual initial angular condition used for the
experimental comparisons.}

\Added{The configured surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)} defines the controller
reference, whereas \mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{phys}}\)} denotes the normal of the
physical plate. A difference between these two directions additionally
contributes to the physical tool--surface mismatch, but it is separate from the
pose-based quantity \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}.}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{figures/surface_reference_geometry.tex}
  \caption{Physical, configured, and achieved angular references.}
  \label{fig:surface_reference_geometry}
\end{figure}

\Added{To establish the configured surface reference and tool normal, two
independent procedures were performed before the contact experiments.
Surface-reference construction determined \mbox{\(p_s\)}, \mbox{\(n_s\)}, and
\mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{surface}}\)} in the robot base frame, while tool-normal
calibration determined \mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\)} in end-effector
coordinates.}

\subsection{Surface-Reference Construction}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

\Added{The complete tool face was seated on the physical plane, and one
end-effector pose was recorded. At that pose, the nominal \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} axis transformed into
the base frame defined the configured surface normal \mbox{\(n_s\)}. The
transformed tool-face-centre offset defined the point \mbox{\(p_s\)} on the
configured reference plane. The surface tangents and \mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{surface}}\)}
were then constructed as in \mbox{\Cref{subsec:surface_task_frame}}. The
resulting configured surface normal corresponded to rotations of
\mbox{\(-1.585^\circ\)} about the base-frame \mbox{\(x\)}-axis and
\mbox{\(0.988^\circ\)} about the base-frame \mbox{\(y\)}-axis.
\mbox{\Cref{app:controller_parameters}} gives the complete numerical values.}

\subsection{Tool Normal Calibration}
\label{subsec:grinding_face_calibration}

\Added{The complete tool face was seated on the plane at three yaw
orientations. For each recorded pose, the physical tool-face normal corresponds
to the same direction in the robot base frame. A unit vector in end-effector
coordinates was therefore fitted so that its base-frame direction varied as
little as possible across the three poses, which yields
\mbox{\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\)}. The calibrated tool normal differed from the
nominal \mbox{\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)} direction by approximately
\mbox{\(1.56^\circ\)}. \mbox{\Cref{app:controller_parameters}} gives the
fitting relation and the numerical vector.}

\section{Initial Angular Condition and Contact Response}
\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}
% ============================================================================

The surface-contact analysis uses three orientation quantities. The
configured orientation offset \(\theta_{\mathrm{offset},t_1}\) defines the
desired pre-contact tool direction commanded during Tool Orientation. The
achieved pose-based initial angular offset \(\theta_{0,t_1}\) describes the
orientation actually reached at the start of Contact Establishment. The
contact-establishment response \(\gamma_{t_1}\) is the orientation change
measured during Contact Establishment.

The configured offset is therefore the commanded condition,
\(\theta_{0,t_1}\) the measured initial condition, and \(\gamma_{t_1}\) the
measured response used for the main experimental comparisons.

All three quantities take their sign from the same rotational direction.
\Cref{fig:rotation_sign_convention} shows the surface-frame plane spanned by
\(t_2\) and \(n_s\), with \(t_1\) pointing out of the page. A positive
rotation about \(t_1\) follows the right-hand rule and rotates \(t_2\)
towards \(n_s\), whatever its magnitude. Positive and negative values of the
three quantities therefore state the direction of rotation in that plane.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{figures/rotation_sign_convention.tex}
  \caption{Sign convention for rotation about \(t_1\).}
  \label{fig:rotation_sign_convention}
\end{figure}


\subsection{Achieved Pose-Based Initial Angular Offset}
\label{subsec:initial_angular_deviation}

\Added{At the start of Contact Establishment,
\mbox{\(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}}\)}, the measured end-effector orientation gives
the achieved calibrated tool-normal direction}

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}})
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}})n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}.
\label{eq:achieved_tool_normal}
\end{equation}

\Added{This direction is compared with the fixed configured surface-normal
direction \mbox{\(-n_s\)}. Let \mbox{\(\phi_0u_0\)} denote the shortest
rotation vector from the achieved tool-normal direction to \mbox{\(-n_s\)}:}

\begin{equation}
R(u_0,\phi_0)n_{\mathrm{Tool}}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}})
=-n_s.
\label{eq:initial_deviation_rotation}
\end{equation}

\Added{This rotation points from the achieved tool orientation back to the
configured surface-parallel orientation. The achieved initial angular offset is
defined in the opposite direction, from \mbox{\(-n_s\)} towards the achieved
tool direction. Its signed component about \mbox{\(t_1\)} is therefore}

\begin{equation}
\theta_{0,t_1}=-t_1^\top(\phi_0u_0).
\label{eq:pose_based_initial_deviation}
\end{equation}

\Added{The magnitude \mbox{\(\phi_0\)} is the total angular mismatch at
\mbox{\(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}}\)} relative to the configured surface reference,
while \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)} is its signed component about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}. The direction \mbox{\(n_s\)} is fixed and is not a surface
normal evaluated at \mbox{\(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}}\)}; the time dependence
enters through the measured tool orientation.}


\subsection{Contact-Establishment Response}
\label{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}

Let \(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}}\) and \(t_{\mathrm{CE,end}}\) denote the
measurement instants at the beginning and end of contact establishment.
Contact Establishment holds \mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{EE,clearance}}\)} as its
rotational reference, with
\mbox{\(R_{\mathrm{EE,clearance}}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}})\)}.
The relative rotation from the measured end orientation back to this reference
is

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{CE}}
=
R_{\mathrm{EE,clearance}}
R_{\mathrm{EE}}^\top(t_{\mathrm{CE,end}}).
\end{equation}

Writing this relative rotation in axis--angle form as
\(R_{\mathrm{CE}}=R(u_{\mathrm{CE}},\phi_{\mathrm{CE}})\) gives the
contact-establishment response vector \(\gamma\) in base coordinates:

\begin{equation}
\gamma
=
\phi_{\mathrm{CE}}u_{\mathrm{CE}}
=
e_R(t_{\mathrm{CE,end}}).
\label{eq:contact_induced_rotation}
\end{equation}

\Added{The response is calculated from the measured end-effector
orientations and resolved about \mbox{\(t_1\)}:}

\begin{equation}
\gamma_{t_1}=t_1^\top\gamma.
\label{eq:surface_resolved_setup_rotation}
\end{equation}

\Added{The quantity \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} is the contact-establishment
response about surface tangent \mbox{\(t_1\)}. Because \mbox{\(\gamma\)}
is formed from the measured end orientation back to the held entry orientation,
its direction is opposite to the start-to-end rotation of the end effector.
Under this convention, an end-effector rotation towards parallel alignment
with the configured surface gives \mbox{\(\gamma_{t_1}\)} the same
surface-tangent direction as the achieved initial angular offset
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)}. The magnitude
\mbox{\(\lvert\gamma_{t_1}\rvert\)} gives the size of the measured
response.}

\Added{The two angular quantities compare different pairs of directions.
\mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)} compares the entry tool direction with
\mbox{\(-n_s\)}, whereas \mbox{\(e_R\)} compares the current end-effector
orientation with the held contact-entry reference. The captured reference gives
\mbox{\(e_R=0\)} at the start of Contact Establishment, and
\mbox{\(\gamma=e_R\)} at its end.}

\section{Secondary Null-Space Pose-Hold Experiment}
\label{sec:nullspace_hold_study}
\label{subsec:nullspace_automatic_method}
% ============================================================================

Projected null-space damping and singular-value conditioning were active
together during the surface-contact experiments. A separate Cartesian pose-hold
experiment therefore evaluated the two terms individually, under the same
repeatable virtual disturbance.


\subsection{Experimental Configuration}
\label{subsec:nullspace_pose_hold_configuration}

The pose-hold controller captured and maintained the initial TCP pose with
the stiffness and damping of \Cref{tab:experimental_hold_gains}. The
translational and rotational matrices were isotropic, so each matrix is the
stated scalar value multiplied by the \(3\times3\) identity matrix. All
twelve pose-hold trials used fixed Cartesian damping.

\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & & \textbf{Value}\\
\midrule
Translational stiffness & \(K_{p,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)\\

Four null-space settings were evaluated in three trials each. These were no
null-space torque, \(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}=0\); projected damping with
\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\); and singular-value
conditioning at \(k_\sigma=1.5\) and \(2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The probe
step, the singular-value deadband, and the relative \abbr{SVD} threshold remained at
\(0.08\,\mathrm{rad}\), \(10^{-6}\), and \(10^{-4}\), respectively.


\subsection{Commanded Disturbance}

The disturbance was implemented as the joint-torque equivalent of a virtual
point force applied at a selected location on link~3. In the link frame, the
point was

\begin{equation}
r_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.200
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_point}
\end{equation}

At the initial posture \(q_{\mathrm{init}}\), the structural null direction
\(v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) is a joint-motion direction that preserves the
end-effector pose to first order, and the translational point Jacobian
\(J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) maps it to the corresponding velocity of the
selected link point. The fixed base-frame force direction was chosen along that
velocity,

\begin{equation}
u_f
=
\frac{J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})}
{\lVert J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\rVert_2}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}
\end{equation}

\Added{This choice aligns the virtual force with the motion of the selected
link point produced by the initial structural null direction, so the
disturbance excites the redundant configuration while the pose-hold impedance
opposes its Cartesian component.}

The commanded force magnitude was \(F_{\mathrm{dist}}=20\,\mathrm{N}\). The virtual force
and its equivalent joint torque were

\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)=F_{\mathrm{dist}}s_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)u_f,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)
=
J_p(q(t))^\top f_{\mathrm{dist}}(t).
\label{eq:nullspace_automatic_disturbance}
\end{equation}

Here \(u_f\) is the fixed unit force direction defined in
\Cref{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}, and \(s_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)\in[0,1]\) is a
dimensionless time-scaling function that switches the commanded virtual
disturbance smoothly on and off. It was defined as

\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,
& t<5\,\mathrm{s},\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1}{2}\left[
1-\cos\!\left(\pi\dfrac{t-5\,\mathrm{s}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}\right)
\right],
& 5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<7\,\mathrm{s},\\[3mm]
1,
& 7\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<8\,\mathrm{s},\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1}{2}\left[
1+\cos\!\left(\pi\dfrac{t-8\,\mathrm{s}}{1\,\mathrm{s}}\right)
\right],
& 8\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<9\,\mathrm{s},\\[3mm]
0,
& t\geq9\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_scale}
\end{equation}

The disturbance increased smoothly from zero to the full commanded
magnitude between \(5\) and \(7\,\mathrm{s}\), remained at full magnitude
until \(8\,\mathrm{s}\), and decreased smoothly to zero by
\(9\,\mathrm{s}\). The equivalent disturbance torque was bounded by

\[
\lVert\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\rVert_2\leq3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\]

\Added{The controller generates a repeatable joint-torque excitation by mapping
the commanded point force through \mbox{\(J_p^\top\)}. Its Cartesian component
is opposed by the pose-hold impedance, and
\mbox{\Cref{eq:nullspace_cartesian_retention_criterion}} evaluates the resulting
position retention.}

In every trial the selected null-space law was active from the start of the
pose-hold state. The first \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) formed a
pre-disturbance settling interval, during which the conditioning torque of the
singular-value-conditioning settings could already move the redundant configuration. The
disturbance then acted from \(5\) to \(9\,\mathrm{s}\), followed by an
observation interval until the end of the trial. Each selected secondary term
remained active from state entry through the complete trial. The
singular-value-conditioning torque acted before, during, and after the
disturbance interval.


\subsection{Evaluation Quantities}

All quantities in this subsection are evaluated over the interval in which
the virtual disturbance acts, \(t\in[5,9]\,\mathrm{s}\). The null-space
controller is intended to modify the redundant joint configuration while
preserving the Cartesian pose. The Cartesian position error therefore verifies
that this primary task remains effectively unchanged during the disturbance. A
deviation below \(2\,\mathrm{mm}\) is treated as acceptable position
retention, so a trial satisfies the criterion when

\begin{equation}
\max_{5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t\leq9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert e_p(t)\rVert_2
<2\,\mathrm{mm},
\label{eq:nullspace_cartesian_retention_criterion}
\end{equation}

where \(e_p(t)\) is the Cartesian position-error vector.

The redundant joint motion is evaluated from the projected joint velocity
defined in \Cref{eq:nullspace_velocity_projector}. The vector
\(\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}\in\mathbb{R}^7\) has units of
\(\mathrm{rad/s}\) and represents the component of the measured joint
velocity that lies in the instantaneous kinematic null space.

Two complementary quantities are formed from this projected velocity. The
first is the cumulative projected null-space motion

\begin{equation}
E_N
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}(t)\rVert_2\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_excursion_metric}
\end{equation}

Taking the norm before integration makes motion in opposite directions
accumulate. \(E_N\) measures the total redundant joint motion and is
particularly relevant to the damping comparison. Its unit is radians, and the
values are expressed in degrees.

The second quantity measures the net displacement, in which motion in
opposite directions cancels. The vector integral of the projected velocity
gives the accumulated projected joint displacement

\begin{equation}
\Delta q_{\mathrm{null}}
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
N_q(q(t))\dot q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_net_joint_displacement}
\end{equation}

The vector \(\Delta q_{\mathrm{null}}\in\mathbb{R}^7\) has units of radians.
It describes the net projected joint motion over the disturbance interval. At
full rank, the Panda has a one-dimensional instantaneous null space.

To express this seven-dimensional net displacement as one scalar component, a
common direction is introduced. Let \(\Delta q_{\mathrm{null},0}\) denote
the arithmetic mean of the three net projected displacement vectors measured in
the \(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}=0\) condition. The corresponding unit direction is

\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{ref}}
=
\frac{\Delta q_{\mathrm{null},0}}
{\lVert\Delta q_{\mathrm{null},0}\rVert_2}.
\label{eq:nullspace_reference_direction_metric}
\end{equation}

The vector \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}\) represents the redundant joint-space direction in
which the commanded virtual disturbance moves the robot in the reference
condition \(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}=0\). The same direction is used
for all tested null-space settings. Their net displacement along this
common direction is

\begin{equation}
\Delta\eta
=
v_{\mathrm{ref}}^\top\Delta q_{\mathrm{null}}.
\label{eq:nullspace_net_displacement_metric}
\end{equation}

A positive \(\Delta\eta\) denotes net motion along the reference
disturbance direction; a negative value denotes motion against it. Values near
zero indicate little remaining net displacement along that direction.
Projection onto \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}\) gives a common directional comparison across the four
tested settings. The unit of \(\Delta\eta\) is radians, and the values are
expressed in degrees, as for \(E_N\).

The singular-value change evaluates how the conditioning indicator
\(\sigma_{\min}\) moves during the virtual disturbance alone. The selected
null-space law was active from the start of every pose-hold trial. Taken on its
own, \(\sigma_{\min}(9\,\mathrm{s})\) would therefore also carry the
configuration reached during the preceding settling interval. Referencing it to
the value at the start of the disturbance gives the change over that interval,

\begin{equation}
\Delta\sigma_{\min}
=
\sigma_{\min}(9\,\mathrm{s})-\sigma_{\min}(5\,\mathrm{s}).
\label{eq:nullspace_singular_value_change_metric}
\end{equation}

A positive \(\Delta\sigma_{\min}\) indicates that \(\sigma_{\min}\)
increased over the disturbance interval, and a negative value that it
decreased. Under the fixed Jacobian convention, an increase corresponds to
motion towards a better-conditioned configuration and a decrease to motion
towards a less well-conditioned one.
