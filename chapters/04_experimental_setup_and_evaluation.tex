\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\section{Experimental Investigation}
\label{sec:experimental_question}

The experimental study evaluates the influence of the commanded angular
tilt, the rotational stiffness about each surface tangent, the
translational stiffness perpendicular to the tilted tangent, and the virtual
centre-of-compliance placement on the contact-induced alignment of the tool
with the workpiece surface. The point-shifted Cartesian impedance described in
the preceding chapters couples translational contact forces to rotational
motion, so the rotational compliance, the in-plane translational compliance and
the spatial position of the compliance centre are all relevant to the resulting
alignment behaviour.

Two properties distinguish these measurements from the earlier campaign
preserved in \cref{app:previous_campaign}. The workpiece plane and the
grinding-face axis are calibrated independently of one another, so the
commanded tilt is defined relative to a measured physical surface
frame rather than to a configured plane that differed from it. The rotational
stiffness is also varied on one tangent at a time rather than on both together,
which separates the \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) contributions that the paired sweep
could not distinguish.

The plane is fitted from three probed points, with a fourth held-out point
lying \(0.82\,\mathrm{mm}\) from the fitted surface. The grinding-face normal is
calibrated from a separate set of seatings, with a held-out angular residual of
\(0.50^\circ\). The projected direction from the first to the second probed
point defines \(+t_1\), and \(t_2=n_s\times t_1\).

All conditions share one baseline impedance in the surface frame,
\[
  K_p=\operatorname{diag}(2000,2000,800)\,\mathrm{N/m},
  \qquad
  K_R=\operatorname{diag}(5,5,50)\,\mathrm{Nm/rad},
\]
a \(60\,\mathrm{mm}\) virtual press at \(50\,\mathrm{mm/s}\), and a
\(5\,\mathrm{s}\) set-up interval. Null-space damping is enabled without the
active singular-value bias. The commanded spin of the grinding face about its
own axis is fixed at \(115.05^\circ\) from \(t_1\), so the leading contact
feature is determined by the calibrated surface frame rather than by the
initial joint configuration.

The measurements are organised as five experimental cases. Case A establishes
the response to the commanded tilt itself and provides the baseline against
which the remaining cases are read. Cases B and C vary one stiffness entry at a
time, Case D varies only the compliance-centre lever, and Case E repeats the
Case-A tilt about the axes of the tool face instead of the surface
tangents.

Cases A--E contain \(75\) contact runs. Every run completed its set-up phase
and is included in the analysis. The null-space records are not reported as a
further case because no matched conditioning-torque comparison was performed.

The robot first orients the grinding-face axis, descends to a geometrically
defined clearance, presses the selected leading tool feature into the plane
during set-up, and then executes the grinding motion. Set-up holds the
clearance orientation as a soft reference and commands no time-varying
task-space tipping trajectory. The response is evaluated from the EE-inferred
tool-axis angle, the rotation during contact, and the feature and TCP
displacements.
\section{Robot and Software Setup}
\label{sec:robot_software_setup_contact}

The experiment is performed on the Franka Emika Panda using the torque-control
interface provided by libfranka. The controller runs inside the Franka Control
Interface with the nominal control period

\begin{equation}
T_s = 1\,\mathrm{ms}.
\label{eq:sampling_time_contact}
\end{equation}

At each control cycle, the current robot state is read, the Cartesian
impedance control law is evaluated, and the resulting joint torque command is
sent back to the robot. The controller uses the end-effector transformation
\(T_{\mathrm{EE}}\), the joint velocity vector \(\dot{q}\), the geometric
Jacobian \(J(q)\), and the model-based Coriolis term provided by the libfranka
model interface. The commanded torque has the form

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{cmd}}
=
J^\top(q)\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{cmd}}
+
\tau_{\mathrm{null}}
+
\tau_c(q,\dot{q}),
\label{eq:chapter4_contact_commanded_torque}
\end{equation}

where \(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{cmd}}\) is the commanded Cartesian impedance
wrench and \(\tau_{\mathrm{null}}\) is the projected null-space torque. The
gravity compensation is not added as a separate term in the user callback,
because the external torque command sent through libfranka is already handled
with robot-side gravity and motor-friction compensation by the Franka Control
Interface.

The active sequence consists of
\texttt{approach\_orient}, \texttt{approach\_descend}, \texttt{set\_up}, and
\texttt{grind}. Hold and manual-guidance modes are available at start-up but are
not additional contact phases. The null-space mode is selected independently
and, when enabled, remains active throughout the automatic sequence.

\subsection{Configured and Physical Plane}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

The controller uses the configured plane point
\(p_s=[\,0.55,\,-0.06,\,0.025\,]^\top\,\mathrm{m}\) and the configured normal
\(n_{\mathrm{cfg}}\equiv n_s\), obtained from \(a=0^\circ\) and
\(b=5^\circ\) in \cref{eq:implemented_surface_normal_angles}. This plane
determines the approach, clearance, feature projection, surface frame and
commanded tool orientation.

The physical workpiece orientation used for the offline alignment metric was
estimated separately by seating the tool face against the surface and reading
back the robot pose. Three repetitions gave
\(a_{\mathrm{phys}}=-0.72\pm0.31^\circ\) and
\(b_{\mathrm{phys}}=14.99\pm1.91^\circ\). The corresponding normal is denoted
\(n_{\mathrm{phys}}\). The angular separation between \(n_{\mathrm{cfg}}\)
and \(n_{\mathrm{phys}}\) is \(10.01^\circ\), providing the initial geometric
misalignment for the reported alignment study. The repeatability of the
hand-seating procedure limits the certainty of absolute residual angles;
changes measured within a run are less sensitive to this offset.

\section{Contact Geometry}
\label{sec:contact_geometry}

The task geometry is defined by a surface plane, a tool edge, and a selected
local linearized centre of compliance. Let

\begin{equation}
n_s \in \mathbb{R}^3,
\qquad
\|n_s\| = 1
\label{eq:surface_normal}
\end{equation}

be the surface normal expressed in the robot base frame. In the implemented
configuration it is generated from rotations \(a\) about base \(x\) and \(b\)
about base \(y\),

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
R_y(b)R_x(a)e_z
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin(b)\cos(a) & -\sin(a) & \cos(b)\cos(a)
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\label{eq:implemented_surface_normal_angles}
\end{equation}

The current values are \(a=0^\circ\) and \(b=5^\circ\), and the plane passes
through \(p_s=[\,0.55,\,-0.06,\,0.025\,]^\top\,\mathrm{m}\). Two orthonormal
tangent directions \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) span the surface plane. The first tangent
is obtained by projecting the configured auxiliary direction into the plane,
and \(t_2=n_s\times t_1\). The implemented column order is therefore

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{surface}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_1 & t_2 & n_s
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:surface_task_frame}
\end{equation}

consistent with the surface task frame of
\cref{eq:surface_task_frame_matrix}. Consequently, every surface-frame vector
or diagonal gain in this chapter follows the order
\([\,t_1,t_2,n_s\,]\), unless a table explicitly states another semantic order.
For the active auxiliary direction \(a_s=e_x\) and \(a=0^\circ\),
\(b=5^\circ\), these axes are
\[
t_1=[\,\cos 5^\circ,\ 0,\ -\sin 5^\circ\,]^\top,
\qquad
t_2=[\,0,\ 1,\ 0\,]^\top .
\]
Thus, rotation about \(t_2\) is rotation about base \(y\), whereas rotation
about \(t_1\) is tipping about the orthogonal tangent in the tilted base
\(x\)-\(z\) plane.

The grinding face is represented explicitly rather than by one fixed point. Its
centre is \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the end-effector
origin, with total dimensions \(40\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and
\(120\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). The controller transforms its
four corners to the base frame and selects the leading corner, edge, or complete
face along the descent direction. Features whose projected heights differ by no
more than \(0.1\,\mathrm{mm}\) are averaged. This prevents an exact single-axis
tilt from arbitrarily choosing one endpoint of a physical edge.

Let \(a_{\mathrm{tool,EE}}\) denote the configured physical face-normal axis.
For the current tool \(a_{\mathrm{tool,EE}}=e_z\), and the orientation target
satisfies

\begin{equation}
R_d a_{\mathrm{tool,EE}}=-n_s .
\label{eq:implemented_tool_axis_target}
\end{equation}

The minus sign points the \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) grinding face toward the surface.
The controller applies the minimum rotation that maps the starting nominal
tool axis to \(-n_s\), thereby preserving the remaining twist as closely as
possible; it does not set \(R_d=R_{\mathrm{surface}}\).

The parallel-jaw grasp does not make the tool-to-EE transform perfectly
torsionally rigid. Manual inspection showed approximately
\(\pm2^\circ\) of relative tool rotation about \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). For the
tested mounting pose this direction lies mainly in the surface-tangent plane
and is informally associated with the \(t_2\) response. It is nevertheless
written as \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\) here: \(t_2\) is defined in the base frame by
the calibrated plane, whereas
\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}e_{y,\mathrm{EE}}\) changes with the EE
orientation, so the two axes must not be identified without an explicit
frame transformation.

The selected centre of compliance is represented by a point \(p_c\), with the
lever \(r_c=p_{\mathrm{TCP}}-p_c\) defined in
\cref{eq:pole_to_tcp_lever}. Here \(p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\) is the origin of the end-effector pose reported by
the robot; any configured flange-to-end-effector transform must therefore be
included when relating this point to the physical hand and tool.
The lower-case subscript \(c\) is reserved for the chosen virtual centre
\(p_c\). The upper-case subscript \(C\) is used later for the physical contact
point \(p_C\), for which the distinct contact lever is
\(r_C=p_C-p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\). These two lever arms have different directions
and must not be interchanged.

If a force \(\mathbf{f}\) acts at the contact, the corresponding moment about
the TCP depends on \(r_C\). Therefore, the selected stiffness
matrix should not be interpreted only as three independent translational
springs and three independent rotational springs. For a virtual-centre
behaviour, translation and rotation must be selected consistently.

\section{Gain Selection Methodology}
\label{sec:cartesian_stiffness_selection}

This chapter keeps the notation of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}. A subscript
\(\mathrm{task}\) means that a gain is written in a local task frame; for a
surface-frame quantity the axis order is \([t_1,t_2,n_s]\). The implementation
does not, however, use one common frame for every phase and every gain block:

\begin{itemize}
    \item approach translation and rotation are diagonal in the surface frame
    and transformed to the base frame;
    \item set-up and grind translation are diagonal directly in base
    \([x,y,z]\), while their rotational gains are diagonal in
    \([t_1,t_2,n_s]\);
    \item hold gains are isotropic in the base frame; the enabled pre-set-up
    gate overrides only translation with an isotropic base-frame gain and
    retains the isotropic approach rotational gain; and
    \item during set-up only, the selected base-frame translational and
    rotational blocks can be shifted from a commanded centre of compliance to the TCP, producing
    a coupled \(6\times6\) gain.
\end{itemize}

The phase-complete stiffness map is given in
\cref{tab:active_stiffness_design_points}; the matching automatic-damping
policy is given in \cref{tab:active_automatic_damping_policy}. These tables
include hold, both approach states, the pre-set-up gate, set-up, grind, and
manual guidance. Frame labels are part of every gain value and must be retained
when a value is calculated or changed.

The connection to the controller notation is the following. The controller law
of \cref{eq:cartesian_impedance_control_law} uses base-frame errors and
base-frame gains. For the experimental interpretation, the same quantities can
be written in the surface task frame as

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{task}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{e}_{p,\mathrm{task}}\\
\mathbf{e}_{R,\mathrm{task}}
\end{bmatrix}
=
T_R^\top
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{e}_p\\
\mathbf{e}_R
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\dot{x}_{\mathrm{task}}
=
T_R^\top
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{\mathrm{EE}}\\
\omega_{\mathrm{EE}}
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:experiment_task_frame_errors}
\end{equation}

where \(T_R=\operatorname{diag}(R_{\mathrm{surface}},R_{\mathrm{surface}})\).
This representation is used when both blocks are being interpreted in the
surface frame. It must not be used to imply that the implemented set-up
translation block is surface-frame diagonal; that block is already defined in
base XYZ.

The complete local Cartesian stiffness matrix uses the same task-frame notation
introduced in \cref{eq:full_task_frame_gain_blocks}. In the standard
spatial-stiffness block form \citep{Fasse1997SpatialImpedance,Ott2008},

\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{task}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
K_{p,\mathrm{task}} & K_{pR,\mathrm{task}}\\
K_{Rp,\mathrm{task}} & K_{R,\mathrm{task}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:contact_stiffness_block}
\end{equation}

Here, \(K_{p,\mathrm{task}}\) relates translational displacement to force,
\(K_{R,\mathrm{task}}\) relates rotational displacement to moment, and the
off-diagonal blocks \(K_{pR,\mathrm{task}}\) and \(K_{Rp,\mathrm{task}}\)
describe translation--rotation coupling.
This block form is useful for explaining the physical coupling between sliding
and rotation during edge contact. The implemented controller constructs the full
matrix analytically from a manually selected centre of compliance during set-up. Its
location and the source stiffness blocks are the design variables; the 36 matrix entries are
not tuned independently.

The gain construction uses a symmetric positive-definite stiffness and a
symmetric positive-semidefinite damping matrix,

\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{task}} = K_{\mathrm{task}}^\top,
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{task}} \succ 0.
\label{eq:contact_stiffness_spd}
\end{equation}

The congruence preserves these properties. They provide direct
checks on the assembled \(6\times6\) matrices without introducing additional
experiment-specific tuning variables.

\subsection{Quasi-Static Stiffness Sizing and Automatic Damping}
\label{subsec:initial_stiffness_damping_values}

The quasi-static calculation is an offline method for sizing and tracing the
stiffness values. It is not executed by the controller and it is not a contact
force estimator. At low velocity, the elastic parts of the implemented command
are considered separately from damping and inertia,

\begin{equation}
\mathbf f_K=K_p\mathbf e_p,
\qquad
\mathbf m_K=K_R\mathbf e_R .
\label{eq:quasi_static_translational_stiffness}
\end{equation}

For a principal coordinate \(i\), a declared elastic force--error design pair
\((F_i^\star,\Delta x_i^\star)\), or moment--angle pair
\((M_i^\star,\Delta\theta_i^\star)\), gives

\begin{align}
k_{p,i}
&=
\frac{F_i^\star}{\Delta x_i^\star},
\label{eq:initial_translational_stiffness}\\
k_{R,i}
&=
\frac{M_i^\star}{\Delta\theta_i^\star}.
\label{eq:initial_rotational_stiffness}
\end{align}

The starred quantities are engineering design scales. They are not measured
contact loads, certified maxima, or software limits. This distinction prevents
a chosen virtual-spring load from being reported as an observed environment
force. If a moment scale is estimated from an offset force, its component about
the selected unit axis \(a_i\) is
\[
M_i^\star
=
\left|a_i^\top(r_C\times f_C)\right|
\leq
\lVert r_C\rVert\lVert f_C\rVert ,
\]
rather than the scalar lever-arm bound being used without its axis direction
\citep{Murray1994,Siciliano2009}.

\paragraph{Frame-consistent directional stiffness.}
The scalar rule in \cref{eq:initial_translational_stiffness} applies directly
only in a principal-gain frame. If a gain is diagonal in frame \(R_F\), its
base-frame matrix and its effective slope along a base-frame unit direction
\(u\) are

\begin{equation}
K_{p,0}
=
R_F\operatorname{diag}(k_{p,1},k_{p,2},k_{p,3})R_F^\top,
\qquad
k_{p,\mathrm{eff}}(u)
=
u^\top K_{p,0}u
=
\sum_{i=1}^{3}k_{p,i}(r_i^\top u)^2 .
\label{eq:directional_effective_stiffness}
\end{equation}

For an error \(\mathbf e_p=\delta u\), the elastic component along \(u\) is
\(\delta k_{p,\mathrm{eff}}(u)\). A non-principal direction can also produce
the transverse component
\(\delta(I-uu^\top)K_{p,0}u\). The same construction applies to
\(K_{R,0}\) and a rotational direction. This calculation is essential for the
active set-up translation, which is diagonal in base XYZ while the push follows
the tilted surface normal.

\paragraph{Set-up/grind calculation.}
The active \(t_2\) direction equals base \(y\). Taking the completely blocked
\(30\,\mathrm{mm}\) grind stroke as a \(60\,\mathrm{N}\) elastic design scale
gives

\begin{equation}
k_y=\frac{60}{0.030}=2000\,\mathrm{N/m},
\qquad
k_x=k_y .
\label{eq:setup_lateral_stiffness_design}
\end{equation}

For the press direction, choose a nominal \(65\,\mathrm{N}\) elastic scale at
the configured \(0.180\,\mathrm{m}\) endpoint, so that
\(k_{n,\mathrm{eff}}^\star=65/0.180=361.11\,\mathrm{N/m}\).
The coordinate starts near \(-0.020\,\mathrm{m}\) at the clearance hand-off
and ends at \(+0.180\,\mathrm{m}\). The complete scheduled reference travel is
therefore approximately \(0.200\,\mathrm{m}\), which takes \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) at
\(0.050\,\mathrm{m/s}\) and deliberately matches the set-up timeout. The first
\(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) closes the geometric clearance; the \(180\,\mathrm{mm}\)
endpoint is the virtual post-plane spring coordinate used for the blocked-load
design scale. It is neither a commanded material penetration nor an expected
robot displacement.
With \(a=0^\circ\), \(b=5^\circ\), and
\(K_{p,0}=\operatorname{diag}(k_x,k_y,k_z)\), solving
\[
k_{n,\mathrm{eff}}^\star
=
k_x\sin^2 b+k_z\cos^2 b
\]
gives

\begin{equation}
k_z
=
\frac{k_{n,\mathrm{eff}}^\star-k_x\sin^2(5^\circ)}
     {\cos^2(5^\circ)}
=
348.6\,\mathrm{N/m}
\;\longrightarrow\;
350\,\mathrm{N/m}.
\label{eq:setup_base_z_stiffness_design}
\end{equation}

Thus the implemented rounded block is
\(\operatorname{diag}(2000,2000,350)\,\mathrm{N/m}\) in base XYZ. Expressing
this same matrix in the active surface frame gives

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top K_{p,0}R_{\mathrm{surface}}
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
1987.47 & 0 & 143.26\\
0 & 2000 & 0\\
143.26 & 0 & 362.53
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{N/m}.
\label{eq:active_setup_translation_surface_representation}
\end{equation}

Consequently, a completely blocked \(0.180\,\mathrm{m}\) normal reference
change would correspond to approximately \(65.3\,\mathrm{N}\) along the normal,
\(25.8\,\mathrm{N}\) along \(t_1\), and \(70.2\,\mathrm{N}\) total elastic-force
magnitude. These are conservative virtual-spring scales for a hypothetical
full tracking error. The robot normally follows part of the reference, so they
are neither predicted measured forces nor physical penetration values.

For the rotational block, a \(15\,\mathrm{N}\) design force through the
\(60\,\mathrm{mm}\) tool half-length gives a moment scale of approximately
\(0.90\,\mathrm{Nm}\). Allowing about \(10^\circ\) about the two surface
tangents but only \(1^\circ\) about the surface normal and converting the
angles to radians gives

\begin{equation}
k_{R,t_1}=k_{R,t_2}
\approx\frac{0.90}{0.1745}=5.16
\;\longrightarrow\;5,
\qquad
k_{R,n}
\approx\frac{0.90}{0.01745}=51.6
\;\longrightarrow\;50
\quad\mathrm{Nm/rad}.
\label{eq:setup_rotational_stiffness_design}
\end{equation}

The ten-to-one ratio makes tipping about the tangents compliant while retaining
stronger twist restraint. During set-up these are source gains at the selected
centre of compliance; after the congruence shift they are not, in general, the diagonal
rotational slopes seen for a pure rotation at the TCP. Grind uses the same
source blocks without the centre of compliance shift.

\paragraph{Automatic damping.}
Stiffness is selected offline, whereas Cartesian damping is calculated by the
controller at entry to each phase group. Starting from the joint-space mass
matrix and Jacobian, the implementation forms

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_0&=JM(q)^{-1}J^\top,\\
\Lambda_0&=\operatorname{sym}\!\left[
\left(\operatorname{sym}(\mathcal H_0)+10^{-9}I_6\right)^{-1}
\right],\\
\Lambda_F&=\operatorname{sym}\!\left(T_F^\top\Lambda_0T_F\right),
\qquad
\operatorname{sym}(A)=\tfrac12(A+A^\top).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:implemented_cartesian_inertia}
\end{equation}

Only the positive diagonal entries of \(\Lambda_F\) are retained. For each
principal stiffness \(k_i\), the coefficient is

\begin{equation}
d_i
=
\min\!\left(
d_{\max},
\max\!\left(d_{i,\min},\,2\zeta\sqrt{\Lambda_{F,ii}k_i}\right)
\right).
\label{eq:directional_damping_heuristic}
\end{equation}

Here \(d_{\max}=8000\). The active configuration does not take the manual
coefficients as lower bounds, hence \(d_{i,\min}=0\); configured
manual coefficients are fallbacks, not lower bounds. For a fitted phase, let
\(c_i=2\sqrt{\Lambda_{F,ii}k_i}\). The least-squares factor used to approach a
target vector \(d^\star\) is

\begin{equation}
\beta^\star
=
\frac{c^\top d^\star}{c^\top c}.
\label{eq:fitted_automatic_damping_factor}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pole-Based Gain Design}
\label{subsec:impedance_around_pole}

The compliance-centre design is the construction used during \texttt{set\_up}. A
surface-frame rotation changes the principal directions of the translational
and rotational blocks but leaves the translation--rotation blocks zero. It
therefore does not move the centre of compliance away from the TCP. The
point-shift construction of
\cref{eq:offset_adjoint,eq:coupled_tcp_stiffness,eq:coupled_tcp_damping}
creates the required translation--rotation coupling. The complete sign and
block-matrix derivation is kept in
\cref{sec:coupled_pole_stiffness}; it is not repeated here.

The active source stiffness, written wholly in base-frame coordinates, is

\begin{equation}
K_{c,0}
=
\operatorname{blkdiag}\!\left(
\operatorname{diag}(2000,2000,350),
R_{\mathrm{surface}}\operatorname{diag}(5,5,50)
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top
\right).
\label{eq:active_mixed_frame_pole_stiffness}
\end{equation}

The entries of the translational and rotational blocks have units
\(\mathrm{N/m}\) and \(\mathrm{Nm/rad}\), respectively. Equation
\eqref{eq:active_mixed_frame_pole_stiffness} makes the mixed-frame construction
explicit: translation is diagonal in base XYZ, whereas only rotation is
diagonal in surface coordinates \([t_1,t_2,n_s]\).

This construction changes the gain matrices without adding a separate force,
moment, or time-varying task-space rotation command. Its experimental effect is
judged from the EE-inferred tool-axis alignment, selected-feature and TCP
displacements, and commanded joint torques.

\paragraph{Choosing the centre of compliance.}
The implementation accepts an arbitrary base-frame offset from the projected
leading-feature reference captured at clearance:

\begin{equation}
p_c=p_{\Pi,\mathrm{clr}}+\Delta p_c^0,
\qquad
\Delta p_c^0=
\begin{bmatrix}-0.04&0.08&0\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:active_pole_choice}
\end{equation}

The superscript \(0\) emphasizes that the three components are base XYZ, not
\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\). The reference is held constant for the run. A scalar choice
\(p_c=p_s+h n_s\) is a useful theoretical special case, but it is not the active
parameterization. Because the centre of compliance is held constant from first contact, the
lever is constant too,
\[
r_c=p_{\mathrm{TCP,clr}}-p_c,
\]
where \(p_{\mathrm{TCP,clr}}\) is the TCP position captured at the same
clearance hand-off. Each \(\Delta p_c^0\) yields different coupled
\(K_{\mathrm{TCP}},D_{\mathrm{TCP}}\) through
\cref{eq:coupled_tcp_stiffness,eq:coupled_tcp_damping}; the sign and frame of
the vector must therefore
be stored with every trial.

For the configured \(a=0^\circ\), \(b=5^\circ\) surface, resolving the
base-XYZ offset in surface coordinates gives

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top\Delta p_c^0
\simeq
\begin{bmatrix}-39.85&80.00&-3.49\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\mathrm{mm}.
\label{eq:active_pole_offset_surface_components}
\end{equation}

The centre of compliance is therefore placed mainly in the \(-t_1,+t_2\) quadrant relative to
the projected selected feature. The offset is not itself the lever used by the
adjoint: the exact lever also contains the captured TCP-to-feature geometry.
For a purely translational error, define \(f=K_{p,0}e_p\). The moment produced
by the coupling block is the direct sign check

\begin{equation}
m_c=[r_c]_\times^\top f=-r_c\times f.
\label{eq:active_pole_moment_sign_check}
\end{equation}

At the nominal aligned geometry, the transverse lever is approximately
\(0.03985t_1-0.08t_2\). A blocked press with
\(f\approx-Fn_s\), \(F>0\), consequently gives
\(m_c\approx-F(0.08t_1+0.03985t_2)\): the selected quadrant biases negative
rotation about both surface tangents. Reversing either transverse offset
reverses the associated moment bias. This is the geometric rationale for the
chosen direction. It is nevertheless a directional design hypothesis, not a
guarantee of correction: the exact base-diagonal force need not be perfectly
normal, and the bias assists alignment only when its signed components oppose
the signed residual orientation error. That comparison is deferred until the
physical alignment signal is added to the run record.

\subsection{Code-Aligned Selection Order}
\label{sec:role_of_gain_methods}

The calculation and implementation must be applied in the same order as the
controller:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item State a phase-specific force--error or moment--angle design pair and
    calculate the principal source stiffness using
    \cref{eq:initial_translational_stiffness}.
    \item Attach each diagonal to its implemented frame, form the base-frame
    block, and check the commanded path with
    \cref{eq:directional_effective_stiffness}. For set-up, the base-XYZ
    translation and surface-frame rotation blocks remain distinct.
    \item At phase-group entry, calculate and cache the damping from the
    current TCP task inertia using
    \cref{eq:implemented_cartesian_inertia,eq:directional_damping_heuristic}.
    \item During set-up only, shift the selected source stiffness and damping
    through the centre of compliance using
    \cref{eq:coupled_tcp_stiffness,eq:coupled_tcp_damping}. Grind immediately
    returns to the decoupled source blocks.
    \item Check the resulting elastic load and joint-torque scales, then change
    one declared gain family at a time while retaining the frame labels,
    computed damping, compliance-centre offset, and remaining parameters with the run
    record.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting workflow is

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{phase design envelope}
&\rightarrow \text{frame-labelled source }K
\rightarrow \text{task-inertia }D\\
&\rightarrow \text{set-up-only compliance-centre shift}\\
&\rightarrow \text{load/torque check}
\rightarrow \text{controlled trial}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gain_selection_workflow}
\end{equation}

\subsection{One-at-a-Time Gain Adjustment}
\label{sec:direct_stiffness_comparison}

After the initial stiffness values have been selected, a parameter study can
determine how sensitive the response is to each value. Only one stiffness
coordinate is changed in a given study so that its effect is not mixed with
changes in other controller parameters.

Let \(k_{i,\mathrm{base}}\) be the value currently used by the controller for
coordinate \(i\), as listed in
\cref{tab:active_stiffness_design_points}. Candidate values for a local study
are calculated as

\begin{equation}
k_{i,\mathrm{test}}=\alpha k_{i,\mathrm{base}},
\qquad
\alpha\in\{0.5,\ 0.75,\ 1.0,\ 1.25,\ 1.5\},
\label{eq:active_gain_scale_sweep}
\end{equation}

where \(\alpha=1\) reproduces the current controller value. For example, the
two set-up tipping stiffnesses are
\(K_{R,t_1}=K_{R,t_2}=5\,\mathrm{Nm/rad}\). If their equality is retained,
the common local candidates are \(2.5\), \(3.75\), \(5.0\), \(6.25\), and
\(7.5\,\mathrm{Nm/rad}\) for both entries. This example defines a possible
paired fine-tuning study; it is not the axis-specific comparison reported in
\cref{sec:results_summary_table}.

All other source gains, the centre of compliance offset, phase timing, and null-space
parameters are held constant during such a study. Consequently, any systematic
change in the measured response can be associated with the selected stiffness
coordinate. For the same displacement or angular error, the corresponding
elastic force or moment is multiplied by \(\alpha\).

The damping coefficient does not need to be tuned independently. When the
controller next enters the relevant phase group, it recalculates damping from
the changed stiffness and the task inertia. If the entry pose and the
automatic-damping factor are unchanged, then
\cref{eq:directional_damping_heuristic} gives approximately

\begin{equation}
d_{i,\mathrm{test}}
\approx
\sqrt{\alpha}\,d_{i,\mathrm{base}} .
\label{eq:damping_change_with_stiffness}
\end{equation}

For example, doubling a stiffness increases the corresponding damping
coefficient by approximately \(\sqrt{2}\), not by a factor of two. The
coefficient is cached at phase-group entry and is not recomputed continuously
within that phase.

For broad contact-alignment tuning, the tangent-axis rotational stiffness
controls the tool's resistance to tipping and is varied first. The reported
Case-B study varies \(K_{R,t_1}\) while holding \(K_{R,t_2}\) fixed and then
reverses these roles, because a sweep that moves both entries together cannot
identify their individual effects. The normal
rotational stiffness \(K_{R,n}\) is treated separately because it resists
twist about the surface normal. Translational stiffness can then be studied in
the base \(x\) and \(y\) directions and in the effective surface-normal
direction.

The set-up translational stiffness is diagonal in the base frame, whereas the
surface is tilted. Therefore, a desired effective normal stiffness
\(k_{n,\mathrm{eff}}^\star\) cannot generally be assigned directly to the
base-\(z\) entry. If \(k_x\) and the surface tilt \(b\) remain fixed, the
required base-\(z\) stiffness is
\[
k_z
=
\frac{k_{n,\mathrm{eff}}^\star-k_x\sin^2 b}{\cos^2 b},
\]
which accounts for the surface orientation.

The same one-at-a-time rule applies to the approach, gate, and hold gains using
the design pairs in \cref{tab:active_stiffness_design_points}. Each run records
the damping values printed by the controller because stiffness alone is
insufficient to reconstruct them without the phase-entry pose, Jacobian \(J\),
and joint-space mass matrix \(M(q)\).

\paragraph{Evaluation of candidate gains.}
Each candidate stiffness is compared using the evaluation quantities defined
in \cref{sec:alignment_evaluation}. The primary outcome is the physical-plane
alignment improvement
\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys}}\). Contact-induced rotation, the
physical-plane residual angle, selected-feature and TCP displacement, and the
model-estimated normal load provide the secondary comparison. The reported
\(5/15/50\,\mathrm{Nm/rad}\) study in
\cref{sec:results_summary_table} uses contact-induced rotation,
physical-plane improvement, and estimated normal load; the displacement
signals remain available as run diagnostics. Repeated settings are summarised
by their mean and standard deviation. A change is interpreted as a parameter
effect only when it exceeds the Case-C repeatability scale and is not explained
by an incompatible change in loading or displacement.

\section{Experimental Procedure}
\label{sec:experimental_procedure_contact}

The experimental sequence is executed as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Record the active parameter set. Move the Panda to the configured
    initial joint pose, or use the optional damped manual guidance and recapture
    the selected start pose.
    \item Execute the configured gripper action. In the current configuration
    this is a grasp at \(70\,\mathrm{mm}\) width and \(60\,\mathrm{N}\), rather
    than an
    open command. Seat the tool consistently within its approximately
    \(\pm2^\circ\) rotational play about \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\), and record this
    non-rigid mounting condition with the run metadata.
    \item Construct the known plane and
    \(R_{\mathrm{surface}}=[t_1,t_2,n_s]\), construct the \(40\times120\) mm tool
    face, and set the active base-XYZ compliance-centre offset. Configure the Franka collision
    behaviour before starting torque control.
    \item In \texttt{approach\_orient}, hold the TCP position and rotate the
    physical \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) axis toward \(-n_s\). Continue only after at
    least \(0.5\,\mathrm{s}\) and a tool-axis error no greater than
    \(0.035\,\mathrm{rad}\).
    \item In \texttt{approach\_descend}, move along \(-n_s\) at
    \(0.1\,\mathrm{m/s}\), with a maximum commanded travel of
    \(0.640\,\mathrm{m}\). Select the leading face/edge/corner continuously.
    Transition when the exact most-leading corner height is at most
    \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) above the plane; the averaged tied feature is used for
    the projected reference and can differ by at most the
    \(0.1\,\mathrm{mm}\) tie tolerance. Estimated force is logged and printed
    but does not trigger this transition. The enabled pre-set-up gate holds
    the reached pose until the operator presses Enter.
    \item At this clearance hand-off, freeze the selected tool feature, TCP pose,
    orientation reference, external-wrench baseline, and projected
    leading-feature surface reference. The source variable names contain
    \texttt{first\_contact}, but no force contact has yet been declared.
    \item In \texttt{set\_up}, ramp the signed push coordinate continuously from
    its captured value, approximately \(-0.020\,\mathrm{m}\), toward
    \(+0.180\,\mathrm{m}\) at
    \(0.050\,\mathrm{m/s}\) along \(-n_s\). Apply the current coupled compliance-centre
    \(6\times6\) stiffness and damping. The approximately \(0.200\,\mathrm{m}\)
    coordinate span takes \(4\,\mathrm{s}\), matching the timeout; the final
    \(+0.180\,\mathrm{m}\) coordinate is a virtual post-plane spring reference,
    not a claim of \(180\,\mathrm{mm}\) physical penetration. After
    \(2\,\mathrm{s}\), a \(150\,\mathrm{Nm}\) change in the model-estimated
    external-moment norm may end the phase. Operationally, this is a permissive
    placeholder rather than a calibrated contact criterion; unless such a
    large change occurs, the
    independent \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) timeout governs the transition.
    \item In \texttt{grind}, retain the final set-up preload and command a
    quintic smooth ping-pong sweep of \(0.03\,\mathrm{m}\) amplitude at
    \(0.2\,\mathrm{Hz}\) along \(t_2\). In the callback that satisfies the
    set-up exit condition, the controller switches immediately, without gain
    interpolation, to the decoupled set-up gain blocks; the point-shifted branch
    is no longer active. A wrench and \(J^\top w_{\mathrm{cmd}}\) step is
    therefore possible at this hand-off.
    \item Stop with \texttt{e+Enter}, persist the buffered log, and evaluate the
    EE-inferred tool-axis error, edge/TCP displacement, commanded joint-torque
    trace, and phase timing.
\end{enumerate}

For a controlled comparison, only the declared independent variable---for
example block-diagonal versus point-shifted impedance or the base-XYZ compliance-centre
offset---may change between repetitions. Pool only runs with the same controller
configuration, tool geometry, phase duration, and fixed gains; otherwise report them as
separate configurations. The comparison is not meant to
prove a universal optimal stiffness matrix; it identifies which tested
centre-of-compliance setting gives the most useful behaviour for this tool,
plane, and contact sequence.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}

The tool-axis alignment metric must be distinguished from the controller's
rotation error. Let
\(\widehat a_T=R_{\mathrm{EE}}a_{\mathrm{tool,EE}}^0\) be the grinding-face
axis inferred from the nominal calibrated tool-to-EE transform. If
\(Q_g(t)\) denotes the unmeasured relative rotation of the tool in the
gripper, the actual physical axis is

\begin{equation}
a_T
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}Q_g(t)a_{\mathrm{tool,EE}}^0,
\qquad
\lvert\theta_g(t)\rvert\lesssim2^\circ
\ \text{about }y_{\mathrm{EE}} .
\label{eq:tool_mount_play}
\end{equation}

The controller logs \(\widehat a_T\), not \(a_T\). The following alignment
angles therefore use \(\widehat a_T\) and are EE-inferred quantities. They
coincide with physical tool-face angles only when \(Q_g(t)=I\).
Two signed target axes are required:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathrm{cfg}}&=-n_s, &
a_{\mathrm{phys}}&=-n_{\mathrm{phys}},\\
\theta_{\mathrm{align},j}
&=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(
\operatorname{clip}(\widehat a_T^\top a_j,-1,1)
\right),&
\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{align},j}&=\widehat a_T\times a_j,
\quad j\in\{\mathrm{cfg},\mathrm{phys}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

The minus signs are required by the current tool-axis convention. The
controller logs \(\theta_{\mathrm{align,cfg}}\), the angle to the configured
normal. This
online quantity verifies the implemented command geometry, but it is not the
directly measured physical-tool alignment: \(n_s\) and
\(n_{\mathrm{phys}}\) differ in the recorded campaign, and \(Q_g(t)\) is not
measured.

The primary computed metric is instead the offline EE-inferred angle
\(\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys}}\). Set-up improves alignment when

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys,after}}
<
\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys,before}}.
\label{eq:alignment_error_reduction}
\end{equation}

The corresponding physical-plane improvement is

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys}}
=
\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys,before}}
-
\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys,after}}.
\label{eq:alignment_error_improvement}
\end{equation}

Thus \(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys}}>0\) means that alignment with the
measured plane improved under the rigid-mount assumption. With the observed
gripper play, it describes the combined robot--gripper--tool response and
cannot prove the physical-face rotation independently. The rotation during
contact itself is
described by the base-frame rotation vector of the finite before--after
relative rotation,

\begin{equation}
\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_C
=
\operatorname{Log}\!\left(
R_{\mathrm{after}}R_{\mathrm{before}}^\top
\right)^\vee .
\label{eq:contact_induced_rotation}
\end{equation}

The physical-plane corrective vector
\(\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{align,phys}}=\widehat a_T\times(-n_{\mathrm{phys}})\) points
locally along the rotation that carries the EE-inferred tool axis toward the
measured plane normal. The rotation during contact is favorable when it has a
positive component along this corrective axis,

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{align,phys,before}}^\top
\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_C
>
0,
\label{eq:contact_rotation_condition}
\end{equation}

which is a local sign check rather than a global large-rotation test.

The corresponding displacement of the selected physical feature is

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_C
=
p_{C,\mathrm{after}}-p_{C,\mathrm{before}},
\qquad
s_C=\lVert\Delta p_C\rVert .
\label{eq:contact_feature_displacement}
\end{equation}

The TCP displacement is reported in the same way. These values expose
translation and sliding that an orientation-only metric would miss.

During set-up, the controller instead logs
\(\mathbf{e}_R
=R_{\mathrm{EE}}\operatorname{Log}
(R_{\mathrm{EE}}^\top R_{\mathrm{clr}})^\vee\), expressed in the base frame
after masking, where \(R_{\mathrm{clr}}\) is the orientation frozen at the
clearance hand-off. This control error begins near zero and can
\emph{increase} as the tool tips during contact. It must not be plotted as
\(\theta_{\mathrm{align},j}\) without reconstructing the nominal tool axis.
The general log records the angle of the EE-inferred tool axis to the
configured normal directly. It is used as an online geometry check. The reported alignment outcome is rescored offline
against the separately estimated \(n_{\mathrm{phys}}\), as described in
\cref{sec:results_alignment}; the unmeasured \(Q_g(t)\) remains outside that
rescoring.

\subsection{Metric Use by Experimental Case}
\label{sec:evaluation_criteria_contact}

All five cases use physical-plane alignment improvement as the primary
response. They differ in the independent variable stored with each run and in
the supporting quantities reported alongside the primary outcome.

\noindent\textbf{Primary---physical-plane alignment improvement.}
\(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys}}\), with a positive value denoting
improvement.

\noindent\textbf{Physical-plane residual.}
\(\theta_{\mathrm{align,phys,after}}\), reported as a range when the
physical-plane calibration uncertainty affects its absolute value.

\noindent\textbf{Contact-induced rotation.}
The magnitude and direction of the finite before--after rotation
\(\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_C\).

\noindent\textbf{Contact displacement.}
Selected-feature and TCP displacement during set-up.

\noindent\textbf{Load diagnostic.}
The steady model-estimated normal load, explicitly distinguished from a direct
force measurement.

The residual changes in tip angle, configured-normal angle, and estimated load

\section{Safety Limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

The phase-specific damping is computed from the task inertia using
\cref{eq:implemented_cartesian_inertia,eq:directional_damping_heuristic}. This
directional calculation does not itself establish safe contact; the real
controller remains discrete, model dependent, and coupled to a stiff
environment \citep{Pollayil2023ImpedancePlanning}.

Before the torque callback, the reported configuration calls
\texttt{setCollisionBehavior} with equal joint-side acceleration and nominal
thresholds of \(140\,\mathrm{Nm}\), and equal Cartesian force/moment thresholds
of \(240\,\mathrm{N}\) or \(\mathrm{Nm}\) for all six components. These are
Franka reflex thresholds, not commanded-torque or wrench saturations. The user
callback implements no separate Cartesian-force clipping, moment clipping,
joint-torque clipping, or torque-rate limiter. The values therefore document
the tested robot configuration; they are not a general human-safety claim.

Additional procedural and geometric guards are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item a \(0.640\,\mathrm{m}\) maximum approach travel and a geometric
    \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) clearance hand-off;
    \item an operator gate before set-up, using a
    \(5000\,\mathrm{N/m}\) isotropic position hold with automatically fitted
    damping;
    \item a \(4\,\mathrm{s}\) set-up timeout independent of the permissive
    \(150\,\mathrm{Nm}\) external-moment-change stopping threshold; the timeout
    is expected to govern unless the estimated moment changes exceptionally;
    \item the configured set-up stiffness and push must be checked against the
    collision settings before a run; the scalar product
    \(350\,\mathrm{N/m}\times0.180\,\mathrm{m}\approx63\,\mathrm{N}\) is only a
    base-\(z\) approximation, while the frame-consistent blocked-reference
    scales are \(65.3\,\mathrm{N}\) normal and \(70.2\,\mathrm{N}\) in total
    from \cref{eq:active_setup_translation_surface_representation}; none is a
    contact-force measurement; and
    \item after a normally completed sequence run, the general CSV is inspected
    for phase transitions, commanded torque, displacement, and oscillation.
\end{itemize}

Franka reflex/contact flags are not columns of the 112-column general sequence
log. They are available only in the compact sigma diagnostic during eligible
hold tests. Moreover, a sequence-ending reflex exception does not guarantee
that the general CSV is written; only the compact diagnostic buffer is
explicitly persisted on that exception path. Hardware/controller event records
must therefore supplement the CSV when diagnosing an aborted sequence.

These checks are necessary because a stiffness matrix that is geometrically
meaningful can still be unsuitable for real hardware if it produces aggressive
torques or poorly damped contact behaviour. In particular, the
\(150\,\mathrm{Nm}\) set-up threshold is applied to the moment change in the
model-estimated external wrench. This is libfranka's model-based estimate
associated with stiffness frame \(K\), not a separately measured TCP contact
moment. Its
large value avoids treating ordinary model fluctuations as a contact event and
makes the timeout the practical default transition. It is an engineering
placeholder, not a measured safety boundary or a validated contact threshold.
In the reported runs the threshold was not reached, and the set-up phase
therefore ended at the configured timeout.
