\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

This chapter presents the experimental configuration, calibration procedure,
controller parameters, test sequence, and evaluation quantities used to
characterise the contact-induced alignment of the grinding tool. The main test
series consists of surface-contact measurements in which the commanded tilt,
directional Cartesian stiffness, and virtual centre of compliance were varied
under otherwise matched conditions. A separate Cartesian pose-hold study was
used to examine the two null-space torque contributions without surface
contact.


\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental_design}
\label{sec:experimental_question}

The surface-contact measurements were divided into five cases. Case~A
established the response to commanded angular offsets about the two calibrated
surface tangents. Case~B varied the rotational stiffness about the tilted
tangent, while Case~C varied the translational stiffness perpendicular to that
tangent. Case~D changed the position and sign of the virtual-centre-of-
compliance lever. Case~E applied the angular offset about the grinding-face
axes and compared the resulting motion with the corresponding surface-tangent
conditions.

The commanded angular offset defines the orientation difference present at the
beginning of set-up. The rotational stiffness determines the resistance to
rotation about the tilted tangent, whereas the translational stiffness
perpendicular to that tangent affects the translational response coupled to the
alignment motion. In Case~D, shifting the Cartesian impedance from a selected
centre of compliance to the TCP introduces translation--rotation coupling and
therefore changes the moment produced by the commanded press.

The surface plane and the grinding-face direction were calibrated
independently and used to define the surface and tool geometry throughout the
measurements. The rotational stiffness about \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) was varied
separately, while the orthogonal rotational-stiffness entry remained at its
baseline value. This arrangement allowed the response associated with each
surface tangent to be evaluated independently.

Unless stated otherwise for a particular case, the translational stiffness
used during set-up and grinding was

\begin{equation}
K_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
2000 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 2000 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 800
\end{bmatrix}
\,\mathrm{N/m},
\label{eq:experimental_baseline_translation}
\end{equation}

expressed in the surface frame. The corresponding rotational stiffness was

\begin{equation}
K_R
=
\begin{bmatrix}
5 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 5 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 50
\end{bmatrix}
\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad},
\label{eq:experimental_baseline_rotation}
\end{equation}

also in the surface frame. Both matrices use the coordinate order
\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\). The fixed phase, damping, null-space, and trajectory
parameters are specified in \Cref{sec:experimental_controller_parameters}.

The five cases contain \(75\) surface-contact runs distributed across
\(25\) parameter settings. Within each controlled comparison, only the stated
parameter was changed. The remaining gain entries, calibrated surface and tool
geometry, phase sequence, and evaluation procedure were retained.

The principal evaluation quantity was the change in the angle between the
calibrated surface normal and the grinding-face direction calculated from the
measured end-effector orientation. The rotation during set-up, the displacement
of the point or edge of the grinding face used for the set-up motion, the TCP
displacement, and the model-estimated external load were evaluated alongside
this angle. Their definitions and treatment across repeated runs are presented
in \Cref{sec:experimental_evaluation}.

The surface-contact measurements and the Cartesian pose-hold study were treated
as separate data sets. The pose-hold study described in
\Cref{sec:nullspace_hold_study} applies an internally commanded disturbance and
examines projected null-space damping and singular-value conditioning apart
from the surface-contact response.

\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:experimental_system}


\subsection{Robot, Tool, and Surface}
\label{subsec:experimental_robot_tool_surface}

The experiments were performed with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika
Panda operated through the Franka Control Interface. Joint-torque commands were
transmitted through libfranka at the nominal control period

\begin{equation}
T_s=1\,\mathrm{ms},
\label{eq:sampling_time_contact}
\end{equation}

corresponding to a nominal control rate of \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\). The real-time
controller and its phase sequence are described in
\Cref{ch:implementation}.

The rectangular grinding tool was held by the Franka Hand using custom gripper
fingers. Its grinding face measured \(40\,\mathrm{mm}\) along
\(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and \(120\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). The
centre of the grinding face was located \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along
\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the reported end-effector origin. The complete
calibrated tool geometry is presented in
\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}.

The gripper permitted approximately \(\pm2^\circ\) of relative tool rotation
about \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). Consequently, the grinding-face direction used in
the evaluation was calculated from the measured end-effector orientation and
the calibrated grinding-face direction in the end-effector frame. Relative
motion between the tool and the gripper contributes to the measurement
uncertainty.

The surface plate was mounted on a raised base board. All experiments used dry
contact between the grinding face and the plate. The controller included no
explicit environment-friction model. The surface plane and grinding-face
direction were calibrated separately before the experimental campaign, as
described in \Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}.

During approach, the controller determines which point or edge of the
rectangular grinding face lies furthest in the descent direction. This point
or edge is used for the geometric clearance calculation and the subsequent
set-up motion. Its position was calculated from the measured end-effector pose
and the calibrated tool geometry.

\subsection{Software and Real-Time Computer}
\label{subsec:experimental_software_computer}

The controller computer ran Ubuntu~20.04.3 with the
\texttt{5.9.1-rt20} Linux kernel and the \texttt{PREEMPT\_RT} patch. The
controller was compiled using \texttt{g++~9.3}, the C++14 language standard,
and the \texttt{-O2} optimisation level. Eigen~3.3.7 and libfranka~0.7.1 were
used for the matrix calculations and communication with the robot.

The controller operated through the libfranka joint-torque interface. At each
control cycle, the measured robot state and the quantities provided by the
robot model were used to calculate the commanded joint torque. The
implementation of the real-time callback is documented in
\Cref{sec:real_time_control_loop}.

Each contact run followed the implemented sequence of tool orientation,
surface approach, set-up, and grinding. Pose hold and manual guidance were used
only for preparation or separate experiments. The combined null-space mode
remained active throughout the contact sequence.


\subsection{Recorded Experimental Quantities}
\label{subsec:experimental_recorded_quantities}

The quantities required for the subsequent evaluation were written to the
controller's in-memory logging buffer during each run and exported to a CSV
file after torque control had ended. The recorded quantities included:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the measured end-effector position and orientation;
    \item the measured joint position and velocity;
    \item the commanded Cartesian reference and Cartesian error;
    \item the position of the point or edge of the grinding face used for the
          set-up motion;
    \item the active controller phase and null-space mode;
    \item the commanded Cartesian wrench and final joint-torque command; and
    \item the model-estimated external force and moment reported by libfranka.
\end{itemize}

The commanded wrench and the model-estimated external wrench were retained as
separate quantities. The latter was used as a supporting measure of the contact
response and represents the model-based estimate supplied by libfranka rather
than a direct force measurement. The complete logging implementation and CSV
structure are described in \Cref{sec:recorded_signals}.

\section{Surface and Tool Calibration}
\label{sec:surface_tool_calibration}
\label{sec:contact_geometry}

The surface plane and the direction normal to the grinding face were
calibrated independently before the contact experiments. The surface
calibration provided the point, normal direction, and tangent directions used
by the controller. The tool calibration provided the grinding-face direction
expressed in the end-effector frame.

The calibrated quantities were written to the controller parameter files and
were subsequently used for approach, clearance calculation, commanded tool
orientation, gain orientation, compliance-centre placement, and evaluation of
the experimental results.


\subsection{Surface-Plane Calibration}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

Three positions distributed over the plate were probed to determine the
surface plane. A fourth position was reserved as an independent geometric
check. At each position, the point on the surface was calculated from the
measured end-effector pose and the calibrated centre of the grinding face.

The plane used by the controller is represented by

\begin{equation}
n_s^\top(p-p_s)=0,
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_plane}
\end{equation}

where \(p_s\) is one point on the plane and \(n_s\) is its unit normal,
expressed in the robot base frame.

The final calibrated plane point stored in
the controller parameters was

\begin{equation}
p_s
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.5153\\
-0.1072\\
0.0031
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_point}
\end{equation}

This point represents the position of the seated grinding-face centre used
during calibration.

The controller stores the surface orientation through rotations \(a\) about
the base \(x\)-axis and \(b\) about the base \(y\)-axis. The corresponding
normal is calculated from

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
R_y(b)R_x(a)e_z
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin(b)\cos(a)\\
-\sin(a)\\
\cos(b)\cos(a)
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:implemented_surface_normal_angles}
\end{equation}

The calibrated angles in the final controller configuration were

\begin{equation}
a=-1.585^\circ,
\qquad
b=+0.988^\circ.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tilt_angles}
\end{equation}

These values give the base-frame surface normal

\begin{equation}
n_s
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
0.017245\\
0.027663\\
0.999469
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_s\|=1.
\label{eq:surface_normal}
\end{equation}

The perpendicular distance of the independently probed fourth point \(p_4\)
from the calibrated plane was calculated as

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
\left|
n_s^\top(p_4-p_s)
\right|.
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_residual}
\end{equation}

The resulting validation residual was

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
0.82\,\mathrm{mm}.
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_value}
\end{equation}

The first surface tangent is taken from the calibration itself. The direction
\(a_s\) from the first to the second probed point is projected into the
calibrated plane and normalised:

\begin{equation}
t_1
=
\frac{
    (I-n_sn_s^\top)a_s
}{
    \left\|
        (I-n_sn_s^\top)a_s
    \right\|
}.
\label{eq:calibrated_first_surface_tangent}
\end{equation}

The tangent therefore follows the probed geometry rather than a base-frame
axis, and the surface frame is rotated in the plane relative to the base
\(x\)- and \(y\)-directions.

The second tangent completes the right-handed surface frame:

\begin{equation}
t_2=n_s\times t_1.
\label{eq:calibrated_second_surface_tangent}
\end{equation}

For the calibrated normal, the resulting tangent directions are approximately

\begin{equation}
t_1
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
-0.905786\\
-0.422854\\
 0.027332
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
t_2
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.423385\\
-0.905776\\
 0.017765
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tangent_values}
\end{equation}

The complete surface frame is therefore

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{surface}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_1 & t_2 & n_s
\end{bmatrix}
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}
-0.905786 & 0.423385 & 0.017245\\
-0.422854 &-0.905776 & 0.027663\\
 0.027332 & 0.017765 & 0.999469
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:surface_task_frame}
\end{equation}

The surface normal lies approximately \(1.9^\circ\) from the base
\(z\)-direction, while the tangents are rotated substantially within the plane
relative to the base \(x\)- and \(y\)-directions.

All surface-frame quantities in the contact campaign use the coordinate order

\begin{equation}
[\,t_1,t_2,n_s\,].
\end{equation}

This order applies to commanded angular offsets, rotational stiffness,
surface-resolved displacement and rotation, and the compliance-centre levers
used in Case~D.


\subsection{Grinding-Face Calibration}
\label{subsec:grinding_face_calibration}

A separate set of seatings was used to establish the relationship between the
end-effector orientation and the direction normal to the grinding face. The
final controller configuration represents this direction by

\begin{equation}
a_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
-0.006011\\
0.000246\\
0.999982
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|a_{\mathrm{EE}}\|=1,
\label{eq:calibrated_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

expressed in the end-effector frame. The direction departs from
\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) by \(0.34^\circ\), which is the residual seating offset
of the tool in the gripper.

An additional seating was reserved as an independent validation measurement.
The angular residual between the grinding-face direction calculated from this
end-effector orientation and the calibrated surface normal was

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tool}}
=
0.50^\circ.
\label{eq:grinding_face_calibration_residual}
\end{equation}

During each controller cycle, the grinding-face direction in the robot base
frame is calculated from the measured end-effector orientation:

\begin{equation}
a_T
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}a_{\mathrm{EE}}.
\label{eq:grinding_face_direction_base}
\end{equation}

The direction \(a_T\) is therefore calculated from the measured end-effector
orientation and the calibrated grinding-face direction. It is not obtained
from an independent orientation sensor on the tool.

The commanded flat orientation turns the grinding face toward the calibrated
surface according to

\begin{equation}
R_d a_{\mathrm{EE}}
=
-n_s.
\label{eq:implemented_tool_axis_target}
\end{equation}

The negative sign follows from the configured value

\begin{equation}
s_a=-1,
\end{equation}

which directs the positive grinding-face axis opposite the outward surface
normal. The remaining rotation about the grinding-face direction was set by a
commanded offset of \(115.05^\circ\) about the surface normal and was held at
that value in every reported run. The offset places \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)
approximately \(25^\circ\) from \(t_2\).

The parallel-jaw mounting permitted approximately
\(\pm2^\circ\) of relative tool rotation about \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\).
This relative motion contributes to the uncertainty of the alignment
calculated from the end-effector orientation.


\subsection{Calibrated Grinding-Face Geometry}
\label{subsec:calibrated_contact_geometry}

The grinding face was represented by a rectangle expressed in the
end-effector frame. Its centre was configured as

\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{face,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.020
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:grinding_face_center_ee}
\end{equation}

The half-width and half-length vectors were

\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{width,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.020\\
0\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{length,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0.060\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:grinding_face_half_dimensions}
\end{equation}

The complete grinding face therefore measured

\begin{equation}
40\,\mathrm{mm}
\times
120\,\mathrm{mm},
\end{equation}

with the width along \(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\), the length along
\(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\), and its centre \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along
\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the reported end-effector origin.

For a point on the grinding face with end-effector-frame offset
\(r_{g,\mathrm{EE}}\), its base-frame position is calculated from

\begin{equation}
p_g
=
p_{\mathrm{EE}}
+
R_{\mathrm{EE}}r_{g,\mathrm{EE}}.
\label{eq:controlled_grinding_face_position}
\end{equation}

The controller transforms all four corners of the grinding face into the robot
base frame and compares their positions along the descent direction. The point
or edge of the grinding face that approaches the surface first is then used
for the geometric clearance calculation and the subsequent set-up motion.

Projected corner heights differing by at most

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_g
=
0.0001\,\mathrm{m}
=
0.1\,\mathrm{mm}
\label{eq:grinding_face_selection_tolerance}
\end{equation}

are treated as belonging to the same edge or face. Their positions are
averaged to form one geometric reference. This prevents a nominal edge
configuration from switching between its two endpoints because of small
numerical differences.

In the following sections, this geometry-based reference is called the point
or edge of the grinding face used for the set-up motion. Its position is used
for the clearance calculation, commanded set-up displacement, and reported
grinding-face displacement.

The point or edge is obtained entirely from the measured end-effector pose,
the calibrated grinding-face geometry, and the descent direction. The
controller therefore uses a geometric surface reference rather than a
separately measured physical contact position. The implementation of the
corner transformation and geometric selection is described in
\Cref{sec:surface_task_plane}.

\section{Controller Parameters Used in the Experiments}
\label{sec:experimental_controller_parameters}
\label{sec:cartesian_stiffness_selection}

The controller parameters were assigned according to the role of each phase.
Approach used a comparatively soft isotropic Cartesian impedance in the
surface frame. The operator gate applied a stiffer translational hold at the
clearance pose. Set-up and grinding used lower stiffness along base \(z\) than
along base \(x\) and \(y\), together with lower rotational stiffness about the
surface tangents than about the surface normal. The coordinate frame therefore
forms part of every gain definition.


\subsection{Fixed Phase and Motion Parameters}
\label{subsec:fixed_phase_motion_parameters}

The phase-transition and trajectory parameters retained during the
surface-contact measurements are listed in
\Cref{tab:experimental_phase_parameters}. The set-up push coordinate starts at
the signed distance captured at the clearance transition, approximately
\(-0.020\,\mathrm{m}\), and moves toward \(+0.060\,\mathrm{m}\). At the
configured speed, the reference reaches this endpoint after approximately
\(1.6\,\mathrm{s}\) and is then held there until the phase ends. The endpoint
is a Cartesian spring reference beyond the calibrated plane and is not
interpreted as physical penetration of the same magnitude.

=
\begin{bmatrix}

\end{bmatrix}
\,\mathrm{N/m},
\qquad

\section{Contact Experiment Procedure}
\label{sec:contact_experimental_procedure}
\label{sec:experimental_procedure_contact}

Before each run, the active parameter set was recorded and the robot was moved
to the configured initial joint posture. Damped manual guidance could be used
to adjust this posture, after which the selected start pose was captured. The
tool was held by the Franka Hand using the configured grasp width of
\(66\,\mathrm{mm}\) and a grasping force of \(60\,\mathrm{N}\). The seating
was repeated in the same manner for every measurement, while the approximately
\(\pm2^\circ\) rotational play about \(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\) remained part of
the physical mounting condition.

The calibrated surface frame and grinding-face geometry were constructed
before torque control began. The active Cartesian gains, damping settings,
null-space parameters, and compliance-centre definition were then loaded, and
the robot-side collision behaviour was configured.

During tool orientation, the TCP position was held while the calibrated
grinding-face direction was rotated toward \(-n_s\), including the commanded
angular offsets of the active case. The controller remained in this phase for
at least \(0.5\,\mathrm{s}\) and continued to surface approach when the
remaining tool-axis error was at most \(0.035\,\mathrm{rad}\).

Surface approach moved the Cartesian reference along the descent direction at
\(0.1\,\mathrm{m/s}\), with a maximum commanded travel of
\(0.640\,\mathrm{m}\). During this motion, the controller continuously
calculated the point or edge of the rectangular grinding face that approached
the surface first. The geometric transition was reached when the leading
corner height was at most \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) above the calibrated plane.
Corner heights agreeing within \(0.1\,\mathrm{mm}\) were averaged according
to the geometric selection described in
\Cref{subsec:calibrated_contact_geometry}.

At the clearance transition, the operator gate held the reached pose until the
set-up motion was confirmed. The controller then stored the TCP pose, the
orientation reference, the point or edge of the grinding face used for the
set-up motion, its projection onto the plane, and the model-estimated external
force and moment used as bias values. This transition was defined by the
calibrated geometry; the model-estimated force was recorded for evaluation but
did not determine the transition.

During set-up, the signed press coordinate moved from its captured value,
approximately \(-0.020\,\mathrm{m}\), toward \(+0.060\,\mathrm{m}\) at
\(0.050\,\mathrm{m/s}\) along the descent direction. Cases~A, B, C, and E
used the decoupled impedance, whereas Case~D applied the point-shifted
\(6\times6\) stiffness and damping matrices associated with the selected
centre of compliance. The orientation stored at the clearance transition
remained the rotational reference, so the alignment motion arose from the
contact response rather than from a time-varying tipping command.

After the minimum set-up time of \(2\,\mathrm{s}\), the phase could end when
the norm of the bias-subtracted model-estimated external moment reached
\(150\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The independent \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) timeout provided
the second termination condition. The final signed coordinate is a virtual
Cartesian spring reference and therefore describes the controller command
rather than a measured penetration depth.

The pre-grind gate then held the reached pose until the transition to grinding
was confirmed. After set-up, the controller retained the final press reference
and switched to the decoupled set-up gain matrices. The tangential grinding
sweep was disabled for the reported measurements, so grinding maintained the
press reference without a commanded tangential motion.

The operator ended the run through the terminal interface. The buffered data
were then written to the run-specific CSV file, and the stored parameter set
was retained with the measurement. Between the three repetitions of one
setting, the calibrated geometry, fixed gain entries, phase timing, and
procedure remained unchanged. Only the parameter assigned to the corresponding
case was modified.


\section{Evaluation Metrics and Repeated-Run Treatment}
\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}

\subsection{Alignment Change}
\label{subsec:experimental_alignment_change}

The grinding-face direction used in the evaluation was calculated from the
measured end-effector orientation and the calibrated direction expressed in
the end-effector frame:

\begin{equation}
a_T
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}a_{\mathrm{EE}}.
\label{eq:evaluation_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

Its angle to the calibrated surface was

\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\cos^{-1}\!\left(-a_T^\top n_s\right).
\label{eq:contact_alignment_error_cross}
\end{equation}

The sign convention follows the orientation target
\(R_d a_{\mathrm{EE}}=-n_s\). The angle is calculated from the measured
end-effector orientation and the calibrated tool geometry. Relative rotation
of the tool in the gripper therefore contributes to its uncertainty.

The alignment change during set-up was calculated from

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\theta_{\mathrm{align,before}}
-
\theta_{\mathrm{align,after}},
\label{eq:alignment_error_improvement}
\end{equation}

where the initial value is taken at the clearance transition and the final
value at the end of set-up. Positive values represent a reduction of the
angular difference between the grinding face and the calibrated surface.


\subsection{Rotation, Displacement, and Model-Estimated Load}
\label{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}

The finite rotation during set-up was evaluated from

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}
=
\operatorname{Log}\!\left(
R_{\mathrm{after}}R_{\mathrm{before}}^\top
\right)^\vee.
\label{eq:contact_induced_rotation}
\end{equation}

Its components in the surface frame were obtained from

\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}^{S}
=
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{set}}.
\label{eq:surface_resolved_setup_rotation}
\end{equation}

This representation separates the rotations about \(t_1\), \(t_2\), and
\(n_s\) and permits direct comparison with the commanded tilt direction.

Let \(p_g\) denote the position of the point or edge of the grinding face used
for the set-up motion. Its displacement was

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_g
=
p_{g,\mathrm{after}}-p_{g,\mathrm{before}},
\qquad
s_g
=
\lVert\Delta p_g\rVert.
\label{eq:contact_feature_displacement}
\end{equation}

The TCP displacement was evaluated in the same form:

\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}
=
p_{\mathrm{TCP,after}}-p_{\mathrm{TCP,before}},
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{TCP}}
=
\lVert\Delta p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\rVert.
\label{eq:tcp_displacement_metric}
\end{equation}

The surface-frame components of both displacement vectors were retained to
show whether the angular change was accompanied by normal motion or tangential
sliding.

The supporting load quantity was calculated from the model-estimated external
force reported by libfranka after subtraction of the bias captured at the
clearance transition. Its normal magnitude was

\begin{equation}
F_n
=
\left|
n_s^\top
\left(
\hat f_{\mathrm{ext}}-\hat f_{\mathrm{bias}}
\right)
\right|.
\label{eq:model_estimated_normal_load}
\end{equation}

This quantity represents the robot-model estimate associated with the external
wrench. It was used to compare the load level between matched runs and was not
treated as a direct force measurement.

The controller orientation error \(e_R\) is referenced to the orientation
stored at the clearance transition. It therefore describes deviation from the
held controller reference, while \(\theta_{\mathrm{align}}\) describes the
relationship between the grinding face and the calibrated surface. The latter
was used as the principal alignment quantity.


\subsection{Treatment of Repeated Runs}
\label{subsec:experimental_repeated_run_treatment}
\label{sec:evaluation_criteria_contact}

Each parameter setting was repeated three times. For a measured quantity
\(y\), the three values were summarised by the arithmetic mean

\begin{equation}
\bar y
=
\frac{1}{3}
\sum_{j=1}^{3}y_j
\label{eq:experimental_sample_mean}
\end{equation}

and the sample standard deviation

\begin{equation}
s_y
=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{2}
\sum_{j=1}^{3}
\left(y_j-\bar y\right)^2
}.
\label{eq:experimental_sample_standard_deviation}
\end{equation}

Case~A provides the reference response to commanded tilt. Cases~B and C compare
the alignment change with the corresponding stiffness coordinate, while
retaining the rotation, displacement, and model-estimated load as supporting
quantities. Case~D compares the response with the surface-frame lever
component and its sign. Case~E compares tool-axis and surface-tangent
definitions of the commanded tilt.

The comparison was restricted to settings with matching calibrated geometry,
phase timing, fixed gains, and null-space parameters. Differences were
interpreted together with the within-setting variation and the zero-tilt
reference reported in \Cref{sec:results_repeatability}. No continuous optimum
was estimated from the discrete parameter levels.


\section{Secondary Null-Space Pose-Hold Study}
\label{sec:nullspace_hold_study}
\label{subsec:nullspace_automatic_method}

The two null-space torque contributions were examined separately in Cartesian
pose hold without surface contact. The pose-hold controller retained the
initial TCP pose, while an internally commanded joint torque represented the
effect of a point force applied at a specified robot-link location. This
repeatable software command provided a common disturbance input for all
settings.

The force point was assigned to link~3 at the link-frame offset

\begin{equation}
r_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.200
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_point}
\end{equation}

At the initial posture \(q_0\), the structural null direction \(v_7(q_0)\)
and the translational point Jacobian \(J_p(q_0)\) defined the base-frame force
direction

\begin{equation}
u_f
=
\frac{
J_p(q_0)v_7(q_0)
}{
\lVert J_p(q_0)v_7(q_0)\rVert_2
}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}
\end{equation}

The direction was retained throughout the trial. The controller evaluated the
current point Jacobian and applied

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)
=
J_p(q(t))^\top f_d(t),
\qquad
f_d(t)
=
20s(t)u_f\,\mathrm{N}.
\label{eq:nullspace_automatic_disturbance}
\end{equation}

The scale \(s(t)\) increased from zero to one through a half-cosine between
\(5\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(7\,\mathrm{s}\), remained at one until
\(8\,\mathrm{s}\), and returned to zero during the following second. The
joint-torque-equivalent disturbance was limited to

\begin{equation}
\lVert\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\rVert_2
\leq
3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{equation}

Four null-space settings were tested with three repetitions each. The first
setting disabled the null-space torque. The second used projected damping with
\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\). The remaining settings used
singular-value conditioning with \(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) and
\(k_\sigma=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), respectively. The SVD probe step, sign
deadband, and relative singular-value threshold remained at
\(0.08\,\mathrm{rad}\), \(10^{-6}\), and \(10^{-4}\).

The predefined task-retention limit was a peak Cartesian position error of
\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\). The projected joint motion during the disturbance was
measured by

\begin{equation}
E_N
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert N_q(q)\dot q\rVert_2
\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_excursion_metric}
\end{equation}

The integral was converted to degrees for comparison with the angular
quantities reported elsewhere. Singular-value conditioning was evaluated from
the change in the smallest singular value between the initial and final
states. Cartesian task retention was evaluated from the peak position-error
norm during the pose hold. The acceptance outcome and measured differences
between the four settings are reported in the results chapter.

The disturbance in this study is the torque equivalent of a commanded point
force. It characterises the response to a repeatable controller input and does
not represent a separately measured physical force or an accidental link
contact.


\section{Robot-Side Monitoring and Procedural Limits}
\label{sec:experimental_monitoring_limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

Before torque control started, the robot-side collision behaviour was
configured with equal acceleration and nominal joint-torque thresholds of
\(140\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The six Cartesian force and moment components used
equal thresholds of \(240\,\mathrm{N}\) or
\(240\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), according to the component type. These values define
the conditions for the Franka reflex response.

The joint-torque callback returned the calculated controller command directly
to libfranka. Robot-side monitoring therefore supplied the reflex mechanism,
while the configured values represented collision thresholds rather than
Cartesian-wrench or joint-torque saturation limits. They document the robot
configuration used during the measurements and are not interpreted as a
validated human-safety margin.

The motion sequence added several procedural limits. Surface approach was
bounded to \(0.640\,\mathrm{m}\), and the transition to set-up occurred at a
geometrically defined clearance of \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\). The operator gate held
the reached pose with the translational stiffness of
\Cref{eq:experimental_gate_stiffness} until the set-up motion was confirmed.
Set-up was bounded by a \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) timeout and by the alternative
\(150\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) change in the norm of the model-estimated external
moment after the minimum phase time.

The set-up stiffness and signed press reference also define a useful command
scale. For a completely blocked \(0.060\,\mathrm{m}\) displacement along the
calibrated normal, the elastic force calculated from
\Cref{eq:experimental_setup_translational_stiffness} has a normal component of

\begin{equation}
F_{n,\mathrm{block}}
=
0.060\,
n_s^\top K_{p,\mathrm{set}}n_s
=
48.0\,\mathrm{N},
\label{eq:experimental_blocked_normal_force_scale}
\end{equation}

and, because \(K_{p,\mathrm{set}}\) is diagonal in the surface frame, a total
magnitude of the same value,

\begin{equation}
\left\lVert
K_{p,\mathrm{set}}
\left(0.060n_s\right)
\right\rVert_2
=
48.0\,\mathrm{N}.
\label{eq:experimental_blocked_total_force_scale}
\end{equation}

This value describes the virtual elastic command under a fully blocked
reference. The actual robot motion reduces the tracking error, and the value is
therefore a command-scale bound rather than a measured contact load.

The set-up moment threshold was applied to the change in libfranka's
model-estimated external moment after subtraction of the value stored at the
clearance transition. It served as a controller termination condition rather
than a calibrated contact-force or contact-moment boundary. The timeout,
collision configuration, operator gates, logged torque command, displacement,
and model-estimated wrench were considered together when assessing each run.
