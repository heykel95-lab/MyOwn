\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

This chapter describes how the controller developed in \Cref{ch:implementation}
was evaluated experimentally. The methodology is organised in the order in
which the experiments can be reproduced: the physical system is introduced
first, followed by the surface reference and tool calibration, the common
contact-test
configuration and procedure, the main surface-contact matrix, the quantities
used for evaluation, and the separate Cartesian pose-hold study for the
null-space controller. Supporting checks of the compliance-centre geometry are
reported in \Cref{app:supporting_contact_checks}.

The main data set consists of surface-contact measurements in which the
commanded tool orientation offset, directional Cartesian stiffness, and virtual
\abbr{CoC} were varied under otherwise matched conditions. The primary
response quantity is the signed contact-establishment response. A second,
independent experiment evaluates the null-space terms under a repeatable
internally commanded disturbance.

\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:experimental_system}

\subsection{Robot, Tool, and Surface}
\label{subsec:experimental_robot_tool_surface}

The experiments were performed with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika
Panda operated through the Franka Control Interface. Joint-torque commands were
transmitted through libfranka at the nominal control rate of
\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\), corresponding to a sampling period of
\(1\,\mathrm{ms}\). The real-time
controller, Cartesian impedance law, contact geometry, and state sequence are
described in \Cref{ch:implementation}.

The rectangular grinding tool was held by the Franka Hand using custom gripper
fingers. The tool face measured \(40\times120\,\mathrm{mm}\), with the
width along \(X_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and the length along \(Y_{\mathrm{EE}}\). Its
centre was located \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) along \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) from the
reported end-effector origin. The tool was clamped by custom pads screwed to
the gripper fingers. Mechanical clearance in that clamping allowed the tool to
rotate relative to the gripper by approximately \(\pm2^\circ\) about
\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\), which corresponds approximately to the \(t_2\) direction
in the flat experimental configuration. The robot does not measure this
relative rotation: it is absent from the joint and end-effector state. The
\(\pm2^\circ\) was measured in the unloaded condition, and the instantaneous
relative tool--gripper rotation was not tracked during the contact
experiments. It is therefore a mechanical property of the mounting rather than
a calibrated measurement uncertainty.

Orientation quantities reconstructed from \(R_{\mathrm{EE}}\) and the
calibrated tool normal consequently describe the tool orientation under the
assumption of a fixed tool-to-end-effector transformation, and any motion of
the tool within the gripper is not included in them.

The surface was a plane plate mounted on a raised base board. All reported
measurements used dry contact. The configured surface reference and the tool
normal were determined separately before the measurements, as described in
\Cref{sec:surface_tool_calibration}.

\subsection{Real-Time Computer and Software}
\label{subsec:experimental_software_computer}

The controller computer ran Ubuntu~20.04.3 with the
\texttt{5.9.1-rt20} Linux kernel and the \texttt{PREEMPT\_RT} patch. The
controller was compiled using \texttt{g++~9.3}, C++14, and optimisation level
\texttt{-O2}. Eigen~3.3.7 was used for matrix calculations and
libfranka~0.7.1 for communication with the robot.

Before the measurements, the computer--robot connection was checked with the
\texttt{communication\_test} program supplied with libfranka~0.7.1
\citep{libfrankaCommunicationTest}. The program reports the fraction of
control commands received by the robot and issues its lower
communication-quality warning below an average command-success rate of
\(0.90\). The experimental system passed this criterion. This check verifies
communication quality for operation through the nominal \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\)
interface; it is not a worst-case execution-time or scheduling-jitter
measurement.


\subsection{Operating and Safety Configuration}
\label{subsec:reproducibility_record}
\label{sec:experimental_monitoring_limits}
\label{sec:damping_safety_limits_contact}

Before torque control started, the robot-side collision behaviour was
configured with equal acceleration and nominal joint-torque thresholds of
\(140\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The six Cartesian force and moment channels used
thresholds of \(240\,\mathrm{N}\) or \(240\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), according to the
component type. These thresholds supplied the Franka reflex mechanism and were
kept unchanged throughout the reported measurements.

The complete run-specific controller configuration is given in
\Cref{app:controller_parameters}.

\subsection{Surface-Plane Calibration}
\label{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol}

This calibration determines a fixed plane reference \((p_s,n_s)\) in the robot
base frame. The surface tangents and the surface frame used throughout the
experiments are constructed from that pair.

The complete tool face was seated flat on the plate, and one end-effector pose
\((p_{\mathrm{EE}},R_{\mathrm{EE}})\) was recorded. No motion was commanded
while that pose was taken. At this stage the physical tool normal
\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\) had not yet been calibrated, so the nominal
\(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) axis was used as the seated direction. The recorded
orientation then carries the orientation of the plane,

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
R_{\mathrm{EE}}[\,0,\ 0,\ 1\,]^\top ,
\label{eq:plane_seated_normal}
\end{equation}

with the sign taken to give a positive base-frame \(z\) component, so that
\(n_s\) is the outward normal.

The same seated pose also supplies a point on the plane. The centre of the tool
face sits at the fixed end-effector-frame offset \(r_{\mathrm{face,EE}}\) of
\Cref{eq:grinding_face_center_ee}, so its base-frame position is

\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{cal}}
=
p_{\mathrm{EE}}+R_{\mathrm{EE}}r_{\mathrm{face,EE}}.
\label{eq:plane_seated_face_centre}
\end{equation}

Because the complete face is seated on the plate, that centre lies on the
plane, and \(p_s=p_{\mathrm{cal}}\). The configured reference plane is consequently

\begin{equation}
n_s^\top(p-p_s)=0,
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_plane}
\end{equation}

with \(n_s\) the unit outward surface normal. The plane point stored in the
controller was

\begin{equation}
p_s
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.5153\\
-0.1072\\
0.0031
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_point}
\end{equation}

The controller stores the plane orientation through rotations \(\alpha_s\) about
the base \(x\)-axis and \(\beta_s\) about the base \(y\)-axis. With \(e_{z_0}\)
the base-frame \(z\)-axis,

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
R_y(\beta_s)R_x(\alpha_s)e_{z_0}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin(\beta_s)\cos(\alpha_s)\\
-\sin(\alpha_s)\\
\cos(\beta_s)\cos(\alpha_s)
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:implemented_surface_normal_angles}
\end{equation}

Inverting that relation returns the stored angles from the measured normal,

\begin{equation}
\alpha_s=\arcsin(-n_{s,y}),
\qquad
\beta_s=\operatorname{atan2}(n_{s,x},n_{s,z}).
\label{eq:surface_tilt_from_normal}
\end{equation}

The stored reference angles were

\begin{equation}
\alpha_s=-1.585^\circ,
\qquad
\beta_s=+0.988^\circ,
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tilt_angles}
\end{equation}

which give, to six decimal places,

\begin{equation}
n_s
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.017245\\
0.027663\\
0.999469
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_s\|=1.
\label{eq:surface_normal}
\end{equation}

The surface calibration was completed before the physical tool normal was
identified, so \(n_s\) was calculated with the nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\)
axis and retained unchanged for all reported experiments. The subsequent tool
calibration showed that the physical tool-face normal differs from that axis by
approximately \(1.56^\circ\). Substituting \(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\) for the nominal
axis in \Cref{eq:plane_seated_normal} would therefore rotate the stored surface
normal. Since the surface was not recalibrated after \(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\) had
been determined, the originally stored \(p_s\), \(n_s\) and
\(R_{\mathrm{surface}}\) were used consistently in both the controller and the
evaluation, and the resulting difference is common to the complete data set.

Repeating the measurement while the tool face remains seated gives the
consistency check of the stored plane. The repeat pose supplies a normal
\(n_{\mathrm{cal}}\) through \Cref{eq:plane_seated_normal} and a face centre
\(p_{\mathrm{cal}}\) through \Cref{eq:plane_seated_face_centre}. Comparing them
with the stored values gives

\begin{equation}
\psi_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
\arccos\left(n_{\mathrm{cal}}^\top n_s\right),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}
=
\left|n_s^\top(p_{\mathrm{cal}}-p_s)\right| .
\label{eq:surface_plane_validation_residual}
\end{equation}

Reading the orientation per axis adds \(\Delta\alpha_s\) and
\(\Delta\beta_s\). Both are differences between the tilt angles of
\(n_{\mathrm{cal}}\), returned by \Cref{eq:surface_tilt_from_normal}, and the
stored angles of \Cref{eq:calibrated_surface_tilt_angles}.
\(\psi_{\mathrm{plane}}\), \(\Delta\alpha_s\) and \(\Delta\beta_s\) describe
the orientation, and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{plane}}\) the position of the plane
along its own normal. A single seated point constrains no orientation, so
neither part describes the agreement on its own. \(\psi_{\mathrm{plane}}\)
additionally carries any difference between the axis configured for the repeat
measurement and the nominal axis of \Cref{eq:plane_seated_normal}. An
orientation of the plate measured independently of the tool was not recorded.

The in-plane orientation was fixed by projecting the base-frame \(x\)-axis
\(e_{x_0}\) onto the configured reference plane:

\begin{equation}
t_1
=
\frac{(I-n_sn_s^\top)e_{x_0}}
{\left\|(I-n_sn_s^\top)e_{x_0}\right\|}.
\label{eq:calibrated_first_surface_tangent}
\end{equation}

The second tangent completes the right-handed frame,

\begin{equation}
t_2=n_s\times t_1.
\label{eq:calibrated_second_surface_tangent}
\end{equation}

For the configured reference plane, to six decimal places,

\begin{equation}
t_1
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.999851\\
-0.000477\\
-0.017238
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
t_2
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.000000\\
 0.999618\\
-0.027667
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:calibrated_surface_tangent_values}
\end{equation}

The surface frame used throughout the contact measurements is therefore, to
six decimal places,

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{surface}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_1 & t_2 & n_s
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 0.999851 & 0.000000 & 0.017245\\
-0.000477 & 0.999618 & 0.027663\\
-0.017238 &-0.027667 & 0.999469
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:surface_task_frame}
\end{equation}

All surface-resolved quantities use the coordinate order
\([t_1,t_2,n_s]\).


\subsection{Tool Normal Calibration}
\label{subsec:grinding_face_calibration}

This calibration determines \(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\), the direction normal to the
physical tool face in end-effector coordinates. It is not assumed to coincide
with nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\), because the manufactured tool and its
mounting introduce a small geometric offset.

To identify \(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\), the complete tool face was seated flat on the
same plate at four different yaw orientations. Rotating a flat tool about the
surface normal changes the end-effector orientation, and leaves the physical
direction normal to the seated face unchanged in the base frame. The
calibration therefore searches for the one direction fixed in the end-effector
frame that becomes the same base-frame direction in every flat yaw pose. Only
the recorded orientations enter that search, so no value of the calibrated
plane of \Cref{subsec:plane_calibration_protocol} reaches
\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\).

The three captured rotations were used for the estimate. With
\(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}\) the rotational part of
the captured pose, perfect seating would carry the tool normal onto one
base-frame direction \(\bar n_B\) in all three,

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{cal},1}n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}
=
R_{\mathrm{cal},2}n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}
=
R_{\mathrm{cal},3}n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}
=
\bar n_B.
\label{eq:tool_axis_invariant_direction}
\end{equation}

Seating scatter leaves no direction that satisfies this exactly. The tool
normal was therefore taken as the unit direction that comes closest across
every pair of captures,

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}
=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\lVert n\rVert=1}
\sum_{i<j}
\bigl\lVert(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}-R_{\mathrm{cal},j})n\bigr\rVert^2 ,
\label{eq:tool_axis_least_squares}
\end{equation}

whose minimiser is the eigenvector of
\(\sum_{i<j}(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}-R_{\mathrm{cal},j})^\top
(R_{\mathrm{cal},i}-R_{\mathrm{cal},j})\) belonging to the smallest eigenvalue.
The sign was chosen toward nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\). The final value used in
every reported run was

\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0.026387057\\
-0.006678514\\
0.999629492
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\|n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\|=1.
\label{eq:calibrated_grinding_face_direction}
\end{equation}

It differs from nominal \(+Z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) by approximately \(1.56^\circ\).
The calibration identifies the effective geometric direction used by the
controller and evaluation; it does not determine the physical source of this
offset.

\(n_{\mathrm{Tool,EE}}\) is the output of this calibration, and
\Cref{sec:tool_orientation_given_surface} carries it into the base frame during
operation. Flat contact requires that base-frame direction to coincide with the
inward surface normal \(-n_s\). The commanded spin about the desired
tool axis, measured from \(t_1\), was \(90^\circ\) for all reported
runs. In the flat target orientation the projected long face axis is aligned
with the \(t_2\) line.

\subsection{Repetitions and Data Recording}
\label{subsec:experimental_recorded_quantities}

The recorded signals and \abbr{CSV} structure are documented in
\Cref{sec:recorded_signals} and \Cref{app:recorded_data}.

Three repetitions were recorded for each parameter setting. Results are
reported as arithmetic means, with sample standard deviations given for the
main comparisons and where relevant in the supporting checks. The main
surface-contact study comprises \(111\) runs across \(37\) settings. A further
\(45\) runs across \(15\) settings were performed as supporting checks and are reported in
\Cref{app:supporting_contact_checks}. In total, \(156\) surface-contact runs
across \(52\) settings were recorded. No run was discarded. Together with the
\(12\) pose-hold runs of \Cref{sec:nullspace_hold_study}, the complete
experimental data set contains \(168\) recorded runs.

Within each controlled comparison, the calibrated geometry, common state
parameters, fixed stiffness and damping, and null-space settings remained unchanged. Only the
parameter assigned to the corresponding comparison was varied.

controller parameters. Case~A establishes the TCP-centred reference, where

in end-effector coordinates. The tangential coordinates reported for Case~D
are the surface-frame projections of those settings in the flat target
orientation.

axis, and intermediate tangent-plane directions
are treated as supporting checks in

\section{Experimental Condition and Response Quantity}
\label{sec:experimental_evaluation}
\label{sec:alignment_evaluation}
% ============================================================================

The commanded tool-orientation offset \(\theta_{\mathrm{cmd}}\) is the
controller input used to produce the pose-based initial angular deviation
\(\theta_{0,t_i}\). Contact Establishment then produces the signed response
\(\gamma_{t_i}\), which is used for the main experimental comparisons.


\subsection{Pose-Based Initial Angular Deviation}
\label{subsec:initial_angular_deviation}

At the start of Contact Establishment, the calibrated tool normal in base
coordinates is
\(n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}})\). Let the rotation vector
\(e_0=\phi_0u_0\) denote the shortest rotation from this direction back to the
configured flat direction \(-n_s\), so that

\begin{equation}
R(u_0,\phi_0)n_{\mathrm{Tool},0}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}})
=-n_s.
\label{eq:initial_deviation_rotation}
\end{equation}

The pose-based initial angular-deviation vector is defined in surface
coordinates as

\begin{equation}
\theta_{0,S}
=
-R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top e_0
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta_{0,t_1} & \theta_{0,t_2} & \theta_{0,n}
\end{bmatrix}^\top.
\label{eq:pose_based_initial_deviation}
\end{equation}

The minus sign makes a component directed away from the configured flat
orientation carry the same sign as the command that produced it. This angle is
calculated from the measured end-effector pose and the calibrated tool normal.
It is therefore the achieved reference-relative condition rather than an
independent measurement of the physical tool--surface angle.


\subsection{Signed Contact-Establishment Response}
\label{subsec:experimental_supporting_metrics}

Let \(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}}\) and \(t_{\mathrm{CE,end}}\) denote the
measurement instants at the beginning and end of contact establishment. The corresponding
measured end-effector orientations relative to the robot base frame are

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{CE,start}}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}(t_{\mathrm{CE,start}}),
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{CE,end}}=R_{\mathrm{EE}}(t_{\mathrm{CE,end}}).
\end{equation}

Contact-establishment holds the orientation captured at the clearance transition as its
rotational reference, and the measured orientation coincides with it at the
start of the state, so \(R_{\mathrm{CE,start}}\) is that held reference. The
relative rotation from the measured end orientation back to it is therefore

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{CE}}
=
R_{\mathrm{CE,start}}R_{\mathrm{CE,end}}^\top.
\end{equation}

Writing this relative rotation in axis--angle form as
\(R_{\mathrm{CE}}=R(u_{\mathrm{CE}},\phi_{\mathrm{CE}})\) gives the
measured contact-establishment rotation vector \(\gamma_0\) in base coordinates:

\begin{equation}
\gamma_0
=
\phi_{\mathrm{CE}}u_{\mathrm{CE}}
=
e_R\big|_{\mathrm{end\ of\ Contact\ Establishment}} .
\label{eq:contact_induced_rotation}
\end{equation}

The calculation uses only the measured end-effector orientations and therefore
does not require the instantaneous tool-to-gripper rotation, which was not
tracked during the contact experiments. The surface frame enters solely as the
directions the rotation is resolved along:

\begin{equation}
\gamma_S
=
R_{\mathrm{surface}}^\top\gamma_0
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\gamma_{t_1} & \gamma_{t_2} & \gamma_n
\end{bmatrix}^\top,
\qquad
\gamma_{t_i}=t_i^\top\gamma_0,
\quad i\in\{1,2\}.
\label{eq:surface_resolved_setup_rotation}
\end{equation}

The reported quantity \(\gamma_{t_i}\) is the signed contact-establishment response about
surface tangent \(t_i\). It represents the end-of-contact-establishment orientation change
relative to the held contact-establishment reference, resolved along that tangent. Its sign is
chosen such that a response reducing the initial angular deviation
\(\theta_{0,t_i}\) carries the same sign as that deviation. A correcting
response is positive for a positive initial deviation and negative for a
negative one, and its magnitude is the size of the correction. Reading the sign
alone is therefore not enough, since \(\theta_{0,t_i}\) fixes which direction
counts as correcting. The response is primary because it is obtained directly
from the measured end-effector orientations and requires no independent
measurement of the physical surface normal or tool orientation. The
pose-based and geometry-based consistency checks are reported in
\Cref{app:metric_agreement} and are not used for the main comparisons.

\section{Secondary Null-Space Pose-Hold Experiment}
\label{sec:nullspace_hold_study}
\label{subsec:nullspace_automatic_method}
% ============================================================================

The surface-contact measurements keep projected damping and singular-value
conditioning active together. A separate Cartesian pose-hold experiment was
therefore used to compare their individual effects under a repeatable
internally commanded disturbance.


\subsection{Experimental Configuration}

The pose-hold controller maintained the initial TCP pose with the stiffness and damping of
\Cref{tab:experimental_hold_gains}. Every entry was isotropic, so each matrix
is the stated value times the identity. Unlike the contact-sequence states, the
twelve pose-hold trials used fixed Cartesian damping rather than inertia-scaled
damping.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Isotropic Cartesian stiffness and damping of the pose-hold trials.}
\label{tab:experimental_hold_gains}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & & \textbf{Value}\\
\midrule
Translational stiffness & \(K_{p,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(2000\,\mathrm{N/m}\)\\
Rotational stiffness    & \(K_{R,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)\\
Translational damping   & \(D_{p,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(50\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}\)\\
Rotational damping      & \(D_{R,\mathrm{hold}}\) & \(8\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Four null-space settings were tested with three repetitions each: no null-space
torque, projected damping with
\(d_{\mathrm{null}}=2\,\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}\), sigma conditioning with
\(k_\sigma=1.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), and sigma conditioning with
\(k_\sigma=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). The probe step, sigma deadband, and relative
SVD threshold remained at \(0.08\,\mathrm{rad}\), \(10^{-6}\), and
\(10^{-4}\), respectively.


\subsection{Commanded Disturbance}

The disturbance represents the joint-torque equivalent of a commanded point
force acting at a specified location on link~3. The point was defined by the
link-frame offset

\begin{equation}
r_p
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
0.200
\end{bmatrix}
\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_point}
\end{equation}

At the initial posture \(q_{\mathrm{init}}\), the structural null direction
\(v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) and the translational point Jacobian
\(J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})\) define the fixed base-frame force direction

\begin{equation}
u_f
=
\frac{J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})}
{\lVert J_p(q_{\mathrm{init}})v_7(q_{\mathrm{init}})\rVert_2}.
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}
\end{equation}

The commanded force magnitude was \(F_d=20\,\mathrm{N}\). The virtual force
and its equivalent joint torque were

\begin{equation}
f_d(t)=F_d s_d(t)u_f,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)
=
J_p(q(t))^\top f_d(t).
\label{eq:nullspace_automatic_disturbance}
\end{equation}

Here \(u_f\) is the fixed unit force direction defined in
\Cref{eq:nullspace_disturbance_direction}, and \(s_d(t)\in[0,1]\) is a
dimensionless time-scaling function that switches the commanded virtual
disturbance smoothly on and off. It was defined as

\begin{equation}
s_d(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,
& t<5\,\mathrm{s},\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1}{2}\left[
1-\cos\!\left(\pi\dfrac{t-5\,\mathrm{s}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}\right)
\right],
& 5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<7\,\mathrm{s},\\[3mm]
1,
& 7\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<8\,\mathrm{s},\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1}{2}\left[
1+\cos\!\left(\pi\dfrac{t-8\,\mathrm{s}}{1\,\mathrm{s}}\right)
\right],
& 8\,\mathrm{s}\leq t<9\,\mathrm{s},\\[3mm]
0,
& t\geq9\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:nullspace_disturbance_scale}
\end{equation}

Thus the disturbance increased smoothly from zero to the full commanded
magnitude between \(5\) and \(7\,\mathrm{s}\), remained at full magnitude
until \(8\,\mathrm{s}\), and decreased smoothly to zero by
\(9\,\mathrm{s}\). The equivalent disturbance torque was limited to

\[
\lVert\tau_{\mathrm{dist}}\rVert_2\leq3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\]

The disturbance is internal to the controller and therefore provides a
repeatable comparison input; it is not a separately applied physical force.

The equivalent torque is formed from the point Jacobian alone and carries no
null-space projector, so it is a joint-torque excitation rather than a
null-space command. Its image therefore has a component along the six Cartesian
directions as well as along the redundant direction, and the Cartesian
impedance resists that component while the disturbance acts.
\Cref{eq:nullspace_cartesian_retention_criterion} therefore checks that the
commanded pose was retained rather than assuming it.

In every trial the selected null-space law was active from the start of the
pose-hold state. The first \(5\,\mathrm{s}\) therefore formed a
pre-disturbance settling interval, during which the conditioning torque of the
sigma-only settings could already move the redundant configuration. The
disturbance then acted from \(5\) to \(9\,\mathrm{s}\), followed by an
observation interval until the end of the trial. No secondary term was enabled
or disabled within a trial: the sigma-conditioning torque remained active
throughout the complete sigma-only trial and was not switched on after the
disturbance had been removed.


\subsection{Evaluation Quantities}

All quantities in this subsection are evaluated over the interval in which the
internally commanded virtual disturbance acts,
\(t\in[5,9]\,\mathrm{s}\). The first criterion checks whether the primary
Cartesian pose-hold objective is maintained while the disturbance and the
selected null-space law act. The predefined position-retention
criterion is

\begin{equation}
\max_{5\,\mathrm{s}\leq t\leq9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert e_p(t)\rVert_2
<2\,\mathrm{mm},
\label{eq:nullspace_cartesian_retention_criterion}
\end{equation}

where \(e_p(t)\) is the Cartesian position-error vector. This is an acceptance
check that the commanded pose was retained, rather than a primary performance
metric.

The redundant joint motion is evaluated from the projected joint velocity
defined in \Cref{eq:nullspace_velocity_projector}. The vector
\(\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}\in\mathbb{R}^7\) has units of
\(\mathrm{rad/s}\) and represents the component of the measured joint
velocity that lies in the instantaneous kinematic null space.

Two complementary quantities are formed from this projected velocity. The
first is the cumulative projected null-space motion

\begin{equation}
E_N
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\lVert\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}(t)\rVert_2\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_excursion_metric}
\end{equation}

Because the norm is taken before integration, motion in opposite directions does
not cancel. Thus \(E_N\) measures the total redundant joint motion and is
particularly relevant to the damping comparison. Its unit is radians, and the
reported values are converted to degrees.

The second quantity retains the direction of the motion. Integrating the
projected velocity without taking its norm gives the accumulated projected
joint displacement

\begin{equation}
\Delta q_{\mathrm{null}}
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
\dot q_{\mathrm{null}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
=
\int_{5\,\mathrm{s}}^{9\,\mathrm{s}}
N_q(q(t))\dot q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\label{eq:nullspace_net_joint_displacement}
\end{equation}

The vector \(\Delta q_{\mathrm{null}}\in\mathbb{R}^7\) has units of radians.
It describes the net accumulated projected joint motion during the disturbance
interval, so motion in opposite directions can cancel. It is not an integrated
torque: the integrated quantity is joint velocity.

A common direction is introduced to express this seven-dimensional net
displacement by one signed scalar. Let \(\Delta q_{\mathrm{null},0}\) denote
the arithmetic mean of the three net projected displacement vectors recorded in
the condition without null-space torque. The corresponding unit direction is

\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{ref}}
=
\frac{\Delta q_{\mathrm{null},0}}
{\lVert\Delta q_{\mathrm{null},0}\rVert_2}.
\label{eq:nullspace_reference_direction_metric}
\end{equation}

Thus \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}\) represents the redundant joint-space direction in
which the commanded virtual disturbance moves the robot in the reference
condition without a secondary null-space torque. The same direction is used
for all tested null-space settings. Their signed net displacement along this
common direction is

\begin{equation}
\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}
=
v_{\mathrm{ref}}^\top\Delta q_{\mathrm{null}}.
\label{eq:nullspace_net_displacement_metric}
\end{equation}

A positive \(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\) denotes net motion in the same
direction as the reference disturbance response, a negative value denotes net
motion in the opposite direction, and a value near zero indicates little
remaining net displacement along that direction. The quantity is a signed
projection of the accumulated projected joint motion rather than an exact
nonlinear redundant-coordinate displacement, because \(N_q(q)\) changes with
the robot configuration while \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is held fixed. At full
rank the Panda has a one-dimensional instantaneous null space, so this
common direction provides a meaningful signed comparison of the four tested
settings.

The signed \(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\) measures the net displacement
remaining along the common disturbance direction and is especially relevant to
the conditioning comparison. A trajectory can therefore have a large
\(E_N\) but a small \(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\). The change
\(\Delta\sigma_{\min,\mathrm{dist}}\) is retained as a supporting controller
diagnostic. The corresponding results are presented in
\Cref{ch:results_discussion}.
