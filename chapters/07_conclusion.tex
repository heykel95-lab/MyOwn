\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion_summary}

A real-time Cartesian impedance controller was implemented for
contact-induced alignment of a rectangular tool with a configured surface reference. The
controller assigns directional stiffness and damping in the surface frame and can shift the
virtual centre of compliance from the TCP. The shift couples the commanded
press to the rotational impedance through the term
\mbox{\(r_c\times f\)}.

The TCP-centred conditions produced alignment-directed end-effector rotation
for both signs of the initial angular deviation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. Raising
the rotational stiffness reduced the response over the tested range. Changing
the cross-axis translational stiffness produced a smaller effect.

Tangential compliance-centre position produced the largest measured variation.
The displacement that increased the correction-directed response depended on
the sign of the pose-based initial angular deviation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}.
Reversing the displacement reversed the moment contribution from the normal
press. Within the investigated contact configuration and tested positions, a
non-zero tangential centre could therefore not be selected independently of
that sign.

The TCP-centred impedance is the sign-independent fixed centre of compliance
among the tested positions for rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}, because it
selects no tangential direction. A displaced centre provides greater
condition-specific rotational authority when the required sign is known.
These properties are distinct: the fixed reference need not produce the
largest alignment-directed rotation in every condition.

The null-space pose-hold study separated projected joint damping from
singular-value conditioning. Damping reduced redundant joint motion while the
disturbance acted. Under the tested disturbance direction, conditioning strongly
suppressed the net redundant displacement and kept the Cartesian position error
below the specified \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} limit. The lower tested conditioning magnitude
produced the same suppression with less cumulative redundant motion.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Experimental Range}
\label{subsec:conclusion_experimental_range}

The contact study used one Panda configuration, one tool and mounting
arrangement, one configured surface reference, and one press trajectory. The conclusions
are therefore limited to this geometry and the tested stiffness,
initial angular-deviation and compliance-centre ranges. Sustained grinding and a
centre scheduled during contact were not evaluated.

\subsection{Measurement and Tool-Mount Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:conclusion_measurement_uncertainty}

The experimental condition was the pose-based initial angular deviation from
the configured surface reference. The physical plane normal was not measured
independently. The primary response was calculated from the measured
end-effector orientations.
The tool mount exhibited approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of unloaded play
about \mbox{\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}, and its instantaneous motion was not tracked
during contact. The results therefore describe end-effector rotation and do
not provide an independent physical tool--surface angle.
The reported contact evaluation was therefore restricted to rotation about
\mbox{\(t_1\)}; measurements about \mbox{\(t_2\)} were not included because
their response was not sufficiently repeatable for a reliable comparison.

\subsection{Null-Space Configuration and Real-Time Operation}
\label{subsec:conclusion_nullspace_scope}

The null-space terms were separated only in Cartesian pose hold under one
internally commanded disturbance direction. The conditioning torque was active
before the disturbance, so the experiment compares complete closed-loop modes
rather than isolated torque contributions at identical configurations. The
surface-contact study held the combined mode fixed. Worst-case execution time
and scheduling jitter were not measured separately from operation through the
nominal \mbox{\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\)} interface.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

The first priority is an independent measurement of the physical plane normal
and the physical tool-face angle during contact. This would quantify the
surface-reference error and separate end-effector rotation from motion in the
tool mount.

The second priority is a direction-selected compliance-centre strategy. Such a
controller would begin at the TCP, estimate the required alignment direction,
apply a bounded tangential displacement during contact establishment, and return to the TCP
before sustained contact. Future experiments should repeat the evaluation about
\mbox{\(t_2\)} and additional tangent-plane directions using improved tool
constraint and independent measurement of the physical tool orientation.

The third priority is a combined-mode null-space study under physical
disturbance. Varying projected damping and conditioning together would show
whether the pose-hold findings remain valid during contact. A continuous or
hysteretic conditioning law should also be compared with the present
two-point selector to reduce its switching activity.
