\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This thesis developed a real-time Cartesian impedance controller for the
Franka Emika Panda and traced its frame, sign, and reference-point conventions
from the Cartesian error to the final joint-torque command.  The implemented
law uses
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{cmd}}
  =J^\top w_{\mathrm{cmd}}+\tau_{\mathrm{null}}+c(q,\dot q),
\]
with gravity handled by the Franka torque interface.  Translation uses
\(e_p=p_d-p_{\mathrm{TCP}}\); the restoring rotation vector is obtained from
\(R_{\mathrm{TCP}}^\top R_d\), mapped to base components, and multiplied by a
positive rotational stiffness.

The surface geometry is represented by
\(R_s=[t_1,t_2,n_s]\).  The controller maps the configured
\(+z_{\mathrm{EE}}\) tool axis to the signed target \(-n_s\), descends along
\(-n_s\), selects the leading point, edge, or face of the rectangular tool, and
switches to set-up at a geometric clearance.  Set-up then ramps the selected
feature into the plane and ends after a minimum time when the external-moment
change reaches its threshold, or at the timeout.  Grind freezes the final
preload and optionally adds a smooth tangential sweep.  The external wrench is
model-based and is logged during descent, but it does not trigger the clearance
transition.

A center of compliance is implemented during set-up by the adjoint-congruence
construction derived once in \cref{sec:coupled_pole_stiffness}. In the active
tagged configuration, the pole and TCP references are captured at the
clearance hand-off, so the controller uses the constant lever
\(r_c=p_{\mathrm{TCP,clr}}-p_c\). The resulting off-diagonal blocks couple
translation and rotation while preserving symmetry and positive
semidefiniteness. The experiment evaluates the realized response through the
physical tool-axis angle and selected-feature/TCP displacement.

Redundancy is handled with a tolerance-truncated SVD of the \(6\times7\)
Jacobian.  The implementation reciprocates only the retained
\(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_6\), constructs
\(N_\tau=(I-J_\rho^+J)^\top\), and uses \(v_7\) as the structural one-dimensional
null direction at full row rank.  The selected mode can apply projected joint
damping, a constant-magnitude torque in whichever sign of \(v_7\) improves
\(\sigma_{\min}\), both terms, or neither.  The probe distance
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{probe}}\)
changes only the two sampled configurations; it does not scale the commanded
torque.

Chapter~\ref{ch:results_discussion} reports a campaign of \(71\) runs recorded
with \texttt{FULL-Working-Clean}, gated on a measured equilibrium, a coupled-path
correctness check and a five-repetition noise floor. Its central result is that
the placement of the centre of compliance governs the contact alignment through
its \emph{tangential} component: over \(36\) pole runs the tangential offset
accounts for the alignment outcome (\(R^2=0.907\)) while the offset along the
surface normal does not (\(R^2=0.005\)). The older
\texttt{Difference-Chasles-Pole} results are retained alongside it for
comparison. Their qualitative claim --- that pole placement decides whether the
coupling resists or assists alignment --- is confirmed; their attribution of the
effect to the normal offset is not.

\section{Main Contributions}
\label{sec:main_contributions}

The main contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a frame-consistent Cartesian impedance implementation with explicit
        position, orientation, wrench-ordering, lever-arm, and contact-moment
        conventions;
  \item an implemented center-of-compliance construction that shifts a
        pole-frame \(6\times6\) stiffness and damping to the TCP through an
        adjoint congruence;
  \item a geometric tool and phase supervisor comprising manual guidance/hold,
        tool-axis alignment, clearance-based descent, set-up, optional gates,
        and grinding;
  \item phase-specific gain frames and model-based Cartesian-inertia damping,
        with manual damping values retained as fallbacks;
  \item a four-mode null-space controller based on a truncated-SVD
        pseudoinverse, including the explicit distinction between
        \(\sigma_{\min}=\sigma_6\) and the structural null vector \(v_7\); and
  \item full-rate and compact ring-buffer logging that exposes the phase,
        selected tool feature, null-space probes, and final torque command for
        offline analysis; and
  \item a provenance-controlled measurement campaign of \(71\) runs, gated on a
        measured equilibrium, a coupled-path correctness check and a
        five-repetition noise floor, establishing that the tangential component
        of the compliance centre governs contact alignment while the normal
        component does not.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

No dedicated force/torque sensor is used. The signal
\texttt{O\_F\_ext\_hat\_K} is libfranka's model-based estimate referenced to
stiffness frame \(K\). It remains an implementation diagnostic, and its
bias-subtracted moment norm is used by the optional set-up exit condition. It
is not treated as a direct contact-wrench measurement.

The transition called ``first contact'' in several source and CSV identifiers
is actually the configured \(20\,\mathrm{mm}\) geometric clearance.  The stored
\texttt{first\_contact\_point} is the selected feature projected onto the
surface. The general log now carries the tool-axis-to-\(-n_s\) angle
alongside the rotation error referenced to the frozen clearance orientation, so
the surface-alignment metric no longer depends on a wrench-comparison model.
That angle is nevertheless scored against the \emph{configured} surface normal;
\cref{sec:results_alignment} rescores it against the physically measured plane,
which is required because the configured tilt is deliberately mis-specified.
The exact clearance comparison uses the most-leading corner, while the
averaged tied feature supplies the projected reference. In addition, the
transition callback changes to set-up gains before regenerating the set-up
target, so the first phase-2 sample can contain a one-cycle approach-target
mismatch.

The set-up moment threshold of \(150\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) is never reached: the
measured moment settles near \(30\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), so the phase always exits on
the timeout and no transition in this campaign is moment-triggered. The timeout
originally coincided with the end of the press ramp at \(4\,\mathrm{s}\), which
terminated the phase during the force overshoot; \cref{sec:results_equilibrium}
measures equilibrium at \(t\approx4.6\,\mathrm{s}\) and the timeout is now
\(12\,\mathrm{s}\).  The set-up and grind TCP target also
subtracts a tool offset rotated by the frozen clearance orientation.  If the
tool rotates appreciably, the live physical feature can move tangentially
relative to that reference; whether this behavior is desirable must be tested.
This conversion is stated explicitly in
\cref{eq:software_ee_target_from_feature}. At the set-up-to-grind hand-off, the
coupled matrices are replaced immediately by the decoupled blocks in the same
callback, without interpolation. The commanded wrench and
\(J^\top w_{\mathrm{cmd}}\) can therefore step at that transition.

The SVD uses an unscaled geometric Jacobian whose translational and rotational
rows have different physical units.  Its numerical singular values, cutoff,
and condition measures are consequently scale- and reference-point-dependent.
When a direction is omitted by the cutoff, the projector admits that weak task
direction into its numerical null space.  Moreover, the Euclidean projector is
not dynamically consistent: in general
\(JM_q^{-1}\tau_{\mathrm{null}}\ne0\).  The claim is therefore limited to the
retained kinematic projection, not to zero Cartesian acceleration disturbance.

The sigma torque is a switched, constant-magnitude active bias. Sigma-only mode
is undamped, the finite probes follow only the instantaneous self-motion
tangent, and they are not checked against joint limits. The term is therefore a
two-point conditioning heuristic rather than a passive secondary controller; it
uses neither the sampled slope magnitude nor an iterative search. Active mode
evaluates three SVDs and two additional model Jacobians per callback. In the
current \(11624\)-row trace,
\(99.51\,\%\) of adjacent callback periods are \(1\,\mathrm{ms}\), giving an
effective delivered-callback rate of \(993.1\,\mathrm{Hz}\) over
\(11.704\,\mathrm{s}\). This supports sustained operation in libfranka's
nominal \(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\) interface, but callback-period spacing and the
last-100-command reception fraction
\texttt{control\_command\_success\_rate} do not bound local callback execution
time; worst-case execution time remains unmeasured. Because the active
configuration uses projected damping plus this sigma bias during set-up, its
response is classified as unscripted task-space alignment rather than an
isolated contact-only response.

Finally, the configured Franka collision/reflex thresholds are not a software
wrench, joint-torque, or torque-rate limiter.  Practical safety conclusions
must therefore be based on supervised trials, the robot's certified safety
configuration, measured commands, and the controller timing rather than on an
unimplemented saturation law. The general sequence CSV is written only on the
normal post-run path; reflex/contact flags and exception-path persistence belong
to the compact sigma diagnostic and must not be assumed for every sequence.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

The tool-axis alignment angle is now logged, which is what made
Chapter~\ref{ch:results_discussion} quantitative. The remaining fields worth
adding are the current orientation itself, the desired tool axis, the retained
SVD rank, and a projector residual such as \(\|JN_q\|\). Logging the orientation
would remove the one reconstruction step still required in the analysis, where
the tool axis at the end of the set-up phase is recovered from
\(-\mathbf e_R\) applied to an assumed starting axis.

The clearance variables should be renamed, and live versus frozen tool-offset
kinematics should be compared experimentally. The approach-to-set-up hand-off
should also compute the continuous
set-up target before enabling the set-up gains/coupled branch, eliminating the
one-cycle mixed-state callback. The set-up-to-grind hand-off should blend the
coupled and decoupled matrices to avoid a torque step. The set-up threshold should be retuned against the
measured external-moment range so that timeout and moment-triggered exits are
distinguishable.

For the null space, future work should validate parameter ranges, guard
\(q\pm\alpha_{\mathrm{probe}}\hat v_7\) against joint limits, instrument the
callback wall-clock duration to bound worst-case execution time, archive the
timing trace and command-reception rate, and
compare \(\|JN_q\|\) with the dynamic leakage
\(\|JM_q^{-1}N_\tau\|\).  A characteristic-length scaling could make the
geometric-Jacobian singular values more interpretable.  Energy or power
monitoring could also constrain the active sigma bias
\citep{Hjorth2023EnergyAwareImpedance}. The pole experiment should additionally
be repeated with null-space damping only, or as matched sigma-off/sigma-on
trials, to separate the active secondary bias from the alignment response during
contact.

The coupled-pole campaign has now been repeated with
\texttt{FULL-Working-Clean} and multiple repetitions, and three questions it
raised remain open. The tangential sweep had not reached an optimum at the
positive end of its tested range, so the best placement is bounded below but not
located. The pole component along the second surface tangent was held at
approximately zero throughout and has never been swept, which leaves the
geometric placement rule --- that the centre of compliance belongs opposite the
side of the tool that contacts first --- untested on the axis it concerns.
And the contact itself, recovered from the measured wrench, sits near the middle
of the tool face rather than at the corner the controller's tie-breaking
feature selection reports, which suggests the selection tolerance should be
compared against the measured load distribution.
