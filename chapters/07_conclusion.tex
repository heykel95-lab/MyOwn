\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion_summary}

A real-time Cartesian impedance controller was implemented for
contact-induced alignment of a rectangular tool with a configured surface reference. The
controller assigned directional stiffness and damping in the surface frame. A
shift of the virtual centre of compliance from the TCP coupled the commanded
press to the rotational impedance through \mbox{\(r_c\times f\)}.

At the TCP, Case~A produced measured response-to-entry-magnitude ratios of
\mbox{\(81.3\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(115.1\,\%\)} in the two evaluated directions
of rotation. Raising \mbox{\(K_{R,t_1}\)} from
\mbox{\(5\)} to \mbox{\(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)} reduced the response by
\mbox{\(63.9\,\%\)} relative to the \mbox{\(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\)}
condition in Case~B. By comparison, the Case-C response span across the tested
\mbox{\(K_{p,t_2}\)} values equalled only \mbox{\(0.4\,\%\)} of the
TCP-centred reference.

In Case~D, tangential compliance-centre position produced the largest measured
variation.
Relative to the TCP-centred response magnitudes, the outer position selected
for each direction increased the response by \mbox{\(4.0\,\%\)} and
\mbox{\(4.3\,\%\)}. The outer position in the other displacement direction
reduced the response by \mbox{\(97.6\,\%\)} and \mbox{\(99.2\,\%\)},
respectively. Every tested non-zero position increased the response in one
direction of rotation and reduced it in the other.

For the tested positions and rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}, the TCP-centred
impedance provided a neutral fixed centre with no direction-selected lever. At
\mbox{\(r_c=0\)}, the point-shift contribution satisfies
\mbox{\(r_c\times f=0\)}. Even so, the measured end-effector rotation was towards the
configured flat direction in both tested directions of rotation. A displaced
centre supplied greater rotational authority when its position was selected for
a known direction. The neutral fixed reference and direction-specific authority
are separate properties.

Projected joint damping and singular-value conditioning were separated in the
null-space pose-hold study. Projected damping reduced cumulative redundant
motion relative to the inactive mode. With the internally commanded disturbance
used here, conditioning kept the net redundant displacement close to zero. The
largest measured Cartesian position error was \mbox{\(1.304\,\mathrm{mm}\)},
below the specified \mbox{\(2\,\mathrm{mm}\)} limit. The lower conditioning
magnitude produced comparable suppression with less cumulative redundant
motion.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Experimental Generalisability}
\label{subsec:conclusion_experimental_range}

One Panda configuration, one tool and mounting arrangement, one configured
surface reference, and one press trajectory were used in the contact study. The conclusions
are therefore limited to this geometry and the tested stiffness, achieved
initial angular-offset and compliance-centre ranges. The main comparison
covered rotation about \mbox{\(t_1\)}. Measurements about \mbox{\(t_2\)}
showed greater variability and did not support the same repeatable comparison.
Sustained grinding and a centre scheduled during contact were not evaluated.

\subsection{Calibration and Measurement Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:conclusion_measurement_uncertainty}

The physical plane normal was not measured independently in each experiment.
The achieved pose-based initial angular offset \mbox{\(\theta_{0,t_1}\)} was
calculated relative to the configured surface reference, and the primary
response was calculated from the measured end-effector orientations. These
quantities retain the
uncertainty of the configured surface reference and do not constitute an
independent physical tool--surface-angle measurement. The selected tool point
\mbox{\(p_{\mathrm{Tool}}\)} was the geometric reference for trajectory
generation. The physical pressure centre and instantaneous contact location
were not observed.

\subsection{Tool-Mount Compliance}
\label{subsec:conclusion_tool_mount}

The tool mount exhibited approximately \mbox{\(\pm2^\circ\)} of unloaded play
about \mbox{\(y_{\mathrm{EE}}\)}. Its instantaneous relative rotation was not
tracked during contact. This axis is approximately aligned with
\mbox{\(t_2\)} in the flat configuration and may have contributed to the
greater variability of the \mbox{\(t_2\)} measurements. The contact results
quantify end-effector rotation rather than an independently measured
physical tool-face rotation.

\subsection{Null-Space Validation and Real-Time Operation}
\label{subsec:conclusion_nullspace_scope}

The null-space terms were separated only in Cartesian pose hold under one
internally commanded disturbance direction. The conditioning law applies an
active, non-dissipative torque towards locally larger
\mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)}, so its measured disturbance rejection is specific
to that direction. Before the disturbance, the
conditioning term shifted the singular-value-conditioning entry configurations by approximately
\mbox{\(0.013\,\mathrm{rad}\)} relative to the modes without conditioning.
Complete closed-loop modes are therefore compared rather than isolated torque
contributions at identical configurations. The combined mode was held fixed in
the surface-contact study, so the individual null-space contributions were not
separated during contact and a physical disturbance was not evaluated.
The net redundant displacement \mbox{\(\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{dist}}\)} is a
fixed-direction projection. Because the joint-velocity projector
\mbox{\(N_q\)} changes with the robot configuration, this projection is not an
exact nonlinear redundant coordinate.

\Added{The geometric Jacobian combines translational and rotational rows
without a common length scale, so \mbox{\(\sigma_{\min}\)} is interpreted only
as a relative indicator under the fixed Jacobian convention.}

Worst-case execution time and scheduling jitter were not measured separately
from operation through the nominal \mbox{\(1\,\mathrm{kHz}\)} interface. The
assembled torque command had no application-side saturation or torque-rate
limiter after assembly; robot-side collision and reflex monitoring supplied
the independent protection layer.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

The first priority is an independent measurement of the physical plane normal
and the physical tool-face angle during contact. This would quantify the
surface-reference error and resolve end-effector and tool-mount rotation as
separate quantities.

The second priority is a direction-selected compliance-centre strategy. Such a
controller would begin at the TCP, estimate the required alignment direction,
apply a bounded tangential displacement during contact establishment, and return to the TCP
before sustained contact. The evaluation should be repeated about
\mbox{\(t_2\)} and additional tangent-plane directions with improved tool
constraint and independent measurement of the physical tool orientation.

The third priority is a combined-mode null-space study under physical
disturbance. Varying projected damping and conditioning together would measure
their combined contact response. A comparison with a continuous or hysteretic
conditioning law would quantify the achievable reduction in switching
activity.
