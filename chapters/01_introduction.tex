\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\label{sec:motivation}
Robotic manipulators are traditionally designed for accurate and
repeatable motion. In many industrial applications, this is sufficient
because the environment is structured and contact with humans or
unknown obstacles is avoided. In collaborative and interaction-based
tasks, however, purely rigid position control is not always suitable.
Unlike classical industrial robots, which operate in fenced-off cells
and prioritise rigid trajectory tracking, collaborative robots must be
capable of reacting safely and predictably to contact with their
environment. A robot that reacts compliantly to contact can reduce
interaction forces and can be guided more naturally by a human
operator. Collaborative robots account for a growing share of
industrial robot deployments, since their inherent compliance and
sensor capabilities enable safe human--robot collaboration
\citep{IFR2023WorldRobotics}.

Hogan introduced in 1985 \citep{Hogan1985}, Cartesian impedance control that addresses this problem
by describing the dynamic behaviour of the robot arm as a mass--spring--damper system in
Cartesian coordinates. By deliberately choosing stiffness and damping
parameters, the compliance of the end-effector along each Cartesian
axis can be set individually. Implementing this control strategy
requires precise knowledge of the Jacobian matrix of the manipulator,
which establishes the relationship between Cartesian velocities and
joint angular velocities and additionally enables the mapping of
Cartesian forces to joint torques \citep{Siciliano2009,Khatib1987,Mayr2024CartesianImpedance}.
The main goal of this thesis is therefore to formulate and analyse a
Cartesian impedance controller for a 7-\abbr{DOF} manipulator.

The Franka Emika Panda represents this class of robot: a
7-\abbr{DOF} arm with integrated joint torque sensing, designed to run
at 1\,kHz in torque-control mode through the Franka Control Interface
(\abbr{FCI}) \citep{FrankaFCI2023}. Achieving compliant, force-aware
behaviour on such a platform requires moving beyond position control to
an impedance-based approach in which the robot's dynamic relationship
with the environment is explicitly designed and parameterised.

The theoretical background of this work builds on standard treatments
of serial-manipulator kinematics and dynamics, including homogeneous
transformations, the geometric and analytic Jacobian, redundancy, and
operational-space modelling \citep{Siciliano2009,Craig2005,Murray1994,Khatib1987}.
For the Panda platform, these foundations are especially relevant
because \abbr{FCI} provides direct access to joint states, model
quantities, and the geometric Jacobian in a deterministic 1\,kHz
torque-control loop. The controller developed in this thesis follows
the Jacobian-transpose mapping from Cartesian forces to joint torques
\citep{Khatib1987}, while using a fixed-parameter, quasi-static
impedance model similar in structure to practical Cartesian impedance
software implementations \citep{Mayr2024CartesianImpedance}. This choice
avoids the complexity and model sensitivity of full operational-space
dynamic inversion, remains suitable for the low-velocity interaction
tasks considered here, and leaves adaptive or variable-impedance
extensions as future work \citep{Albu2003,Ott2008,Villani2016}.
The secondary null-space term is treated as an auxiliary redundancy
resolution component, following the broader literature on null-space
projection for redundant torque-controlled robots
\citep{Khatib1987,Dietrich2015NullspaceSurvey}.

This is technically demanding: it requires a verified real-time
software stack, a mathematically sound controller formulation, and a
systematic experimental evaluation. The present thesis addresses these
aspects for the Panda by developing and evaluating a Cartesian
impedance-control approach. The work focuses on the
compliant behaviour of the manipulator, the influence of controller
parameters, and the stability of the resulting impedance behaviour.

Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} develops the theoretical background and
controller formulation for Cartesian impedance control. Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation}
describes the software implementation used to execute the controller on the
Panda. Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments} defines the experimental design and
evaluation procedure, and Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} summarises the results
and limitations.
