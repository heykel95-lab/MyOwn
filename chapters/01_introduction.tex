\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Collaborative robotic manipulators have become central platforms for
research and prototyping in flexible automation and human-robot
interaction.
Unlike classical industrial robots, which operate in fenced-off cells
and prioritise rigid trajectory tracking, collaborative robots must be
capable of reacting safely and predictably to contact with their
environment.
The Franka Emika Panda represents this class of robot: a seven-degree-of-freedom
arm with integrated joint torque sensing, designed to run at 1\,kHz in
torque-control mode through the Franka Control Interface (FCI).

Achieving compliant, force-aware behaviour on such a platform requires
moving beyond position control to an impedance-based approach in which
the robot's dynamic relationship with the environment is explicitly
designed and parameterised.
This is technically demanding: it requires a verified real-time software
stack, a mathematically sound controller formulation, and a systematic
experimental evaluation.
The present thesis addresses all three aspects for the Panda, using
Cartesian impedance control in the group $SE(3)$.

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

The objectives of this thesis, as approved by the examination board, are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Literature research} on robot kinematics, robot dynamics,
        Franka Emika control, FCI/libfranka, and impedance control,
        including scientific analysis and discussion of this research in
        the report.
  \item \textbf{Setup and documentation} of the robot/workstation
        configuration, including network settings, real-time Linux
        requirements, FCI access, and the C++/libfranka development
        environment.
  \item \textbf{Development} of a modular C++ software structure for
        reading robot states, accessing model information, and executing
        basic motion/control examples in a reproducible manner.
  \item \textbf{Implementation and experimental evaluation} of a
        Cartesian impedance-control approach, including analysis of
        stability, tracking performance, compliance, limitations, and
        practical usability.
  \item \textbf{Public presentation} (approx.\ 30--45 minutes
        presentation and approx.\ 15 minutes discussion).
  \item \textbf{Compilation} of a comprehensible report in English
        (approx.\ 50--100 pages, plus appendix if necessary).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis Structure}
\label{sec:structure}

The thesis is organised as follows, each chapter building directly on
the previous one:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:literature} --- State of the Art:}
        Reviews robot kinematics, impedance control, and the Franka
        platform; positions this work in the research landscape.
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:setup} --- System Setup:}
        Documents the complete hardware and software stack required to
        run real-time torque control on the Panda: network connection,
        Franka Desk access, real-time Ubuntu kernel, libfranka, and
        the C++ build environment.
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} --- Theoretical Background:}
        Develops the full mathematical theory of Cartesian impedance
        control in $SE(3)$: coordinate frames, homogeneous
        transformations, pose error, forward and differential
        kinematics, the Jacobian, and the impedance control law with
        full SPD stiffness and damping matrices.
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation} --- Software Implementation:}
        Describes the modular C++ structure that implements the theory:
        state acquisition, model access, the 1\,ms control callback,
        and safety mechanisms.
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments} --- Experiments:}
        Presents four experiments --- Experiment~1 (Cartesian pose
        tracking), Experiment~2 (virtual wall constraint),
        Experiment~3 (stiffness variation), and Experiment~4
        (configuration dependence) --- evaluating stability, tracking
        behaviour, and compliant interaction.
  \item \textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} --- Conclusion:}
        Summarises results, discusses limitations, and outlines
        directions for future work.
\end{itemize}

The appendix provides a step-by-step numerical worked example for the
Panda (Appendix~A) and the complete simulation code and C++ listings
for each experiment (Appendices B--E).
