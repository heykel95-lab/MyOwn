\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\ReviewMark{green}{The abstract states the problem, implemented mechanism,
principal measured ordering, null-space result, and scope limits without
overloading the reader with the full parameter matrix.}

Angular tool--surface mismatch causes edge-first contact; rigid position
control resists the aligning rotation and develops contact forces and moments.

A real-time, phase-dependent Cartesian impedance controller was implemented
for a seven-degree-of-freedom (\abbr{DOF}) Franka Emika Panda. During set-up,
stiffness and damping defined about a virtual centre of compliance \(p_c\) were
transformed to the tool centre point, converting the commanded normal press
into a corrective moment.

The experiments varied directional stiffness and the compliance-centre lever
\(r_c\); its in-plane position produced the largest change. The assisting lever
was perpendicular to the commanded rotation axis, and its direction reversed
with the commanded offset. About \(t_1\), the robot already rotated \(7.57^\circ\) without
a lever, and the assisting \(40\,\mathrm{mm}\) lever increased this to only
\(7.87^\circ\). About \(t_2\), the same lever changed the set-up rotation from
\(-1.63^\circ\) to \(+4.43^\circ\); it was necessary for helpful robot rotation
but insufficient to remove the full initial deviation.

\Revised{Across four tangent-plane offsets, a direction-selected lever increased
the signed set-up rotation in every matched comparison, whereas one fixed lever
produced less rotation. Tool-frame and surface-frame lever definitions also
diverged after the end effector rotated.}

In complementary pose-hold trials, projected null-space damping reduced
redundant motion, and both conditioning torque commands returned
\(\sigma_{\min}\) approximately to its initial level. Signed set-up rotation was
the common contact-test metric. The supporting alignment angle was inferred
from the end-effector pose, so unmeasured tool-mount motion limits its physical
interpretation.
