\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This thesis investigates the design and implementation of a real-time Cartesian impedance controller for a redundant 7-\abbr{DOF} collaborative robot. The main objective is to achieve compliant behaviour in Cartesian space while maintaining a mathematically clear relation between pose error, impedance wrench, Jacobian mapping, and joint torque commands. Special attention is given to the interpretation of full stiffness and damping matrices. For fixed-point tracking, the desired point defines the centre of the virtual spring, whereas the stiffness matrix defines the shape and orientation of the restoring behaviour around this point.

The controller is formulated using local and base-frame matrices, allowing rotated principal stiffness directions to be represented by full symmetric positive definite matrices in the base frame. The implementation is developed in C++ with a libfranka-style real-time torque-control structure. The experimental design includes fixed-point tracking with rotated stiffness directions, virtual-wall interaction, stiffness variation, and configuration-dependent joint torque distribution.
