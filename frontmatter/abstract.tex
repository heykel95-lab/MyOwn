\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Surface-placement and tool-mount tolerances can produce angular misalignment
between a grinding tool and a surface. As a result, the tool can make edge-first
contact and generate high contact loads.

A real-time Cartesian impedance controller was implemented for a
seven-degree-of-freedom (\abbr{DOF}) Franka Emika Panda. Its compliance was
defined relative to the surface geometry and about a virtual centre. Moving this
centre away from the tool centre point (TCP) coupled the commanded press to an
additional rotational moment. During contact establishment, the controller held
the orientation reference fixed while pressing the tool towards the surface.

The experiments varied the virtual centre and one rotational and one
translational stiffness parameter. A configured angular offset defined the
desired pre-contact orientation and was applied in both directions of rotation
about the same axis in the surface plane. The offset achieved at contact entry
was calculated relative to the configured surface. The response was calculated
from the measured end-effector orientations.

Within the tested ranges, centre position affected the response more than
either stiffness parameter. Each tested displacement from the TCP increased the
response towards parallel alignment in one rotational direction and reduced it
in the other. The TCP-centred condition produced this response in both
rotational directions and provided the neutral fixed setting. A displaced
centre produced a slightly larger response when selected for the corresponding
direction of rotation.
A separate free-space study held the end-effector pose. Internal-motion damping
reduced cumulative motion. Under the tested disturbance, active conditioning
kept the measured net change along the evaluated internal direction close to
zero.
