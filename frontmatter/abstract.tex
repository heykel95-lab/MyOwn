\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Small geometric errors between a rigidly controlled tool and a workpiece can
produce undesirable contact loads and prevent the tool from aligning with the
surface. This thesis develops a real-time Cartesian impedance controller for a
7-\abbr{DOF} Franka Emika Panda and investigates how tangential rotational
stiffness and the spatial position of a virtual centre of compliance influence
contact-induced alignment.

The controller uses explicitly defined base and surface frames, phase-dependent
Cartesian gains, model-based damping, and a Jacobian-transpose joint-torque
command. During set-up, a pole-frame \(6\times6\) stiffness and damping matrix
is shifted to the TCP through an adjoint congruence. The resulting
translation--rotation coupling allows a translational contact response to
generate a rotational moment about the selected virtual centre. An SVD-based
null-space controller is implemented for the redundant seventh joint, although
its effect is not evaluated in a separate experimental campaign.

The experimental study comprises \(92\) recorded runs, of which \(89\) are
admissible for their recorded quantities. Five repetitions were performed for
each tangential rotational-stiffness setting. Reducing the stiffness from
\(50\) to \(5\,\mathrm{Nm/rad}\) increased the mean alignment improvement from
approximately \(0.79^\circ\) to \(2.38^\circ\), while the mean
model-estimated normal load remained within
\(60.27\)--\(60.77\,\mathrm{N}\).

The position of the virtual compliance centre was evaluated along the two
surface tangents and the physical surface normal. An additive quadratic model
in the two in-plane coordinates accounts for \(R^2=0.962\) of the alignment
response over \(57\) position-study runs and predicts a local maximum near
\(t_1=+62\,\mathrm{mm}\) and \(t_2=+30\,\mathrm{mm}\). This coordinate pair
was not tested directly. Unsuitable in-plane placements produced negative
alignment improvements as low as \(-10.31^\circ\). Adding the physical normal
coordinate increased \(R^2\) only from \(0.962\) to \(0.966\) over the
investigated range, indicating substantially weaker additional explanatory
power than the in-plane coordinates.

The results establish a strong and non-monotonic relationship between
in-plane compliance-centre placement and contact-induced alignment for the
investigated configuration. Their generality is limited by the use of one
robot, tool, workpiece, and fixture; uncertainty in the hand-estimated physical
plane; cross-shaped rather than full two-dimensional sampling; and the use of
a model-estimated external wrench instead of an independent force/torque
sensor.
