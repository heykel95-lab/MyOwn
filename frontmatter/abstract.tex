\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\ReviewMark{green}{The abstract states the problem, implemented mechanism,
principal measured ordering, null-space result, and scope limits without
overloading the reader with the full parameter matrix.}

Angular tool--surface mismatch causes edge-first contact. Under rigid position
control, the robot resists the aligning rotation and develops contact forces
and moments.

A real-time, phase-dependent Cartesian impedance controller was implemented
for a seven-degree-of-freedom (\abbr{DOF}) Franka Emika Panda. During set-up,
impedance gains defined at a virtual centre of compliance were transformed to
the tool centre point, coupling the commanded normal press to a corrective
moment.

The systematic investigation asked how directional stiffness and
compliance-centre direction, sign, and magnitude affect alignment, and whether
tilt determines lever selection. Within the tested range, the in-plane
compliance-centre position produced the largest change. The assisting lever lay
perpendicular to the tilt axis and reversed sign with the tilt, giving about
\(6^\circ\) improvement in both tangent-axis tests. Its best tested magnitude
depended on axis and tilt angle. Higher rotational stiffness reduced alignment,
with axis-dependent behaviour.

\Revised{Across four tangent-plane directions, a tilt-selected
\mbox{\(60\,\mathrm{mm}\)} lever assisted every condition, whereas one fixed lever
reduced the matched improvement by \mbox{\(32\)--\(48\,\%\)}. The matched reductions
support tilt-dependent lever selection across the tested directions.}

Complementary Cartesian pose-hold trials showed that projected null-space
damping reduced redundant motion. Both tested conditioning torque commands
returned the minimum singular value to approximately its initial level after
the disturbance.
