\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Collaborative robots working alongside humans require controllers that respond to unplanned contact in a safe and predictable way. Cartesian impedance control addresses this by regulating the apparent mechanical impedance of the end-effector, making the robot behave as a virtual spring-damper system that yields to external forces rather than resisting them rigidly. Choosing and interpreting the stiffness and damping matrices that govern this behaviour is non-trivial, particularly when compliant directions must be oriented independently of the robot base frame.

This thesis investigates the design, implementation, and evaluation of a real-time
Cartesian impedance controller for the Franka Emika Panda, a redundant 7-\abbr{DOF}
collaborative robot. The controller is intended to produce compliant Cartesian motion while
keeping the relation between pose error, impedance forces and moments, Jacobian mapping, and
commanded joint torques traceable. The formulation uses stiffness and damping matrices defined
in the end-effector frame and transforms them into the robot base frame.

The controller is implemented in C++ with libfranka and Eigen inside a real-time torque-control callback.
The evaluation covers set-point convergence, trajectory tracking, compliance under external interaction,
and commanded joint torque distribution. In these tests, the controller converges toward the desired pose
with damped transients, tracks the selected Cartesian trajectory with bounded error, shows the expected
stiffness-dependent compliance, and keeps the commanded torques smooth for the evaluated parameter sets.
