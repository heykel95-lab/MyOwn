\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Surface-positioning and tool-mounting tolerances can produce angular
misalignment between a grinding tool and a surface. This misalignment can cause
edge-first contact and high contact loads.

A real-time Cartesian impedance controller was implemented on a
seven-degree-of-freedom (\abbr{DOF}) Franka Emika Panda. Directional stiffness
and damping were defined relative to a configured surface frame, and the
impedance reference could be shifted from the tool centre point (TCP) to a
virtual centre of compliance (\abbr{CoC}). Displacing the \abbr{CoC} from the
TCP coupled the commanded translational press to an additional commanded TCP
moment. During Contact Establishment, the controller advanced the tool towards
the surface while holding the contact-entry orientation as the rotational
reference.

The surface-contact experiments varied the \abbr{CoC} position, the rotational
stiffness, and the cross-axis translational stiffness. Positive and negative
pre-contact angular offsets were tested about the same surface-tangent axis.
The achieved initial angular offset at contact entry was calculated relative to
the configured surface reference. The contact-establishment response was
calculated from the measured end-effector orientations at the beginning and end
of Contact Establishment.

Within the tested ranges, the tangential \abbr{CoC} position produced the
largest measured response variation. Increasing the rotational stiffness from
\(5\) to \(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\) reduced the response by
\(63.9\,\%\) relative to the \(5\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\) condition. The
tested cross-axis translational stiffness values produced a response span equal
to only \(0.4\,\%\) of the TCP-centred reference. For the two offset
directions, the \abbr{CoC} displacement selected to reinforce the corrective
rotation increased the response magnitude by \(4.0\,\%\) and
\(4.3\,\%\). The opposite displacement reduced the corresponding response
magnitude by \(97.6\,\%\) and \(99.2\,\%\). With the \abbr{CoC} at the
TCP, the measured end-effector response was directed towards the configured
surface-parallel orientation for both offset directions. That condition
introduced no direction-selective \abbr{CoC} moment.

A separate Cartesian pose-hold experiment evaluated the null-space controller
under a repeatable internally commanded disturbance. Projected null-space
damping reduced the cumulative redundant motion. With singular-value
conditioning active before and during the disturbance, the measured net
redundant displacement remained close to zero along the evaluated reference
direction. The largest measured Cartesian position error was
\(1.304\,\mathrm{mm}\), below the specified \(2\,\mathrm{mm}\) limit.
