\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Collaborative robots operating in close proximity to humans require control strategies that can respond to unplanned contact in a safe, compliant, and predictable manner. Cartesian impedance control addresses this requirement by regulating the apparent mechanical impedance at the robot end-effector, allowing the robot to behave like a virtual spring-damper system that yields to external forces instead of resisting them rigidly. In this framework, deviations between the desired and actual end-effector pose, meaning its position and orientation, are converted into virtual Cartesian forces and moments, which are then mapped through the robot Jacobian into joint torques applied by the actuators. The resulting robot behaviour is determined by the chosen stiffness and damping matrices, whose formulation and implementation are central to achieving intuitive and reliable compliant motion.

This thesis investigates the design and implementation of a real-time Cartesian
impedance controller for the Franka Emika Panda, a redundant 7-\abbr{DOF}
collaborative robot. The controller is intended to produce compliant Cartesian
motion while keeping the relation between pose error, impedance forces and
moments, Jacobian mapping, null-space behavior, and commanded joint torques
traceable. The formulation uses stiffness and damping matrices defined in the
end-effector frame and transforms them into the robot base frame.

The experimental focus is narrowed to a contact-alignment task. A tool edge is
brought into contact with a table. The contact wrench contains both force and
moment components, and the Cartesian compliance allows a small translational and
rotational displacement. This displacement can be interpreted geometrically as a
screw motion; the corresponding instantaneous screw axis, or the closest point
of this axis to the tool edge, describes the passive alignment tendency of the
tool. The central question is how the Cartesian stiffness matrix should be
selected so that the contact wrench generates a useful passive self-aligning
motion around a virtual center of rotation while the robot remains stable, torque
limited, and safe for contact operation.
The first stiffness and damping candidates are computed from a quasi-static
compliance relation and a critical-damping approximation, and are then compared
experimentally. In addition, the thesis describes a least-squares
identification method for computing effective translation--rotation stiffness
and damping contributions from the logged contact motion and contact moment
model.
