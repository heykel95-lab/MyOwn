\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\ReviewMark{green}{The abstract states the problem, implemented mechanism,
principal measured ordering, null-space result, and scope limits without
overloading the reader with the full parameter matrix.}

Angular misalignment between a grinding tool and a surface can lead to
edge-first contact and increased contact loads. In applications where the
surface pose and tool mounting are subject to tolerances, the direction and
magnitude of this misalignment are generally unknown before contact. This
thesis investigates a Cartesian impedance control approach that uses the
contact interaction itself to support the rotational alignment of the tool
with a planar surface.

A real-time Cartesian impedance controller was implemented for a
seven-degree-of-freedom (\abbr{DOF}) Franka Emika Panda. Translational and
rotational compliance are defined relative to the surface geometry. The
controller also allows the virtual centre of compliance to be shifted away
from the tool centre point (TCP). This shift introduces
translation--rotation coupling into the Cartesian impedance. During the normal
press against the surface, the coupling generates an additional moment whose
direction depends on the position of the compliance centre. A suitable
displacement can therefore support the required alignment rotation without an
additional rotational trajectory.

The experimental investigation examined the influence of directional
Cartesian stiffness and of the position, direction, definition frame, and
magnitude of the virtual compliance-centre displacement. The measurements show
that the compliance-centre position produced the largest variation in the
alignment response over the tested parameter ranges. An assisting tangential
displacement lies perpendicular to the required rotation direction, and its
sign follows the sign of the initial angular misalignment. This relation was
also observed for intermediate directions in the surface plane. About the
weaker of the two principal alignment directions, a selected
\(40\,\mathrm{mm}\) displacement changed the measured set-up rotation from
\(-1.63^\circ\) to \(+4.43^\circ\).

The tested non-zero tangential displacements therefore provide
condition-dependent alignment authority. Among the tested positions, the
TCP-centred condition is the direction-independent fixed reference for the
investigated task because its selection requires no prior knowledge of the
misalignment direction or sign. A displaced centre can then be selected when
additional rotational alignment is required.

A complementary null-space study showed that projected damping reduced
redundant joint motion, while singular-value conditioning limited the net
disturbance-induced redundant displacement under the tested disturbance
direction. The experiments covered the contact-alignment phase up to the
pre-grinding gate; sustained grinding was outside the experimental evaluation.
