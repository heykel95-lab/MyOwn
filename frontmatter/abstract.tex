\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Collaborative robots that operate close to humans need control that responds to unplanned contact in a safe and compliant way. Cartesian impedance control meets this need by regulating the apparent mechanical impedance at the end-effector, so the robot behaves like a virtual spring-damper system that yields to external forces instead of resisting them rigidly. The resulting behaviour is set by the chosen stiffness and damping matrices, whose formulation and implementation are central to reliable compliant motion.

This thesis investigates the design and implementation of a real-time Cartesian
impedance controller for the Franka Emika Panda, a redundant 7-\abbr{DOF}
collaborative robot. The formulation keeps the relation between pose error,
impedance wrench, Jacobian mapping, null-space behavior, and joint torques
traceable, and defines the stiffness and damping matrices in a task frame
aligned with the contact surface before transforming them into the base frame.

The experimental focus is narrowed to a contact-alignment task. A tool edge is
brought into contact with a surface. The downward contact force acts at the
offset tool edge and produces a moment that, under a compliant rotational
stiffness, rotates the tool toward the surface, so the alignment is passive rather
than commanded. The resulting displacement combines translation and rotation as
a screw motion, whose instantaneous axis describes the passive alignment
tendency. The central question is how to select the Cartesian stiffness so that
this passive self-alignment is useful and pivots near the contact, while the
robot remains compliant and the Franka collision behavior is configured for the
experiment.
Initial candidates are computed from a quasi-static compliance relation and a
critical-damping approximation and compared experimentally; a least-squares
fit then identifies the effective translation--rotation stiffness and damping.
