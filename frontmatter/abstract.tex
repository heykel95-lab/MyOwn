\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Collaborative robots that operate close to humans need control that responds to unplanned contact in a safe and compliant way. Cartesian impedance control meets this need by regulating the apparent mechanical impedance at the end-effector, so the robot behaves like a virtual spring-damper system that yields to external forces instead of resisting them rigidly. The resulting behaviour is set by the chosen stiffness and damping matrices, whose formulation and implementation are central to reliable compliant motion.

This thesis investigates the design and implementation of a real-time Cartesian
impedance controller for the Franka Emika Panda, a redundant 7-\abbr{DOF}
collaborative robot. The formulation keeps the relation between pose error,
impedance wrench, Jacobian mapping, null-space behavior, and joint torques
traceable.  It distinguishes gains expressed in the base frame from gains in
the surface frame \([t_1,t_2,n_s]\), and computes phase-dependent damping from a
model-based Cartesian-inertia estimate.

The controller aligns a configured tool axis, descends to a geometric clearance,
selects the leading feature of a rectangular tool face, ramps that feature into
the surface during set-up, and subsequently executes a tangential grinding
motion.  During set-up, a selectable center of compliance is realized by
transforming a pole-frame \(6\times6\) stiffness and damping to the TCP with a
spatial adjoint.  The resulting off-diagonal blocks couple translation and
rotation while preserving symmetry and positive semidefiniteness.  The
alignment response during contact is evaluated through the physical tool-axis alignment
and the displacement of the selected tool feature and TCP.

Redundancy is handled with projected joint damping and an optional
smallest-singular-value bias.  A tolerance-truncated SVD inverts only retained
Jacobian singular values, while the seventh right-singular vector supplies the
structural null direction.  Buffered full-rate and compact diagnostic logs make
the phase, contact geometry, singular-value probes, and commanded torques
available for offline evaluation.

The controller is evaluated in a campaign of \(89\) provenance-controlled runs,
gated on a measured equilibrium of the set-up phase, a correctness check of the
coupled command path, and a repeatability floor from five repetitions. Sweeping
the centre of compliance across all three axes of the surface frame shows that
its two \emph{in-plane} components govern the contact alignment as a quadratic
surface with an interior optimum, while the offset along the surface normal has
no measurable effect over a range of \(277\,\mathrm{mm}\). Softening the tangential rotational stiffness triples the
alignment gained during contact at an unchanged contact force, and a
sufficiently mis-placed compliance centre rotates the tool away from the surface
rather than onto it.  The thesis thereby
links the sign and frame conventions of the spatial impedance law to its
real-time implementation and to the measured interpretation of pole-based
unscripted task-space alignment experiments.
