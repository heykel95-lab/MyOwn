\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Differences between the configured surface reference and the physical surface,
together with differences between the desired and achieved tool orientation,
can produce an angular mismatch at contact entry. This can lead to edge-first
contact and increased contact loads.

A real-time Cartesian impedance controller was implemented on a
seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Emika Panda to generate compliant
contact-induced rotation towards the configured surface. Directional
translational and rotational stiffness and damping were defined in the surface
frame. The virtual centre of compliance (\abbr{CoC}) could be displaced from
the tool centre point (TCP), introducing translation--rotation coupling into
the Cartesian impedance. The displaced \abbr{CoC} gives the commanded press a
virtual lever arm \(r_c\), which generates the additional moment
\(r_c\times f\) at the TCP.

The surface-contact experiments varied the \abbr{CoC} position, rotational
stiffness, and translational stiffness along \(t_2\). Positive and negative
pre-contact angular offsets were investigated about \(t_1\). The initial
angular condition at contact entry was calculated relative to the configured
surface reference, and the contact response \(\gamma_{t_1}\) was
calculated from the measured end-effector orientations at the beginning and end
of Contact Establishment.

With the \abbr{CoC} at the TCP, contact-induced rotation occurred towards the
configured surface for both initial rotational directions. Increasing the
rotational stiffness from \(5\) to \(50\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\) reduced the
response by \(63.9\,\%\), whereas the tested translational stiffness values
produced a response span equal to only \(0.4\,\%\) of the TCP-centred
reference. The tangential \abbr{CoC} displacement produced a strongly
direction-dependent response. Selecting the displacement direction according to
the initial angular-offset direction increased the final response by
approximately \(4\,\%\) and also produced a faster rotation towards alignment
in the measured time history. The opposite displacement direction reduced the
response by approximately \(98\,\%\) to \(99\,\%\). The displaced \abbr{CoC}
therefore acted mainly as a selector of the preferred rotational direction.

A separate Cartesian pose-hold experiment evaluated the null-space controller
under a repeatable virtual disturbance. Projected damping reduced the
cumulative null-space motion from \(7.596^\circ\) to \(5.687^\circ\).
Singular-value conditioning reduced the net displacement to nearly zero for
both tested torque magnitudes. The higher conditioning torque produced a
slightly smaller final displacement but considerably more cumulative
back-and-forth null-space motion. The Cartesian position remained within the
specified \(2\,\mathrm{mm}\) acceptance limit in all pose-hold trials.
